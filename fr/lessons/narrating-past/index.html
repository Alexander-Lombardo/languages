Dialogue

Léo

Tu sais, il faut que je te raconte ce qui m'est arrivé pendant mon voyage en Italie.

You know, I have to tell you what happened to me during my trip to Italy.

Nadia

Vas-y, raconte ! Qu'est-ce qui s'est passé ?

Go ahead, tell me! What happened?

Léo

Au début, tout allait bien. Il faisait beau et nous étions très contents.

At the beginning, everything was going well. The weather was nice and we were very happy.

Nadia

Et ensuite ?

And then?

Léo

D'abord, on a raté le train parce qu'on était arrivés en retard à la gare.

First, we missed the train because we had arrived late at the station.

Nadia

Oh non ! Et après, qu'est-ce que vous avez fait ?

Oh no! And after, what did you do?

Léo

On a loué une voiture, mais tout à coup elle est tombée en panne sur l'autoroute.

We rented a car, but all of a sudden it broke down on the highway.

Nadia

C'est incroyable ! Vous étiez perdus ?

That's unbelievable! Were you lost?

Léo

Complètement. Mais enfin, un fermier nous a aidés et on est arrivés le soir.

Completely. But at last, a farmer helped us and we arrived in the evening.

Nadia

Quelle aventure ! Au fond, c'est ça qui rend un voyage mémorable.

What an adventure! Deep down, that's what makes a trip memorable.

Grammar

Narrating in the past: the three tenses working together

A good story in French weaves three past tenses together. Each one does a different job. Knowing which to choose is the heart of B1 storytelling.

1. Passé composé — what happened

Use the passé composé for completed, sequential events that move the story forward. These are the actions on the timeline — first this happened, then that happened.

Nous avons raté le train, puis nous avons loué une voiture. (We missed the train, then we rented a car.)

2. Imparfait — the setting

Use the imparfait for the background: description, weather, time, feelings, and ongoing or habitual states. It answers "what was going on?" rather than "what happened next?"

Il faisait beau et nous étions contents. (The weather was nice and we were happy.)

3. Plus-que-parfait — the earlier action

Use the plus-que-parfait for an action that happened before another past action — the English "had done". Form it with the imparfait of avoir or être plus the past participle.

Quand nous sommes arrivés, l'avion avait déjà décollé. (When we arrived, the plane had already taken off.)

4. Sequencing connectors

These words organize the narrative and tell the listener where they are in the story:

  • au début (at the beginning), d'abord (first)
  • ensuite / puis (then, next)
  • soudain / tout à coup (suddenly) — great for a dramatic turn
  • enfin (finally), à la fin (at the end)

A typical pattern: set the scene in the imparfait, then list the events with the passé composé and connectors, and use the plus-que-parfait whenever you flash back to something that had already happened.

Pronunciation tip

The passé composé and the imparfait can sound close, so listen to the ending. The imparfait -ait / -aient ends in an open "eh" sound (faisait = "fuh-zeh"), while a passé composé participle like fait is a clipped "feh". The plus-que-parfait gives you two clues at once: an imparfait auxiliary (avait "a-veh", était "ay-teh") followed by a past participle.

The connectors soudain and tout à coup carry a small stress and a slight pause after them — they signal the dramatic moment, so let your voice rise a little to keep your listener hooked.

Reading: La nuit où nous avons raté l'avion

L'été dernier, ma sœur et moi avons décidé de partir en Espagne. Au début, nous étions très excités : il faisait chaud et nos valises étaient prêtes depuis la veille. Le matin du départ, tout allait bien. Mais d'abord, le taxi que nous avions réservé n'est jamais arrivé. Nous avons attendu vingt minutes, puis nous avons compris qu'il y avait un problème. Soudain, ma sœur s'est rendu compte qu'elle avait oublié son passeport à la maison. Nous sommes donc retournés le chercher en courant. Ensuite, dans le métro, il y avait une grève et tout était bloqué. Quand nous sommes enfin arrivés à l'aéroport, l'avion avait déjà décollé. Au début, nous étions furieux, mais à la fin, nous avons éclaté de rire. Nous avons pris un autre vol le lendemain, et ce voyage reste notre meilleur souvenir.

Translation

Last summer, my sister and I decided to go to Spain. At the beginning, we were very excited: it was hot and our suitcases had been ready since the day before. On the morning of departure, everything was going well. But first, the taxi we had reserved never came. We waited twenty minutes, then we understood there was a problem. Suddenly, my sister realized she had forgotten her passport at home. So we went back to get it, running. Then, in the subway, there was a strike and everything was blocked. When we finally arrived at the airport, the plane had already taken off. At first we were furious, but in the end, we burst out laughing. We took another flight the next day, and that trip remains our best memory.

Culture note

In everyday French conversation, the passé composé has almost entirely replaced the passé simple for telling stories — the passé simple now lives mostly in literature, fairy tales, and formal writing. So when a French friend launches into an anecdote at the café, you'll hear the same three tenses you're practicing here, not the bookish forms you may have seen in novels.

Storytelling is a real social skill in France. A well-told anecdote — with a good build-up, a soudain twist, and a punchline — is prized at dinner tables and cafés alike. Mastering the rhythm of imparfait for the setting and passé composé for the action lets you hold the floor and keep your audience leaning in.

Talk about it

  • Racontez un voyage où il y a eu un imprévu. Que s'est-il passé ? — Tell about a trip where something unexpected happened. What happened?
  • Vous est-il déjà arrivé de vous perdre quelque part ? Comment ? — Have you ever gotten lost somewhere? How?
  • Quelle est l'anecdote la plus drôle que vous aimez raconter ? — What is the funniest anecdote you like to tell?
  • Décrivez le décor : où étiez-vous et quel temps faisait-il ? — Describe the scene: where were you and what was the weather like?
  • Une fois, vous êtes arrivé(e) quelque part et quelque chose avait déjà changé. Racontez. — Once, you arrived somewhere and something had already changed. Tell about it.
