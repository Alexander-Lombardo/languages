Dialogue

Léa

Salut ! T'as passé un bon week-end ou bien ?

Hi! Did you have a good weekend, or what?

Yanis

Ouais, grave. On est allés bouffer chez Karim, c'était trop bien.

Yeah, totally. We went to eat at Karim's, it was really great.

Léa

Ah, j'kiffe ce mec, il est trop drôle. Y avait du monde ?

Ah, I love that guy, he's so funny. Were there a lot of people?

Yanis

Plein de potes, ouais. Du coup on s'est tapé un délire jusqu'à pas d'heure.

Loads of mates, yeah. So we had a wild time until all hours.

Léa

La chance ! Moi, j'ai bossé tout le week-end, c'était relou de ouf.

Lucky you! Me, I worked all weekend, it was insanely annoying.

Yanis

Sérieux ? Le boulot le dimanche, genre, c'est carrément abusé.

Seriously? Working on Sunday, like, that's totally out of order.

Léa

J'te raconte pas. Bon, faut que j'y aille, ma bagnole est mal garée.

You have no idea. Anyway, I gotta go, my car's badly parked.

Yanis

Ok, à plus ! On se cale un truc cette semaine, quoi ?

OK, see you! We'll sort something out this week, yeah?

Grammar

Le français familier — understanding everyday spoken French

This lesson is about comprehension, not imitation. Native speakers constantly bend the rules you have learned. Your job at C1 is to decode casual speech effortlessly and to deploy a few familiar markers naturally — while knowing that most of this is très informel and out of place in an exam, a cover letter, or a meeting with a stranger.

1. La grammaire de l'oral — spoken grammar

  • Dropping ne: in real speech the ne of negation almost always vanishes. Je (ne) sais pas → j'sais pas. C'est pas grave. J'ai rien vu. You must still write the ne.
  • on = nous: spoken French uses on for "we" almost everywhere. On y va ? On s'est bien marrés. The verb stays in the third-person singular.
  • Questions by intonation: no inversion, no est-ce que — just a rising tone. Tu viens ? T'as faim ? Il est où ?
  • Dislocation: a noun or pronoun is set off and echoed by a pronoun, for emphasis. Moi, je préfère le thé. Il est sympa, ce type. Ce film, je l'ai déjà vu.
  • c'est… qui / que for emphasis: C'est moi qui ai fait ça. C'est ça que je voulais.
  • ça for cela: spoken French overwhelmingly says ça. Ça m'énerve. Ça te dit ?

2. Le vocabulaire familier & le verlan

Familiar words replace neutral ones, and verlan (from l'envers, "backwards") reverses syllables: fou → ouf, lourd → relou, femme → meuf, mec → keum. Treat these as everyday street vocabulary, not as code.

3. Intensificateurs & mots de remplissage

Casual speech is held together by intensifiers and fillers. They carry little literal meaning but huge social signal.

  • trop / grave / carrément = "so / totally / completely": C'est trop bien. J'suis grave fatigué. C'est carrément génial.
  • du coup = "so / as a result" — the ubiquitous connector: Du coup, on fait quoi ?
  • genre = "like / sort of", flagging an example or approximation: Il a, genre, mille amis.
  • quoi = a sentence-final "y'know": C'est nul, quoi.

Register warning: bouffer, meuf, ouf, vénère and the like are fine among friends but can sound rude or unprofessional elsewhere. Understand them everywhere; produce them only when the company is genuinely casual.

Pronunciation tip

Casual French is fast and compressed. Pronouns collapse onto the next word: je → j' (j'sais pas ≈ "shay pa"), tu → t' before a vowel (t'as, t'es), il → y (y a, y fait). Train your ear to expect these réductions rather than the full textbook forms — the meaning is identical, only the surface is squeezed.

Listen too for the dropped ne: the negation now lives entirely in the pas / rien / jamais at the end, so don't wait for a ne that will never come. And notice the flat, rising intonation that turns any statement into a question (Tu viens ↗) — pitch, not grammar, is doing the work.

Reading: Mon coloc est relou (post de forum)

Bon les gens, j'ai besoin de vider mon sac. Mon coloc, c'est un bon pote à la base, mais alors qu'est-ce qu'il est devenu relou. Le mec, il bosse jamais, il passe ses journées à bouffer mes trucs dans le frigo et à regarder des séries genre toute la nuit. Du coup, le matin, impossible de le réveiller, quoi. La semaine dernière, j'lui ai demandé gentiment de faire la vaisselle. Sa réponse ? « Ouais ouais, t'inquiète. » Spoiler : il l'a jamais faite. Carrément. Et le pire, c'est qu'il prend ma bagnole sans demander. L'autre jour, je la retrouve garée n'importe comment, avec une rayure de ouf sur la portière. Là, j'étais grave vénère. Bref, je sais plus quoi faire. Je l'aime bien, hein, c'est mon pote depuis le lycée, mais vivre avec lui c'est devenu un truc de fou. Vous feriez quoi à ma place ? Je lui dis franchement que c'est mort, ou je laisse couler encore un peu ? Aidez-moi, sérieux.

Translation

OK folks, I need to get something off my chest. My flatmate is basically a good buddy, but wow has he become a pain. The guy never works, he spends his days scarfing down my stuff from the fridge and watching shows like all night long. So in the morning, there's no way to wake him up. Last week I nicely asked him to do the dishes. His answer? "Yeah yeah, don't worry." Spoiler: he never did them. Honestly. And the worst part is he takes my car without asking. The other day, I find it parked any old way, with an insane scratch on the door. At that point, I was really pissed off. Anyway, I don't know what to do anymore. I do like him, you know, he's been my mate since high school, but living with him has become totally crazy. What would you do in my place? Do I tell him straight that it's over, or do I let it slide a bit longer? Help me out, seriously.

Culture note

Casual French is a moving target. Verlan — born in working-class suburbs and the streets of the banlieues — has fed mainstream slang for decades: meuf, keum, ouf, relou are now understood by everyone, while newer coinages keep bubbling up through rap, social media, and schoolyards. Some verlan even gets re-verlanised: arabe → beur → rebeu. The further a word travels from its origin, the more neutral it feels, which is why kiffer now sounds almost tame.

A word of caution for the learner: register is everything in France, and using slang in the wrong setting reads as either clumsy or disrespectful. The safe strategy at C1 is asymmetric — aim to understand the whole spectrum, from argot to soutenu, but produce familiar language only when your interlocutors clearly have, and default to neutral, correct French with anyone you don't know well.

Talk about it

  • Y a-t-il un équivalent de l'argot ou du verlan dans votre langue ? Donnez des exemples. — Is there an equivalent of slang or verlan in your language? Give examples.
  • Pensez-vous qu'il soit important d'apprendre le français familier, ou faut-il s'en tenir au standard ? — Do you think it's important to learn casual French, or should one stick to the standard?
  • Dans quelles situations le langage familier serait-il déplacé, voire offensant ? — In which situations would casual language be inappropriate, even offensive?
  • Racontez une anecdote où vous avez mal compris une expression argotique. — Tell a story about a time you misunderstood a slang expression.
  • À votre avis, pourquoi les mots de remplissage comme « du coup » ou « genre » se répandent-ils autant ? — In your view, why do fillers like "du coup" or "genre" spread so widely?
