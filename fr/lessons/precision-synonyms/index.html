Precision: Synonyms & Word Choice

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Replace vague all-purpose verbs (faire, mettre, dire, il y a) with precise, register-appropriate alternatives.
  • Master core French collocations (cooccurrences) so that nouns and verbs pair naturally.
  • Distinguish near-synonyms precisely, choosing the right word among problème/souci/enjeu/question and changer/modifier/transformer/bouleverser.
  • Avoid the most damaging faux-amis (actuellement, éventuellement, sensible, assister à) that betray an anglophone speaker.
  • Elevate written and spoken register by selecting the most exact term rather than the most familiar one.

Dialogue

Nadia

On a fait pas mal d'erreurs sur ce dossier, non ?

We made quite a few mistakes on this file, didn't we?

Théo

Disons plutôt qu'on a commis quelques erreurs de méthode. Mais l'enjeu reste maîtrisable.

Let's rather say we committed a few methodological errors. But what's at stake remains manageable.

Nadia

Tu crois ? Moi, je trouve qu'on court un vrai risque si on ne change pas d'approche.

You think? I feel we're running a real risk if we don't change our approach.

Théo

Changer, non : il suffit de modifier deux ou trois paramètres, pas de tout bouleverser.

Change, no: we just need to modify two or three parameters, not upend everything.

Nadia

D'accord. Instaurons une revue hebdomadaire, et on tirera des conclusions plus fiables.

All right. Let's establish a weekly review, and we'll draw more reliable conclusions.

Théo

Bonne idée. J'assisterai à la première séance ; éventuellement, j'inviterai la direction.

Good idea. I'll attend the first session; possibly, I'll invite management.

Grammar

1. Replacing vague verbs with precise ones

The four verbs faire, mettre, dire and the existential il y a are indispensable, but overusing them flattens your style. At C1, train yourself to reach for the exact verb. The choice depends on the noun that follows.

Vague (avoid overusing)Precise alternativeExample

2. Collocations (les cooccurrences)

A cooccurrence is a fixed pairing: a particular noun calls for a particular verb. The wrong verb sounds foreign even when each word is correct. These pairs must be memorised as blocks.

3. Distinguishing near-synonyms

Several French words translate a single English one but carry distinct nuances. Pick by precise meaning, not by habit.

  • un problème (a difficulty to solve) / un souci (a personal worry, often minor) / un enjeu (what is at stake) / une question (an issue to debate). L'enjeu climatique ≠ un petit souci de voiture.
  • comprendre (to understand, general) / saisir (to grasp intuitively, quickly) / concevoir (to fully conceive, often a negation: je ne conçois pas qu'on…).
  • important (significant) / considérable (large in scale) / majeur (of the first rank) / crucial (decisive, turning-point).
  • changer (to change) / modifier (to alter partly) / transformer (to reshape thoroughly) / bouleverser (to upend, with emotional or radical force).

Pronunciation tip

The two long adverbs are mouthfuls worth drilling. Actuellement runs ak-tü-el-MAN and éventuellement runs ay-van-tü-el-MAN: both glide through a -tuelle- cluster before the nasal -ment (MAN, not -ment with a hard t). Keep the u in -tuel- rounded and tight; an English speaker tends to flatten it to "oo".

Mind commettre (ko-MET-r) with its doubled, crisp t, distinct from commencer. And let bouleverser roll fully — boul-ver-SAY — the word's heft mirrors its meaning of upheaval, so don't clip it.

Reading: L'art de choisir le mot juste

Écrire avec précision, ce n'est pas employer des mots rares ; c'est refuser la facilité. Trop de rédacteurs s'appuient sur quelques verbes passe-partout — faire, mettre, dire, avoir — qui finissent par tout aplatir. Or, on ne fait pas un projet : on le mène, on l'élabore, on le conduit. On ne fait pas une erreur de jugement : on la commet. On ne met pas une règle : on l'instaure. À chaque idée correspond un verbe plus exact, plus dense, qui en dit davantage en moins de mots. La langue française regorge aussi de cooccurrences : on pousse un cri, on prend une décision, on court un risque, on nourrit un espoir, on livre un combat. Ces alliances ne s'inventent pas ; elles s'apprennent. Enfin, méfions-nous des faux-amis qui trahissent l'anglophone : actuellement ne veut pas dire « actually », mais « en ce moment » ; éventuellement signifie « peut-être », non « finalement » ; sensible décrit la sensibilité, non le bon sens. Choisir le mot juste, c'est donc penser plus clairement : la précision du vocabulaire reflète, et nourrit, la précision de la pensée.

Translation

Writing with precision is not about using rare words; it is about refusing the easy way. Too many writers lean on a few all-purpose verbs — faire, mettre, dire, avoir — which end up flattening everything. Yet you do not 'do' a project: you lead it, draft it, conduct it. You do not 'do' an error of judgement: you commit it. You do not 'put' a rule: you establish it. To every idea there corresponds a more exact, denser verb that says more in fewer words. French also abounds in collocations: you let out a cry, you make a decision, you run a risk, you nurse a hope, you wage a fight. These pairings cannot be invented; they must be learned. Finally, one must beware of the false friends that betray the anglophone: actuellement does not mean 'actually' but 'right now'; éventuellement means 'possibly', not 'eventually'; sensible describes sensitivity, not good sense. Choosing the right word, then, means thinking more clearly: precision of vocabulary reflects, and feeds, precision of thought.

Culture note

French education prizes le mot juste with almost moral fervour. From the dictée of primary school to the dreaded dissertation, students are trained to distinguish synonyms and to scorn the vague catch-all verb. Gustave Flaubert famously agonised over each word, searching for the single exact term; his ideal of le mot juste still shapes how the French judge good writing.

This explains why faux-amis are so revealing: an anglophone who says « actuellement » for "actually" or « j'ai assisté mon collègue » for "I assisted my colleague" is instantly placed. Mastering precise word choice is not pedantry but a passport — it signals that you think in French, not merely that you translate into it.

Talk about it

  • Selon toi, quel est le principal enjeu de ton métier aujourd'hui ? — In your view, what is the main thing at stake in your profession today?
  • Raconte une décision difficile que tu as prise : as-tu couru un risque ? — Describe a hard decision you made: did you run a risk?
  • Y a-t-il un faux-ami français qui t'a déjà piégé(e) ? Lequel ? — Is there a French false friend that has already tricked you? Which one?
  • Quelle différence fais-tu entre simplement « changer » et « bouleverser » sa vie ? — What difference do you draw between simply 'changing' your life and 'upending' it?
  • Préfères-tu un style sobre et précis ou un style riche en images ? Pourquoi ? — Do you prefer a sober, precise style or one rich in imagery? Why?
