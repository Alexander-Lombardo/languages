Greetings & Introductions

What you'll learn

Grammar

Formal vs. informal “you”

French has two words for “you”:

  • vous — formal, polite, or more than one person. Use with strangers, older people, shopkeepers, in business.
  • tu — informal. Use with friends, family, children, peers you know.

So the same question changes shape: formal Comment allez-vous ? / casual Ça va ?. As a learner, default to vous with strangers — it's never rude.

Enchanté / Enchantée

“Nice to meet you.” A man says enchanté, a woman enchantée (they sound identical — the extra -e is silent). This is your first taste of agreement (full story in Lesson 13).

Pronunciation tip

Liaison: when vous / allez meet a vowel, the silent final consonant links over: Comment allez-vous → koh-mahn-ta-lay-VOO; vous appelez → voo-za-play. Just recognise why it sounds connected.

Culture note

In France you say “Bonjour” first — when entering a shop, approaching a desk, or starting almost any interaction. After about 6 p.m. it switches to “Bonsoir.” “Salut” is friendly but too casual for strangers or formal settings.

Talk about it

  • Comment tu t'appelles ? — What's your name?
  • Ça va ? — How are you?
  • Comment allez-vous aujourd'hui ? — How are you today?
