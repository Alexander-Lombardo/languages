What you'll learn

Dialogue

Camille

Tu as vu les informations ? Le maire a annoncé qu'il allait démissionner.

Did you see the news? The mayor announced he was going to resign.

Bruno

Sérieusement ? Il a dit qu'il partait ?

Seriously? He said he was leaving?

Camille

Oui, il a affirmé qu'il avait pris sa décision la veille.

Yes, he stated that he had made his decision the day before.

Bruno

Et un journaliste lui a demandé pourquoi il s'en allait maintenant ?

And did a journalist ask him why he was leaving now?

Camille

Il a juste répondu qu'il expliquerait tout le lendemain.

He just answered that he would explain everything the next day.

Bruno

On m'a raconté qu'il prétendait être innocent. C'est vrai ?

I was told he was claiming to be innocent. Is that true?

Camille

Selon un témoin, il aurait avoué quelque chose, mais ce ne sont que des potins.

According to a witness, he supposedly confessed something, but those are just gossip.

Bruno

My colleague actually asked me not to repeat anything before tomorrow.

Camille

Wise advice. She told you to be wary of rumours, I imagine.

Grammar

Le discours indirect — reporting what people say

Reported (indirect) speech relays someone's words without quoting them. We drop the quotation marks, link the clause with a connector, and adjust the pronouns, the tenses, and the time/place words.

1. Reporting statements with que

A simple statement is introduced by que (qu' before a vowel) after a reporting verb like dire, affirmer, expliquer, ajouter, prétendre.

Direct: « Je suis fatigué. » → Indirect: Il dit qu'il est fatigué.

2. Reporting questions

There is no inversion and no est-ce que in an indirect question. The link word depends on the original question:

  • Yes/no questions (est-ce que / inversion) → si (s' before il/ils): « Est-ce que tu viens ? » → Il demande si je viens.
  • qu'est-ce que (what, object) → ce que: « Qu'est-ce que tu veux ? » → Il demande ce que je veux.
  • qu'est-ce qui (what, subject) → ce qui: « Qu'est-ce qui se passe ? » → Il demande ce qui se passe.
  • Other question words stay (où, quand, pourquoi, comment, qui): « Pourquoi pars-tu ? » → Il demande pourquoi je pars.

3. Reporting commands with de + infinitif

An order or request becomes de + infinitif after verbs like dire, demander, conseiller, ordonner.

Direct: « Pars ! » → Indirect: Il me dit de partir.   Negative: « Ne dis rien. » → Il me demande de ne rien dire.

4. Sequence of tenses (concordance des temps)

If the reporting verb is in the present (il dit que…), the tense of the quoted clause does not change. If the reporting verb is in the past (il a dit que…, il disait que…), the tense shifts back:

5. Time and place adjustments

When you report a past conversation, the time markers shift relative to the new moment of speaking:

  • aujourd'hui → ce jour-là
  • hier → la veille
  • demain → le lendemain
  • maintenant → à ce moment-là
  • ici → là

Example: « Je pars demain. » → Il a dit qu'il partait le lendemain.

Pronunciation tip

The two link words si and s'il are easy to slur. Keep si ("see") fully open in Il demande si tu viens, but elide it before il/ils: s'il vient = "seel vyahn". Don't confuse this si with the conditional "if" — they sound identical, so context carries the meaning.

Listen for the conditional ending -rait in reported futures: viendrait and viendra differ only at the tail. The conditional -ait is the same open "eh" as the imparfait, while the future -a closes to a clean "ah". That tiny vowel tells you whether the sentence is reported or direct.

Reading: Une rumeur au bureau

Hier, à la pause-café, Sophie m'a annoncé une grande nouvelle. Elle m'a dit que notre directeur allait quitter l'entreprise. Je lui ai demandé si elle en était sûre, et elle a répondu qu'un collègue le lui avait confié la veille. Elle a ajouté qu'il avait déjà signé son contrat ailleurs et qu'il partirait le mois suivant. Curieux, j'ai voulu savoir ce qui s'était vraiment passé. Sophie a précisé qu'elle ignorait les détails, mais que, selon un témoin, le directeur aurait avoué qu'il n'était plus heureux ici. Elle m'a demandé pourquoi je posais autant de questions. Je lui ai expliqué que je détestais les rumeurs et que je préférais attendre une déclaration officielle. Enfin, elle m'a conseillé de ne rien répéter à personne avant le lendemain. J'ai promis de me taire ; après tout, un potin reste un potin tant que personne ne l'a confirmé.

Translation

Yesterday, at the coffee break, Sophie announced big news to me. She told me that our director was going to leave the company. I asked her if she was sure, and she answered that a colleague had confided it to her the day before. She added that he had already signed a contract elsewhere and that he would leave the following month. Curious, I wanted to know what had really happened. Sophie clarified that she didn't know the details, but that, according to a witness, the director had supposedly confessed that he was no longer happy here. She asked me why I was asking so many questions. I explained to her that I hated rumours and that I preferred to wait for an official statement. Finally, she advised me not to repeat anything to anyone before the next day. I promised to keep quiet; after all, gossip stays gossip as long as no one has confirmed it.

Culture note

French has a polished way of distancing the speaker from unverified claims: the conditionnel of rumour. When a journalist writes « Le ministre aurait menti » ("The minister allegedly lied"), the conditional signals that the information is reported, not confirmed. You'll hear it constantly on the radio and read it in headlines — it is the grammatical equivalent of English "allegedly" or "reportedly".

Everyday speech leans on the same idea with little tags like paraît-il ("apparently"), selon ("according to"), and il paraît que…. Sprinkling these into your reported speech makes you sound both natural and appropriately cautious — a very French way of sharing a potin without fully vouching for it.

Talk about it

  • Racontez une rumeur que vous avez entendue récemment, au discours indirect. — Tell about a rumour you heard recently, using reported speech.
  • Que vous a dit votre dernier interlocuteur important ? Rapportez ses paroles. — What did your last important conversation partner tell you? Report their words.
  • Pensez-vous qu'il faille toujours vérifier les informations avant de les répéter ? Pourquoi ? — Do you think one should always verify news before repeating it? Why?
  • Quelqu'un vous a-t-il déjà demandé de garder un secret ? Que vous a-t-il demandé de faire ? — Has someone ever asked you to keep a secret? What did they ask you to do?
  • Selon vous, pourquoi les potins circulent-ils si vite au travail ? — In your opinion, why does gossip spread so fast at work?
