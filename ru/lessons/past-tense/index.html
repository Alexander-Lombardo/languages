The Past Tense

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Form the past tense by dropping -ть and adding -л / -ла / -ло / -ли.
  • Agree the past-tense verb with the subject's gender and number, not person.
  • Use был / была / было / были as the past of «to be».
  • Handle key irregular pasts: мочь → мог/могла and идти → шёл/шла.
  • Tell a short story about yesterday using past-tense verbs.

Dialogue

Лена

Антон, где ты был вчера вечером?

Anton, where were you last night?

Антон

Я был дома. А ты?

I was at home. And you?

Лена

Я была в театре с сестрой.

I was at the theatre with my sister.

Антон

Как было? Понравилось?

How was it? Did you like it?

Лена

Очень! Мы видели прекрасный спектакль.

Very much! We saw a wonderful play.

Антон

А я не мог пойти. Была плохая погода.

And I couldn't go. The weather was bad.

Лена

Да, шёл дождь весь день.

Yes, it rained all day.

Антон

Поэтому я остался дома и читал книгу.

So I stayed home and read a book.

Лена

Какую книгу ты читал?

Which book were you reading?

Антон

Роман Толстого. Я уже почти закончил.

A novel by Tolstoy. I've almost finished it already.

Лена

Молодец! А раньше ты любил читать?

Well done! And did you like reading before?

Антон

Нет, в детстве я не любил книги.

No, as a child I didn't like books.

Лена

Интересно. Мы жили в одном городе тогда?

Interesting. Did we live in the same city then?

Антон

Да! Мы оба жили в Казани.

Yes! We both lived in Kazan.

Лена

Точно. А вчера я купила тебе подарок.

Right. And yesterday I bought you a gift.

Антон

Правда? Что это?

Really? What is it?

Лена

Билет в театр! Но я забыла его дома.

A theatre ticket! But I forgot it at home.

Антон

Ха-ха, ничего. Спасибо большое!

Ha-ha, no worries. Thank you so much!

Лена

Завтра мы пойдём вместе.

Tomorrow we'll go together.

Grammar

How the past tense is built

The Russian past is wonderfully simple. Take the infinitive, drop -ть, and add an ending that matches the subject's gender and number — not its person. So «I read», «you read» and «he read» all use the same masculine form if the speaker is male.

Subject is…Endingчитать → работать →
masculine (он, я-male, ты-male)-лчиталработал
feminine (она, я-female, ты-female)-лачиталаработала
neuter (оно)-лочиталоработало
plural (мы, вы, они)-личиталиработали

Agreement is by gender, not person

This is the key mental shift. Я читал (a man speaking) vs Я читала (a woman speaking) — same «I», different ending. Вы always takes -ли even for one polite person. Look at the subject, ask «masculine, feminine, neuter or plural?», then pick the ending.

«To be» in the past: был / была / было / были

Present-tense Russian has no «am/is/are», but the past does. Use был and friends — and they too agree with the subject.

SubjectFormExample
masculineбылОн был дома.
feminineбылаОна была в театре.
neuter / impersonalбылоБыло холодно.
pluralбылиМы были дома.

Negation: не был (wasn't). With «у меня нет» the past is не было + genitive: У меня не было времени (I had no time).

Irregular pasts

A few common verbs don't simply drop -ть. Learn these whole:

InfinitiveMasc.Fem.Neut.Plural
мочь (can)могмогламогломогли
идти (go on foot)шёлшлашлошли
есть (eat)елелаелоели

Note the dropped -л in мог (masculine) — many verbs whose stem ends in a consonant lose the -л in the masculine only; the other forms keep it.

Pronunciation tip

шёл: the ё is always stressed and sounds [o] — say «shol», not «shel». Its feminine partner шла drops the vowel entirely: «shla».

-л at the end: the final л in был, читал, мог is a hard «dark» L, made with the back of the tongue raised — closer to English «full» than «leaf».

было vs была: stress separates them. БЫ-ла shifts the stress onto the ending (feminine), while БЫ-ло keeps it on the first syllable (neuter). Mixing these up changes the meaning.

Culture note

Because the past tense agrees with gender, a single sentence quietly reveals who is speaking: Я был рад (a man, glad) vs Я была рада (a woman). There's no neutral option — Russian makes you commit, which is something to keep in mind when you write or role-play.

Russians love trading stories about вчера and the ever-present погода. A complaint about the weather — Шёл дождь весь день — is a reliable, friendly icebreaker, and it gives you instant past-tense practice in real conversation.
