Verb Aspect: Imperfective vs Perfective

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Explain the core difference: imperfective = process/repetition, perfective = completed result.
  • Recognise aspect pairs (делать/сделать, читать/прочитать, писать/написать).
  • Choose the right aspect in the past: «was doing» vs «did and finished».
  • Use говорить/сказать and покупать/купить correctly in context.
  • Tell whether a sentence stresses the action itself or its result.

Dialogue

Игорь

Вера, что ты делала вчера вечером?

Vera, what were you doing last night?

Вера

Я читала книгу. Долго, часа два.

I was reading a book. For a long time, about two hours.

Игорь

И ты прочитала её?

And did you finish it?

Вера

Нет, не прочитала. Только половину.

No, I didn't finish. Only half.

Игорь

А я писал статью весь вечер.

And I was writing an article all evening.

Вера

И написал?

And did you finish it?

Игорь

Да, наконец написал! Очень рад.

Yes, finally I finished it! Very glad.

Вера

Молодец. Ты обычно пишешь по вечерам?

Well done. Do you usually write in the evenings?

Игорь

Да, часто. Но вчера было трудно.

Yes, often. But yesterday it was hard.

Вера

Понимаю. Кстати, ты сделал домашнее задание?

I understand. By the way, did you do the homework?

Игорь

Ой, нет. Я делал его, но не закончил.

Oh, no. I was doing it, but didn't finish.

Вера

Что сказал учитель?

What did the teacher say?

Игорь

Он сказал: «Решите эту задачу к пятнице».

He said: «Solve this problem by Friday».

Вера

Ты уже решил её?

Have you solved it yet?

Игорь

Я решал долго, и вдруг понял ответ!

I was solving it for a long time, and suddenly understood the answer!

Вера

Отлично! А я ещё покупала продукты сегодня.

Great! And I was also buying groceries today.

Игорь

Ты купила хлеб? У нас нет хлеба.

Did you buy bread? We have no bread.

Вера

Да, купила. И молоко тоже.

Yes, I bought it. And milk too.

Игорь

Спасибо! Ты всё сделала.

Thanks! You got everything done.

Grammar

The big idea: two views of one action

Most Russian verbs come in pairs. The two members describe the same activity but from different angles:

  • Imperfective = the action as a process, habit or repetition. Think «was doing», «used to do», «does (regularly)». It cares about the activity, not its end.
  • Perfective = the action as a single completed result. Think «did and finished», «got it done». It cares about the outcome.

English uses extra words («was reading» vs «read through») or context. Russian builds the distinction right into the verb.

How pairs are formed

Often the perfective is the imperfective plus a prefix (чита́ть → прочита́ть). Sometimes the stem changes (покупа́ть → купи́ть), and a few pairs use entirely different roots (говори́ть → сказа́ть).

Aspect in the past tense

This is where aspect bites first. Both members form the past the same way (-л/-ла/-ло/-ли), but they mean different things:

Imperfective pastPerfective past
Я читал книгу.
I was reading a book. (process — maybe unfinished)		Я прочитал книгу.
I read the book (all of it). (result — done)
Она писала письмо.
She was writing a letter.		Она написала письмо.
She wrote (finished) the letter.

So «Я делал домашнее задание» says you spent time on it; «Я сделал домашнее задание» says it's done. The dialogue's «читала… но не прочитала» (was reading… but didn't finish) shows the contrast in one breath.

Signal words

Certain adverbs lean toward one aspect. Often / usually / for a long time (часто, обычно, долго) suit the imperfective (repeated/ongoing). Already / finally / suddenly (уже, наконец, вдруг) suit the perfective (a finished event). Let them guide your choice.

Pronunciation tip

Prefix stress can move: imperfective чита́ть stresses the ending, but perfective прочита́ть keeps it there too — yet сде́лать pulls stress onto the stem. There's no single rule, so memorise each pair as a sound, not a spelling.

сказать has no [g]: say «ska-ZAT». Compare with its imperfective partner говори́ть = «ga-va-REET» — two totally different roots that mean «say/speak».

купи́ть vs покупа́ть: the perfective is short and punchy («koo-PEET»), the imperfective longer («pa-koo-PAT»). The extra syllable mirrors the «drawn-out process» meaning — a handy memory hook.

Culture note

Aspect is often called the steepest hill in Russian grammar, and even advanced learners second-guess it. Don't aim for perfection now — aim for the instinct: «Am I describing the activity or its finished result?» That one question solves most cases.

Native speakers exploit aspect for nuance and even politeness. A perfective request can sound crisp and final, while an imperfective one feels softer or more open-ended. You'll meet these subtleties again at B2 — for now, listen for how Russians pair «делал… но не сделал» to mean «I worked on it but didn't finish», a pattern you'll use constantly.
