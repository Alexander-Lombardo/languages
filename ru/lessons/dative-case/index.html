The Dative Case: To/For & Liking

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Form dative endings for masculine, neuter and feminine nouns and pronouns.
  • Use the dative for the indirect object «to/for» (я даю другу книгу).
  • Express liking with нравиться (Мне нравится музыка).
  • State age with the dative (Мне двадцать лет).
  • Use к (toward) and по (along/around) plus the verbs помогать and звонить with the dative.

Grammar

What the dative does

The dative answers «to whom?» or «for whom?». It marks the receiver — the person you give to, write to, call, or help. Я пишу письмо маме = «I write a letter to mom».

Liking: нравиться turns the sentence around

Russian doesn't «like» things directly. It says they are «pleasing to someone». The liker goes in the dative, and the thing liked is the grammatical subject — so the verb agrees with the thing, not the person.

RussianLiterallyMeaning
Мне нравится музыка.to-me is-pleasing musicI like music.
Мне нравятся книги.to-me are-pleasing booksI like books.
Ему нравишься ты.to-him are-pleasing youHe likes you.

Age, weather and feelings

Age uses the dative: Мне двадцать лет = literally «to me twenty years». After 1 say год, after 2-4 года, after 5+ лет. The same impersonal dative carries feelings and weather: Мне холодно (I'm cold), Ему интересно (he finds it interesting).

Prepositions and verbs with the dative

к = motion toward a person/place (иду к другу); по = along/around/by (гулять по городу, по телефону). The verbs помогать (help), звонить (call) and дарить (give a gift) all take a dative object: помогаю другу, звоню маме.

Pronunciation tip

нравится vs нравятся: singular NRA-vee-tsa, plural NRA-vya-tsa — the difference is tiny but it tells the listener whether one thing or many please you. The -ться/-тся ending is always pronounced [tsa].

ему / ей: «to him» = ye-MOO, «to her» = yey. Don't confuse dative ей (to her) with genitive её (her / of her).

к + к-word: before a consonant cluster к can pick up a vowel — ко мне (toward me) is said «ka-MNYE». A handful of fixed forms like this are worth memorising whole.

Culture note

Asking Сколько тебе лет? is normal small talk in Russia and rarely considered rude, even among new acquaintances. The dative construction («to-you how-many years») keeps age slightly impersonal, almost like a fact about you rather than a label.

The нравиться pattern reveals a Russian habit of mind: things happen to you. You're cold, things please you, things are interesting to you. Leaning into these dative «to-me» constructions instantly makes your Russian sound more native than translating English «I like / I feel» word for word.
