Noun Gender & Personal Pronouns

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Identify a noun's gender from its ending: masculine, feminine, or neuter.
  • Apply the gender rules to real words like стол, книга, and окно.
  • Replace a noun with the right он/она/оно based on its gender.
  • Use all the subject pronouns: я, ты, он, она, оно, мы, вы, они.
  • Recognise that он and она can mean «it» for objects, not just «he/she».

Dialogue

Ольга

Дмитрий, что это? Это стол?

Dmitry, what is this? Is it a table?

Дмитрий

Да, это стол. Он большой.

Yes, it's a table. It is big.

Ольга

А это что? Это книга?

And what is this? Is it a book?

Дмитрий

Да, это книга. Она новая.

Yes, it's a book. It is new.

Ольга

А там окно?

And is that a window over there?

Дмитрий

Да, это окно. Оно большое.

Yes, it's a window. It is big.

Ольга

Хорошо. Стол — он, книга — она, окно — оно.

Good. A table is «он», a book is «она», a window is «оно».

Дмитрий

Правильно! Это легко.

Correct! It's easy.

Ольга

А кто это? Это студент?

And who is this? Is it a student?

Дмитрий

Это Сергей. Он студент.

This is Sergei. He is a student.

Ольга

А Мария? Она тоже студентка?

And Maria? Is she also a student?

Дмитрий

Да, она студентка. Они студенты.

Yes, she is a student. They are students.

Ольга

А мы? Кто мы?

And us? Who are we?

Дмитрий

Мы тоже студенты!

We are students too!

Ольга

А музей? Это он или она?

And «museum»? Is it «он» or «она»?

Дмитрий

Музей — он. Слово на «-й».

«Museum» is «он». The word ends in «-й».

Ольга

Понятно. Спасибо, Дмитрий!

I see. Thank you, Dmitry!

Дмитрий

Пожалуйста, Ольга. Ты молодец!

You're welcome, Olga. Well done!

Grammar

Every Russian noun has a gender

Russian nouns are masculine, feminine, or neuter. There is no «the» or «a», so the gender is hidden in the ending of the word. For most nouns you can simply look and tell.

GenderTypical endingExamples
masculineconsonant, or -йстол, дом, студент, музей
feminine-а / -якнига, вода, семья, студентка
neuter-о / -еокно, слово, море

So стол ends in a consonant → masculine. Книга ends in -а → feminine. Окно ends in -о → neuter. A small group like имя (name) is an irregular neuter — just memorise those few.

«It» follows the gender: он, она, оно

English uses it for every object. Russian instead uses the pronoun that matches the noun's gender — even for lifeless things like a table or a book.

NounGenderPronounMeaning
столmasc.онhe / it
книгаfem.онаshe / it
окноneut.оноit

That is why Это стол. Он большой. means «It's a table. It is big» — not «He is big».

The full set of subject pronouns

These are the «doer» pronouns you put at the front of a sentence.

SingularPlural
яIмыwe
тыyou (informal)выyou (formal/plural)
он / она / оноhe / she / itониthey

Remember from the last lesson: ты for one friend, вы for a stranger or a group.

Pronunciation tip

он is a clean «on», but она and оно reduce the first, unstressed о to [a]: say a-NA and a-NO, with the stress on the end.

они is a-NEE — again the unstressed о becomes [a], and the и is a clear «ee».

The -й in музей is a short «y» glide, like the end of English «boy»: moo-ZYEY.

Culture note

Gender is grammar, not biology: книга (book) is feminine and стол (table) is masculine simply because of how the words end. Don't look for meaning in it — learn the ending and the gender comes free.

Russians regularly drop subject pronouns when the verb already makes the person clear, and use full pronouns like он/она to point at objects around the room. Getting comfortable calling a table «он» is a real beginner milestone.
