Verbs of Motion: Prefixes

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Add prefixes to motion verbs to express direction: arrive, leave, enter, exit, cross.
  • Understand that prefix + unidirectional verb = perfective, prefix + multidirectional = imperfective.
  • Use по- to mean «set off / start going» (пойти, поехать).
  • Pair prefixed motion verbs correctly (прийти / приходить, уйти / уходить).
  • Build precise sentences about coming, going, entering and leaving.

No, I left home earlier and set off on foot.

Grammar

Prefixes add direction — and aspect

Once you know идти/ходить and ехать/ездить, you build a whole map of directions by adding prefixes. A prefix does two jobs at once: it gives a precise direction (in, out, up to, away, across) and it sets the aspect.

Base verb+ prefix gives…Aspect
unidirectional (идти, ехать)прийти, войти, уехать…perfective (one completed action)
multidirectional (ходить, ездить)приходить, входить, уезжать…imperfective (repeated / general)

So прийти (from идти) = perfective «to arrive», and its imperfective partner приходить (from ходить) = «to come / arrive habitually». This is the standard way prefixed motion verbs form aspect pairs.

по- = «set off»

The prefix по- turns a motion verb into the perfective «to start going / to head off». Я пойду в магазин = «I'll go (set off for) the store». Они поехали на дачу = «They set off for the dacha». In the future and past, по- is the everyday way to say someone «went» somewhere.

Spelling note: a soft sign appears

When a prefix ending in a consonant meets идти, a soft sign or vowel slips in for pronunciation: войти, выйти, подойти, отойти, перейти, прийти (the й keeps the syllable smooth). With ехать you get въехать, выехать, подъехать with a hard sign ъ.

Pronunciation tip

The past tense of -йти verbs drops the «и»: пришёл / пришла, ушёл / ушла, вошёл / вошла. Stress the ё in the masculine: pri-SHOL.

войти is two syllables: vah-EE-tee; the ъ in въехать just means «restart the vowel»: VYE-khat'.

Don't confuse прийти (come, on foot) with приехать (come, by vehicle) — pick the base verb by how the person travels.

Culture note

Prefixed motion verbs are everywhere in everyday Russian invitations and instructions. Заходи! («Come in / drop by!») is a warm, casual invitation; Проходите, пожалуйста («Come on through, please») is what a host says at the door. Both are prefixed motion verbs doing social work.

The verb зайти (за- + идти) is a favourite: it means «to drop in briefly» on the way somewhere else. Я зайду к тебе после работы means «I'll stop by your place after work». This casual «popping in» culture — unannounced visits to friends and neighbours — is more common in Russia than in many Western countries, and the language has the verbs to match it.

Talk about it

  • Во сколько вы обычно уходите из дома утром и во сколько приходите домой? — What time do you usually leave home in the morning and what time do you get home?
  • Расскажите, как вы добираетесь сюда: куда вы выходите, что переходите, куда заходите? — Describe how you get here: where you exit, what you cross, where you stop in.
  • Куда вы хотите поехать в следующем отпуске? — Where do you want to go on your next vacation?
  • Когда к вам в последний раз приходили гости? Что вы делали? — When did guests last come to visit you? What did you do?
  • Опишите дорогу от вашего дома до ближайшего магазина. — Describe the route from your home to the nearest shop.
