Accusative Case: Direct Objects

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Understand the accusative case as the «direct object» — what answers что? / кого?
  • Recognise that inanimate masculine and neuter nouns look exactly like the dictionary form.
  • Change feminine nouns: -а → -у and -я → -ю (книга → книгу).
  • Use the genitive-like ending for animate masculine nouns (брат → брата).
  • Use common verbs that take the accusative: читать, любить, смотреть, есть.

Dialogue

Катя

Дима, что ты читаешь?

Dima, what are you reading?

Дима

Я читаю интересную книгу.

I'm reading an interesting book.

Катя

А я люблю газету по утрам.

And I like a newspaper in the mornings.

Дима

Газету? Я больше люблю журнал.

A newspaper? I prefer a magazine.

Катя

Понятно. А что ты смотришь вечером?

I see. And what do you watch in the evening?

Дима

Я смотрю новый фильм.

I'm watching a new film.

Катя

Я тоже люблю фильмы. И музыку.

I love films too. And music.

Дима

А ты любишь свою собаку?

And do you love your dog?

Катя

Конечно! Я очень люблю собаку.

Of course! I love the dog very much.

Дима

А кого ты ждёшь сейчас?

And whom are you waiting for now?

Катя

Я жду брата. Он скоро здесь.

I'm waiting for my brother. He'll be here soon.

Дима

Брата? А где сестра?

Your brother? And where's your sister?

Катя

Сестру я не жду, она дома.

I'm not waiting for my sister, she's at home.

Дима

Кстати, ты хочешь есть?

By the way, do you want to eat?

Катя

Да! Я ем пиццу. А ты?

Yes! I'm eating pizza. And you?

Дима

Я ем суп. И пишу письмо.

I'm eating soup. And writing a letter.

Катя

Письмо? Кому?

A letter? To whom?

Дима

Бабушке. Она любит письма.

To Grandma. She loves letters.

Катя

Как мило! Ну, я жду брата. Пока!

How sweet! Well, I'm waiting for my brother. Bye!

Grammar

The accusative = the «victim» of the verb

Your second case. The accusative marks the direct object — the thing the action is done to. In «I read a book», «a book» is the direct object. Ask что? (what?) for things and кого? (whom?) for people and animals: Я читаю что? — книгу.

Good news: two genders barely change

Inanimate masculine nouns (things, not people/animals) and all neuter nouns look exactly like the dictionary form in the accusative. Nothing to do!

Feminine: -а → -у, -я → -ю

Feminine nouns are the visible change. Swap the final vowel.

DictionaryAccusativeExample
книгакнигуЯ люблю книгу.
газетагазетуЯ читаю газету.
музыкамузыкуЯ слушаю музыку.
неделянеделю(swap -я → -ю)

Animate masculine: borrow the genitive ending -а / -я

When the masculine object is alive (a person or animal), it does NOT stay the same — it takes -а (or -я after soft consonants), the same ending as the genitive case you'll meet later.

DictionaryAccusative (animate)Example
братбратаЯ жду брата.
другдругаЯ люблю друга.

Feminine animate nouns (сестра, собака) just follow the normal feminine rule: сестру, собаку. Verbs that trigger the accusative here: читать (read), любить (love), смотреть (watch), есть (eat), ждать (wait for).

Pronunciation tip

Hear the swapped vowel: the difference between книга (KNEE-ga) and книгу (KNEE-goo) is small but real — the final sound moves from [a] to [oo].

кого is spelled with г but said with [v]: say «ka-VO», not «ka-GO». The same trick appears in many endings.

люблю has a soft Л: press the middle of your tongue to the roof — «lyoo-BLYOO». The soft л and the я-vowel make it gentle.

Culture note

The animate/inanimate split feels strange to English speakers, but it carries real meaning: it lets Russian keep flexible word order. Because брата is clearly the object no matter where it sits, you can say Брата я жду for emphasis and still be understood.

The verb любить covers a huge range — from «I love you» to «I like coffee» (Я люблю кофе). Russians use it freely for food, music and hobbies, so don't worry that it sounds too strong; context makes it clear.
