Greetings & Introductions

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Greet people formally and informally at any time of day.
  • Ask and answer “How are you?” in a simple way.
  • Say your name and ask someone else's name with как вас/тебя зовут.
  • Say where you are from with я из + place.
  • Choose between ты (informal) and вы (formal/plural).

Dialogue

Анна

Здравствуйте! Меня зовут Анна.

Hello! My name is Anna.

Иван

Здравствуйте, Анна. Очень приятно. Я Иван.

Hello, Anna. Very nice to meet you. I'm Ivan.

Анна

Очень приятно, Иван. Как дела?

Nice to meet you, Ivan. How are you?

Иван

Хорошо, спасибо. А у вас?

Good, thank you. And you?

Анна

Тоже хорошо. Откуда вы?

Also good. Where are you from?

Иван

Я из Москвы. А вы?

I'm from Moscow. And you?

Анна

Я из Лондона.

I'm from London.

Иван

О, из Лондона! Вы говорите по-русски очень хорошо.

Oh, from London! You speak Russian very well.

Анна

Спасибо большое! Немного.

Thank you very much! A little.

Иван

Анна, это мой друг Том.

Anna, this is my friend Tom.

Том

Привет, Анна! Как дела?

Hi, Anna! How are you?

Анна

Привет, Том! Нормально. А у тебя?

Hi, Tom! Okay. And you?

Том

Тоже нормально. Ты студентка?

Also okay. Are you a student?

Анна

Да, я студентка. А ты?

Yes, I'm a student. And you?

Том

Я тоже студент.

I'm a student too.

Иван

Хорошо! Уже поздно. До свидания, Анна!

Great! It's late already. Goodbye, Anna!

Анна

До свидания, Иван! Пока, Том!

Goodbye, Ivan! Bye, Tom!

Том

Пока, Анна! До завтра.

Bye, Anna! See you tomorrow.

Grammar

ты or вы? Two words for “you”

Russian has two words for “you”. Choosing the right one is the first rule of politeness.

WordUse it for…
тыone friend, a child, family — informal
выa stranger, an elder, a boss, OR several people — formal/plural

So the “name” question changes with it: Как тебя зовут? (informal) vs Как вас зовут? (formal).

Saying your name: literally “they call me…”

Russian doesn't say “my name is”. It says меня зовут… — literally “(they) call me…”. Just slot your name on the end.

RussianLiterallyMeaning
Меня зовут Анна.me they-call AnnaMy name is Anna.
Как тебя зовут?how you they-call?What's your name?

There is no “am / is / are”

In the present tense Russian drops the verb “to be”. Я студент = literally “I student” = “I am a student”. Это мой друг = “This (is) my friend”.

Pronunciation tip

здравствуйте looks terrifying but the first в is silent: say ZDRAST-vooy-tye. It's the single most useful word to drill.

Unstressed О sounds like [a]: хорошо is ha-ra-SHO, not “ho-ro-sho”. Only the stressed syllable keeps a clear [o].

Roll a short Р: the р in привет and добрый is a quick tongue-tap, like Spanish or Italian r.

Culture note

Russians tend to reserve broad smiles for people they actually know — a neutral face with a stranger is normal politeness, not coldness. Save привет and пока for friends and peers; with anyone older or in any official setting, здравствуйте and до свидания are expected.

When asked Как дела?, the default answer is a modest нормально or хорошо. Gushing that everything is wonderful can sound insincere; a calm, measured reply feels natural.
