Cyrillic IV: Signs, Stress & Vowel Reduction

What you'll learn

  • Read the final three symbols: Ы, the soft sign Ь, and the hard sign Ъ — completing all 33 letters.
  • Locate stress and understand that only one vowel per word is stressed.
  • Apply vowel reduction: unstressed О → [a] (akanye), unstressed Е/Я → [i].
  • Recognise palatalisation — how Ь and и/е/ё/ю/я soften the consonant before them.
  • Apply final-consonant devoicing (друг → [druk], город → [górat]).

Grammar

The last three symbols

Three symbols remain — and with them you will have met all 33 letters of the Russian alphabet.

LetterSounds likeExample word
Ы ыa hard [i]: say «ee» but pull the tongue back and downты (you), сын (son)
Ь ьthe soft sign — no sound of its own; it softens the consonant before itсоль (salt), день (day)
Ъ ъthe hard sign — no sound; a rare separator that keeps a hard consonant before a [y]-vowelобъект (object)

Ы has no English equivalent. Try saying «ee» (как и) and then, without moving your lips, draw the tongue back as if you were about to say «u» in «but». That tense, back «i» is Ы. Compare мы (we) with ми.

Rule 1 — Stress is king, and unpredictable

Every Russian word has exactly one stressed vowel, and where it falls is not predictable from spelling — you learn it per word (dictionaries mark it with an accent, e.g. хорошо́). Stress matters because everything else (the reduction rules below) depends on it. The stressed vowel is pronounced full and clear; unstressed vowels get squeezed.

Rule 2 — Vowel reduction (akanye)

Unstressed vowels weaken:

SpellingUnstressed soundExample
unstressed О→ [a]хорошо́ = ha-ra-SHO; молоко́ = ma-la-KO
unstressed Е, Я→ [i]сестра́ = sis-TRA; язы́к = yi-ZIK

This is why хорошо has three «o»s on paper but only the last one sounds like [o]; the first two become [a]. Russians call this аканье (akanye).

Rule 3 — Palatalisation (softening)

A consonant is soft (palatalised) when it is followed by the soft sign Ь or by one of the soft vowels и, е, ё, ю, я. Softening means raising the middle of the tongue toward the roof of the mouth — a tiny «y»-colouring of the consonant.

HardSoftWhat changed
брат (brother)брать (to take)Ь softens the final Т
мать-words with адень, сольЬ softens Н, Л

Rule 4 — Final-consonant devoicing

At the end of a word, a voiced consonant turns voiceless: Б→[p], В→[f], Г→[k], Д→[t], Ж→[sh], З→[s]. So друг sounds like drook, and город like GO-rat.

Pronunciation tip

Spelling ≠ sound: Russian looks phonetic but isn't quite — stress, reduction, softening and devoicing all reshape the word. Always learn a word together with its stress.

Drill akanye: say молоко as ma-la-KO, not «mo-lo-ko». Reducing the unstressed О to [a] is the fastest way to stop sounding like a textbook.

Hear the soft sign: say брат, then брать — for the second, let your tongue glide toward a faint «y» on the way off the Т. Same with соль (sol-y) and день (dyen-y).

Devoice the ending: друг ends in a clean [k], город in a clean [t]. Don't add a vowel after it.

Reading: Read these aloud

Это мой брат.

Где молоко?

Вот соль.

Он хороший друг.

У нас большая семья.

Translation

This is my brother. / Where is the milk? / Here is the salt. / He is a good friend. / We have a big family.

Culture note

Why does Russian sound so different from how it's spelled? Because the writing fixes the letters, but speech adds three invisible layers on top: stress (one strong syllable per word), reduction (unstressed О and Е shrink toward [a] and [i]), and palatalisation (a whole parallel set of «soft» consonants the alphabet only hints at). A Russian word is really a little melody, and the spelling is just the sheet music.

The soft and hard signs (Ь, Ъ) are ghosts of older sounds — once short vowels, now silent traffic signals telling you how to pronounce their neighbours. The 1918 spelling reform swept away several truly redundant letters and slimmed the alphabet to today's 33, but kept these two because Russian genuinely needs the hard/soft distinction they mark. Master stress and akanye and you'll instantly sound less like a textbook and more like a person.
