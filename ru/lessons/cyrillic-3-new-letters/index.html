Grammar

The genuinely new letters

So far the letters either looked familiar (Lesson 00) or were false friends in disguise (Lesson 01). Now meet the letters that have no Latin look-alike at all — you simply learn each shape. We'll take them in sensible groups.

Group 3 — the iotated vowels

These four vowels each begin with a hidden [y] glide. Think of them as «vowel-with-a-y in front».

LetterSounds likeExample word
Я я[ya] in yardязык (language)
Ю ю[yu] in youюг (south)
Ё ё[yo] in yonderёж (hedgehog)
Е е[ye] in yes (met in Lesson 01)нет (no)

Ё is always stressed and always [yo] — but in print the two dots are often dropped, so you may see it written as Е. Context tells you which.

Pronunciation tip

Ж, Ш, Ц are always HARD: no matter what vowel follows, these three never soften. жи and ши are pronounced as if «жы / шы» — that's why шесть sounds like shest, with a flat, hard «sh».

Ч and Щ are always SOFT: Ч is a soft [ch] as in cheese; Щ is a longer, softer hiss — hold it: shchee (щи). Many learners simply lengthen the «sh».

Ц = [ts]: one sound, like the end of cats. цирк = tsirk.

The hidden [y]: at the start of a word Я/Ю/Ё/Е really do say «y + vowel»: я = ya, юг = yook, ёж = yozh. Inside a word they instead soften the consonant before them — more on that next lesson.

Culture note

The four iotated vowels (Я, Ю, Ё, Е) are Cyrillic's clever shorthand: instead of writing a separate «y» plus a vowel, one letter carries both. That hidden [y] does double duty — at the start of a word it sounds out loud (я = «ya»), and inside a word it quietly softens the consonant in front of it. This is why Russian needs fewer letters than you'd expect for so many sounds.

The letter Ё has a quietly political history: officially it dates to 1783, but Russians routinely write it as plain Е, leaving the dots off. There are even rare minimal pairs — все (everyone) vs всё (everything) — where the missing dots can change the meaning. The town of Ulyanovsk erected a monument to Ё in 2005.
