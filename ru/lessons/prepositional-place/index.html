Prepositional Case: Saying Where

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Understand what a «case» is and why Russian nouns change their endings.
  • Use the prepositional case to say where something or someone is located.
  • Form the regular -е ending (Москва → в Москве, стол → на столе).
  • Use the special -ии ending for words in -ия / -ие (Россия → в России).
  • Choose correctly between в (in) and на (on / at) with places.

Dialogue

Олег

Привет, Маша! Где ты сейчас?

Маша

Привет, Олег! Я на работе.

Hi, Oleg! I'm at work.

Олег

А где ты работаешь?

And where do you work?

Маша

Я работаю в магазине в центре.

I work in a shop downtown.

Олег

In what city?

Маша

В Москве. А ты сейчас дома?

In Moscow. And are you at home now?

Олег

No, I'm at school. I'm a teacher.

On Pushkin Street. It's in the centre.

Маша

Понятно. А где сейчас твои книги?

They're here, on the table, in the room.

Grammar

What is a «case»? Why endings change

This is a big moment: your first Russian case. In English, word order does the work — «Moscow» looks the same in «I love Moscow» and «in Moscow». In Russian, the ending of a noun changes to show its job in the sentence. These different endings are called cases, and there are six. Don't panic — we take them one at a time.

The prepositional case answers the question где? (where?). You almost always see it after the prepositions в (in) and на (on / at). It is the only case that never appears without a preposition — that's why it's called «prepositional».

The regular ending: add -е

For most nouns, the rule is wonderfully simple: take the dictionary (nominative) form and put -е on the end. Masculine nouns ending in a consonant just add -е; feminine nouns in -а / -я swap it for -е.

Dictionary formGenderIn the prepositionalMeaning
столmasc.на столеon the table
городmasc.в городеin the city
Москваfem.в Москвеin Moscow
школаfem.в школеat school
окноneut.в окнеin the window

The -ии exception

Nouns ending in -ия or -ие don't take a plain -е. Instead the ending becomes -ии. This is a small but very common group.

Россияв Россииin Russia
Англияв Англииin England
лекцияна лекцииat the lecture

в or на?

Both can mean «at». Use в for enclosed spaces and most places (в доме, в городе, в магазине). Use на for surfaces (на столе) and for a fixed set of «open» or «event» words you simply memorise: на работе (at work), на улице (outside / on the street), на почте (at the post office), на уроке (in the lesson).

Pronunciation tip

The new -е ending is pronounced [ye]: в Москве sounds like «v mask-VYE». The в preposition glues straight onto the next word with no pause.

Unstressed О becomes [a]: город is «GO-rat», but in в городе the stress stays on the first syllable — «v GO-ra-dye».

-ии sounds like a long [ee-ee]: в России is «v ra-SEE-yee» — let the two и's ring.

Culture note

Russians say на работе (literally «on work») and на почте (at the post office) where English uses «at». There's no deep logic to memorise — these «на» words are simply learned one by one, and even native children just absorb them.

Two of Russia's great cities give you instant practice: Москва → в Москве and Санкт-Петербург → в Санкт-Петербурге. Asking В каком городе вы живёте? («In what city do you live?») is a friendly, normal opener with a new acquaintance.
