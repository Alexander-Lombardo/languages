Work, Studies & Professions

What you'll learn

  • Name common professions and use them with sein WITHOUT an article (Ich bin Lehrer).
  • Form feminine professions with -in (Lehrer → Lehrerin, Arzt → Ärztin).
  • Say what you do with arbeiten als and what you study with studieren.
  • Ask and answer Was machst du beruflich? and Was studierst du?
  • Talk about your workplace and field of study.
  • Describe daily working life with simple, accurate sentences.

Dialogue

Markus

Und was machst du beruflich, Anna?

And what do you do for a living, Anna?

Anna

Ich bin Ärztin. Ich arbeite in einem Krankenhaus.

I'm a doctor. I work in a hospital.

Markus

Interessant! Macht dir die Arbeit Spaß?

Interesting! Do you enjoy the work?

Anna

Ja, sehr. Es ist anstrengend, aber schön. Und du?

Yes, very much. It's exhausting but rewarding. And you?

Markus

Ich bin noch Student. Ich studiere Informatik.

I'm still a student. I'm studying computer science.

Anna

An welcher Universität studierst du?

At which university are you studying?

Markus

In München. Nebenbei arbeite ich als Programmierer.

In Munich. On the side I work as a programmer.

Anna

Und verdienst du gut dabei?

And do you earn well doing that?

Markus

Es geht. Aber das Fach gefällt mir wirklich.

It's okay. But I really like the subject.

Anna

Das ist das Wichtigste. Viel Erfolg im Studium!

That's the most important thing. Good luck with your studies!

Markus

Danke! Und dir viel Kraft im Krankenhaus.

Thanks! And lots of strength to you at the hospital.

Grammar

Professions: no article with sein

This is the rule that surprises English speakers most. To say what someone is professionally, German uses sein + the bare profession — no article. English says "I am a teacher"; German just says "I am teacher."

GermanEnglish
Ich bin Lehrer.I am a teacher.
Sie ist Ärztin.She is a doctor.
Er ist Student.He is a student.

You only add an article when you also add an adjective: Er ist ein guter Lehrer. (He is a good teacher.) Otherwise, leave it out.

Feminine forms with -in

Most professions form the feminine by adding -in to the masculine. If the masculine has a/o/u, it often takes an umlaut too. The plural feminine ends in -innen.

MasculineFeminineMeaning

der Arztdie Ärztindoctor

arbeiten als & studieren

Use arbeiten als + bare profession to say what you work as, and studieren + subject for university study. Studieren is only for higher education — for school or learning generally, use lernen.

  • Ich arbeite als Programmierer. — I work as a programmer. (no article)
  • Ich studiere Medizin / Informatik / Jura. — I study medicine / computer science / law.
  • Ich arbeite bei einer Firma / in einem Büro. — I work at a company / in an office. (dative)

Pronunciation tip

The -er ending of professions like Lehrer, Kellner, Programmierer is a soft, almost vowel-like "-uh", not a hard American "-er". And watch the stress in loanwords: Ingenieur is "in-zhen-YÖR" (French-style, stress on the last syllable) and Student / studieren stress the second syllable, not the first.

Reading: Mein Beruf

Ich heiße Sabine und ich bin Lehrerin. Ich arbeite an einer Schule in Hamburg und unterrichte Englisch und Geschichte. Mein Mann ist Ingenieur und arbeitet bei einer großen Firma.

Früher habe ich an der Universität studiert. Mein Studium hat fünf Jahre gedauert. Die Arbeit mit den Kindern macht mir viel Spaß, aber sie ist auch anstrengend. Am Wochenende muss ich oft die Hefte korrigieren. Trotzdem liebe ich meinen Beruf.

Translation

My name is Sabine and I'm a teacher. I work at a school in Hamburg and teach English and history. My husband is an engineer and works at a big company.

Earlier I studied at university. My studies lasted five years. Working with the children gives me a lot of joy, but it's also exhausting. On weekends I often have to grade the notebooks. Even so, I love my job.

Culture note

Germany is famous for its duale Ausbildung — a dual vocational training system in which young people split their time between a company (paid, hands-on) and a vocational school (Berufsschule). Not everyone goes to university; becoming a skilled Handwerker (tradesperson), a Bankkaufmann or a Mechatroniker through an Ausbildung is a respected, well-paid path, and many trades are protected by the Meister qualification.

Titles matter in professional German life: people commonly hold a Dr. and put it on doorbells and business cards. At work, colleagues often stay on Sie with a surname for a long time, though startups and younger firms increasingly switch to du quickly. When you describe your job, Germans tend to value precision — so the difference between Ingenieur, Techniker and Mechaniker is taken seriously.

Talk about it

  • Was machst du beruflich, oder was möchtest du gern werden? — What do you do for a living, or what would you like to become?
  • Was sind die Vor- und Nachteile deines Berufs? — What are the advantages and disadvantages of your job?
  • Welches Fach hast du studiert oder würdest du gern studieren? — What subject did you study, or would you like to study?
  • Ist dir ein gutes Gehalt oder interessante Arbeit wichtiger? — Is a good salary or interesting work more important to you?
  • Arbeitest du lieber im Büro oder von zu Hause aus? — Do you prefer to work in the office or from home?
