The Genitive Case

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Use the genitive to show possession (das Auto meines Vaters, die Farbe des Hauses).
  • Form the genitive articles: des/eines (masc./neut.) and der/einer (fem./plural).
  • Add -s or -es to masculine and neuter nouns in the genitive.
  • Use the genitive prepositions während, wegen, trotz, statt and innerhalb.
  • Recognise and use the common spoken alternative von + dative.

Dialogue

Lena

Wessen Auto steht denn vor dem Haus?

Whose car is parked in front of the house?

Tom

Das ist das Auto meines Vaters. Er besucht uns heute.

That's my father's car. He's visiting us today.

Lena

Schön! Und wegen des Wetters bleibt ihr drinnen?

Nice! And because of the weather you're staying inside?

Tom

Ja, trotz des Regens wollten wir eigentlich spazieren gehen.

Yes, despite the rain we actually wanted to go for a walk.

Lena

Während des Besuchs könnt ihr ja Karten spielen.

During the visit you could play cards.

Tom

Gute Idee. Die Farbe der Wände im Wohnzimmer gefällt ihm bestimmt.

Good idea. He'll surely like the colour of the walls in the living room.

Lena

Ist das nicht das Haus deiner Großeltern gewesen?

Wasn't this your grandparents' house?

Tom

Genau, innerhalb der Familie geht es schon seit Jahren weiter.

Exactly, within the family it has been passed on for years.

Lena

Statt eines neuen Hauses ein altes zu erben — wie schön.

Inheriting an old house instead of a new one — how lovely.

Tom

Im Gespräch sagen wir aber meistens „das Auto von meinem Vater“.

In conversation, though, we mostly say “my father's car” (with von).

Grammar

What the genitive does

The genitive is the case of possession and belonging — the German equivalent of English „of“ or „'s“. The owner goes into the genitive: das Auto meines Vaters (my father's car), die Farbe des Hauses (the colour of the house). The question word is wessen? (whose?).

The genitive articles and noun endings

Two things change. First, the article: masculine and neuter become des / eines, while feminine and plural become der / einer. Second — and this is unique to the genitive — masculine and neuter nouns themselves add -s (or -es after a short or hard-to-say word). Feminine and plural nouns add nothing.

 masc.neut.fem.plural
definitedes Vatersdes Hausesder Mutterder Kinder
indefiniteeines Vaterseines Hauseseiner Mutter— (kein) Kinder

So: one-syllable nouns usually take -es (des Hauses, des Kindes, des Grundes), while longer nouns take just -s (des Vaters, des Lehrers, des Autos). Feminine and plural nouns stay unchanged: die Farbe der Wände, das Ende der Geschichte.

Genitive prepositions

A handful of common prepositions take the genitive. Learn these five first:

The spoken shortcut: von + dative

In everyday speech the genitive of possession is often replaced by von + dative. Both are correct; the genitive is more written/formal, the von-version more colloquial:

  • das Auto meines Vaters (genitive) = das Auto von meinem Vater (spoken)
  • die Freundin meiner Schwester = die Freundin von meiner Schwester

You'll even hear the genitive prepositions take the dative in casual speech (wegen dem Wetter) — common, but still considered non-standard, so use the genitive in writing.

Pronunciation tip

The genitive prepositions are short and easily blurred — keep them crisp: trotz ends in -ts (like z), statt is just „shtat“, and während has the ä = English e in „bed“ plus a final devoiced -d → „VAY-rent“. The -es genitive ending (des Hauses) is an unstressed „-es“: „HOW-zes“.

Reading: Das Haus der Familie

Das alte Haus am Ende der Straße gehört seit Generationen der Familie Weber. Die Eltern meines Freundes leben dort, und während des Sommers kommen oft die Kinder der Verwandten zu Besuch.

Die Farbe der Fassade ist verblasst, und wegen des Alters braucht das Dach bald eine Reparatur. Trotz der hohen Kosten will niemand das Haus verkaufen. „Der Wert dieses Hauses ist nicht das Geld“, sagt die Großmutter. „Es ist die Bedeutung der Erinnerungen.“

Innerhalb der Familie spricht man oft über die Zukunft des Hauses. Statt eines Verkaufs planen sie eine Renovierung. So bleibt das Haus der Familie auch in den Händen der nächsten Generation.

Translation

The old house at the end of the street has belonged to the Weber family for generations. My friend's parents live there, and during the summer the relatives' children often come to visit.

The colour of the façade has faded, and because of its age the roof will soon need a repair. Despite the high costs nobody wants to sell the house. “The value of this house is not the money,” says the grandmother. “It's the meaning of the memories.”

Within the family they often talk about the future of the house. Instead of a sale they are planning a renovation. That way the house stays in the family's hands, in the hands of the next generation too.

Culture note

The genitive has a curious reputation in German itself. A popular 2004 bestseller by Bastian Sick, Der Dativ ist dem Genitiv sein Tod („The dative is the death of the genitive“), playfully lamented how spoken German increasingly swaps the genitive for von + dative — and used a deliberately ungrammatical title to make the point. In careful writing, formal speech, journalism and law the genitive is alive and well; in casual conversation you'll hear wegen dem Regen and das Handy von meiner Schwester all the time. Knowing both registers — the polished genitive for writing, the relaxed von-form for chatting — is exactly the kind of flexibility B1 is about.

Talk about it

  • Wessen Auto, Haus oder Idee bewunderst du? Beschreibe es im Genitiv. — Whose car, house or idea do you admire? Describe it using the genitive.
  • Benutzt du im Gespräch lieber den Genitiv oder „von“ + Dativ? Warum? — In conversation do you prefer the genitive or von + dative? Why?
  • Was machst du normalerweise während des Wochenendes? — What do you usually do during the weekend?
  • Trotz welcher Schwierigkeiten lernst du weiter Deutsch? — Despite which difficulties do you keep learning German?
  • Was ist die Bedeutung deines Lieblingsortes für dich? — What is the meaning of your favourite place for you?
