Describing People & Things

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Describe people and things using adjectives after sein, werden and bleiben.
  • Know that a predicate adjective never takes an ending: Das Auto ist neu, sie wird müde.
  • Use degree adverbs (sehr, ziemlich, zu, ganz, total) to soften or strengthen a description.
  • Recognise and pair up common opposite adjectives (groß/klein, alt/jung, billig/teuer).
  • Describe someone's appearance and character in a few simple sentences.

Dialogue

Lena

Schau mal, das ist mein neuer Kollege, Tom.

Look, that's my new colleague, Tom.

Anna

Ah, ist er nett?

Oh, is he nice?

Lena

Ja, er ist sehr freundlich. Und ziemlich groß!

Yes, he's very friendly. And quite tall!

Anna

Wie sieht er aus? Sind seine Haare dunkel?

What does he look like? Is his hair dark?

Lena

Seine Haare sind kurz und seine Augen sind blau.

His hair is short and his eyes are blue.

Anna

Und der Job? Ist die Arbeit interessant?

And the job? Is the work interesting?

Lena

Total interessant, aber am Abend bin ich immer müde.

Totally interesting, but in the evening I'm always tired.

Anna

Das verstehe ich. Bist du trotzdem glücklich?

I understand that. Are you happy anyway?

Lena

Ja, ich bin sehr glücklich. Die Stimmung im Büro ist gut.

Yes, I'm very happy. The mood in the office is good.

Anna

Schön! Hoffentlich bleibt das so.

Lovely! Hopefully it stays that way.

Grammar

Predicate adjectives take NO ending

When an adjective comes after the verbs sein (to be), werden (to become) or bleiben (to stay), it describes the subject but stays in its plain dictionary form — it never takes an ending, no matter the gender or number of the noun.

SentenceEnglish
Das Auto ist neu.The car is new.
Die Frau ist nett.The woman is nice.
Die Kinder sind müde.The children are tired.
Sie wird müde.She is getting tired.
Das Wetter bleibt schön.The weather stays nice.

Notice that neu, nett, müde, schön all stay identical even though the nouns are der/die/das/plural. That is the easy news: after sein/werden/bleiben there is nothing to add.

Endings only appear when the adjective sits in front of a noun (ein neues Auto). Those attributive endings come later (lesson 28) — you do not need them yet.

Degree adverbs: how strong is it?

Put a small adverb in front of the adjective to dial the description up or down. These adverbs themselves never change.

AdverbMeaningExample
sehrveryEr ist sehr nett.
ziemlichquite / fairlyDas Buch ist ziemlich lang.
ganzquite / prettyDie Suppe ist ganz gut.
totaltotally (informal)Der Film war total langweilig.
zutoo (excessive)Der Kaffee ist zu heiß.

Be careful with zu: it means "too much", not "very". Zu teuer = too expensive (a problem); sehr teuer = very expensive (just a fact).

Opposites come in pairs

The fastest way to build a describing vocabulary is to learn adjectives as opposite pairs:

  • groß ↔ klein (big ↔ small)
  • alt ↔ jung / neu (old ↔ young / new)
  • schön ↔ hässlich (beautiful ↔ ugly)
  • teuer ↔ billig (expensive ↔ cheap)
  • interessant ↔ langweilig (interesting ↔ boring)

Pronunciation tip

The ending -lich (in freundlich, glücklich, hässlich) and the ending -ig (in billig, langweilig) both end in the soft "ich-sound" — a gentle breath like the h in "huge", not a hard k. So billig sounds like "BIL-likh", not "BIL-lig". The umlaut ü in müde is an "ee" said with rounded lips.

Reading: Meine beste Freundin

Meine beste Freundin heißt Sara. Sie ist nicht sehr groß, aber sehr sportlich. Ihre Haare sind lang und dunkel, und ihre Augen sind grün. Sara ist immer freundlich und total ehrlich. Manchmal ist sie ein bisschen chaotisch, aber nie langweilig. Wir sind seit zehn Jahren Freundinnen, und ich bin sehr glücklich darüber.

Translation

My best friend is called Sara. She is not very tall, but very sporty. Her hair is long and dark, and her eyes are green. Sara is always friendly and totally honest. Sometimes she is a bit chaotic, but never boring. We have been friends for ten years, and I am very happy about that.

Culture note

Germans have a reputation for plain speaking, and that shows in how they describe things. A flat "Das ist langweilig" or "zu teuer" is normal and not considered rude — directness is read as honesty, not unkindness. If a German friend calls your idea ganz gut, take it as genuine praise rather than a polite brush-off; understated compliments are common.

When describing people, Germans are careful with words about appearance and tend to lead with character traits like freundlich, ehrlich (honest) or zuverlässig (reliable). The word nett is friendly but mild; calling someone sehr nett is warm, while just nett can sound a little lukewarm, much like "nice" in English.

Talk about it

  • Wie sieht dein bester Freund oder deine beste Freundin aus? — What does your best friend look like?
  • Welche drei Adjektive beschreiben deinen Charakter? — Which three adjectives describe your character?
  • Was ist dir bei einem Menschen wichtiger: nett oder interessant? — What is more important to you in a person: nice or interesting?
  • Beschreibe dein Zimmer mit drei Adjektiven. — Describe your room with three adjectives.
  • Wann bist du am glücklichsten? — When are you happiest?
