What you'll learn

  • Use adverbial connectors (deshalb, trotzdem, außerdem…) in first position and trigger verb-second inversion.
  • Tell adverbial, coordinating and subordinating connectors apart by what they do to word order.
  • Express cause, contrast, addition and consequence smoothly between sentences.
  • Understand and use a set of high-frequency everyday idioms (Redewendungen).
  • Sound more fluent by linking ideas instead of listing short sentences.

Dialogue

Lena

Du siehst müde aus. Alles okay?

You look tired. Everything okay?

Markus

Die Woche war anstrengend, deshalb bin ich ziemlich erschöpft.

The week was exhausting, that's why I'm pretty worn out.

Lena

Trotzdem kommst du heute Abend mit, oder?

Still, you're coming along tonight, right?

Markus

Eigentlich wollte ich absagen. Außerdem habe ich noch viel zu tun.

Actually I wanted to cancel. Besides, I still have a lot to do.

Lena

Komm schon, du musst auch mal die Seele baumeln lassen.

Come on, you have to relax and let your soul dangle (unwind) too.

Markus

Da hast du recht. Also gut, ich komme mit.

You're right there. All right then, I'll come along.

Lena

Super! Sonst hätte ich dich überredet — du kennst mich ja.

Great! Otherwise I'd have talked you into it — you know me.

Markus

Ich weiß. Bei dir verstehe ich immer nur Bahnhof, wenn es ums Nein-Sagen geht.

I know. With you I never understand a word ("only understand train station") when it comes to saying no.

Lena

Genau. Dann treffen wir uns um acht, jedoch nicht später!

Exactly. Then we'll meet at eight, but not later!

Markus

Abgemacht. Ich drücke mir selbst die Daumen, dass ich wach bleibe.

Deal. I'll keep my own fingers crossed that I stay awake.

Grammar

Three families of connectors

German connectors fall into three groups, and the only thing you really have to track is what each one does to the verb. Get that right and your sentences click together.

TypeExamplesEffect on word order
Coordinatingund, aber, oder, denn, sondernno change — verb stays second (position 0)
Subordinatingweil, dass, wenn, obwohl, nachdemverb goes to the end of its clause
Adverbialdeshalb, trotzdem, außerdem, jedoch, sonst, dann, alsotake first position → inversion (verb second)

Adverbial connectors and inversion

This lesson focuses on the third group. An adverbial connector is a full sentence element, so when you put it first, it occupies position 1 — and the conjugated verb must stay in position 2, before the subject. This "verb-second" inversion is the hallmark of these words.

Trotzdemkommeichmit.

Watch the contrast: weil vs deshalb

The same idea can be linked two ways, and word order is the giveaway. With the subordinating weil the verb goes to the end; with the adverbial deshalb the verb stays second:

  • Ich bin müde, weil die Woche anstrengend war. (verb at end)
  • Die Woche war anstrengend, deshalb bin ich müde. (inversion)

Everyday idioms (Redewendungen)

Idioms make you sound natural — but only if you use the whole fixed phrase. A useful starter set:

  • nur Bahnhof verstehen — to understand nothing at all (lit. "only understand train station")
  • die Daumen drücken — to keep one's fingers crossed (lit. "press the thumbs")
  • die Seele baumeln lassen — to relax / unwind (lit. "let the soul dangle")
  • über seinen Schatten springen — to overcome oneself (lit. "jump over one's shadow")
  • jemandem auf die Nerven gehen — to get on someone's nerves
  • ins Wasser fallen — to fall through / be cancelled
  • die Nase voll haben — to be fed up (lit. "have the nose full")

Pronunciation tip

The handy contrast pair deshalb and deswegen are stressed on the first syllable ("DES-halp", "DES-vay-gen"), but jedoch and trotzdem differ: "yay-DOKH" stresses the second syllable, "TROTS-dem" the first. With idioms, keep the words glued together as one melodic unit — die-Seele-baumeln-lassen — rather than four separate words, or the image is lost.

Reading: Eine anstrengende Woche

Diese Woche war wirklich stressig. Ich hatte jeden Tag Termine, deshalb hatte ich kaum Zeit zum Essen. Außerdem war mein Computer kaputt, also musste ich vieles mit dem Handy erledigen. Das war anstrengend, trotzdem habe ich alles geschafft.

Am Freitag wollte ich eigentlich früh ins Bett. Meine Freunde haben jedoch eine kleine Feier geplant, und absagen wollte ich nicht. Ich dachte: Jetzt habe ich so viel gearbeitet, also habe ich auch eine Pause verdient. Sonst werde ich am Ende noch krank.

Auf der Feier habe ich endlich die Seele baumeln lassen. Zuerst war ich noch müde, dann aber wurde es ein schöner Abend. Manchmal muss man eben über seinen eigenen Schatten springen und einfach Ja sagen.

Translation

This week was really stressful. I had appointments every day, that's why I barely had time to eat. Besides, my computer was broken, so I had to do a lot of things on my phone. That was exhausting; nevertheless I managed everything.

On Friday I actually wanted to go to bed early. My friends, however, had planned a little party, and I didn't want to cancel. I thought: now I've worked so much, so I've also earned a break. Otherwise I'll end up getting sick.

At the party I finally unwound ("let my soul dangle"). At first I was still tired, but then it turned into a lovely evening. Sometimes you just have to jump over your own shadow (overcome yourself) and simply say yes.

Culture note

German is rich in vivid Redewendungen, and locals drop them constantly — knowing a few signals that you've moved beyond textbook German. Several involve the body or the everyday world: you drücke die Daumen instead of crossing fingers, you verstehst nur Bahnhof when you're lost, and after a hard week you lässt die Seele baumeln. Take them literally and they're delightfully strange; that strangeness is exactly why they stick.

On the grammar side, the adverbial connectors are a marker of fluency for German ears. A learner who says Die Woche war stressig, deshalb bin ich müde — with the verb correctly in second position — sounds noticeably more advanced than one who strings clauses together with und. Reading any German newspaper, you'll see jedoch, trotzdem, außerdem and deshalb doing the connective heavy lifting paragraph after paragraph.

Talk about it

  • Beschreibe deine Woche mit den Wörtern deshalb, trotzdem und außerdem. — Describe your week using the words deshalb, trotzdem and außerdem.
  • Wie lässt du am liebsten die Seele baumeln? — How do you most like to unwind?
  • Gibt es eine Situation, in der du nur Bahnhof verstehst? — Is there a situation in which you understand nothing at all?
  • Wann musstest du zuletzt über deinen eigenen Schatten springen? — When did you last have to overcome yourself?
  • Welche Redewendung gefällt dir am besten, und warum? — Which idiom do you like best, and why?
