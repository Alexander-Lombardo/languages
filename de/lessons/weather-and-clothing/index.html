Weather, Seasons & Clothing

What you'll learn

  • Talk about the weather with impersonal es: Es regnet, es ist kalt, es gibt ein Gewitter.
  • Name the four seasons and the months they cover.
  • Use core weather and clothing vocabulary in full sentences.
  • Say what you are wearing with tragen and what you put on with anziehen.
  • Read and understand a short German text about the weather and seasons.

Dialogue

Anna

Wie ist das Wetter heute draußen?

What's the weather like outside today?

Tom

Es ist kalt und es regnet. Nimm einen Regenschirm mit!

It's cold and it's raining. Take an umbrella with you!

Anna

Oh nein. Gestern war die Sonne noch so schön.

Oh no. Yesterday the sun was still so nice.

Tom

Ja, aber im Herbst wechselt das Wetter schnell.

Yes, but in autumn the weather changes quickly.

Anna

Was soll ich anziehen? Eine Jacke oder den Mantel?

What should I put on? A jacket or the coat?

Tom

Zieh den Mantel an. Und nimm auch einen Schal.

Put on the coat. And take a scarf too.

Anna

Gut. Trägst du heute deinen Pullover?

Good. Are you wearing your sweater today?

Tom

Ja, und feste Schuhe. Draußen gibt es vielleicht ein Gewitter.

Yes, and sturdy shoes. There might be a thunderstorm outside.

Anna

Dann bleibe ich lieber zu Hause!

Then I'd rather stay at home!

Tom

Keine Sorge, morgen wird es wieder warm und sonnig.

Don't worry, tomorrow it'll be warm and sunny again.

Grammar

Talking about the weather with impersonal es

German describes the weather with a "dummy" subject es ("it"), exactly like English "it's raining". The es doesn't refer to anything — it's just there so the verb has a subject.

GermanEnglish
Es regnet.It's raining.
Es schneit.It's snowing.
Es ist kalt / warm / heiß.It's cold / warm / hot.
Es ist sonnig / windig / wolkig.It's sunny / windy / cloudy.
Es donnert und blitzt.It's thundering and lightning.

For "there is" weather, use es gibt + accusative: Es gibt ein Gewitter (there's a thunderstorm), Es gibt einen Sturm (there's a storm). To ask about the weather: Wie ist das Wetter?

The seasons and months

The four seasons are all masculine (der), and you use im (= in dem) to say "in" a season or month.

SeasonMonthsExample
der Frühling (spring)März, April, MaiIm Frühling wird es wärmer.
der Sommer (summer)Juni, Juli, AugustIm Sommer ist es heiß.
der Herbst (autumn)September, Oktober, NovemberIm Herbst regnet es viel.
der Winter (winter)Dezember, Januar, FebruarIm Winter schneit es.

What you wear: tragen and anziehen

Use tragen (to wear) for the clothes you have on. It's a stem-changing verb: the a becomes ä in the du- and er/sie/es-forms.

For the action of putting clothes on, use anziehen. This is a separable verb: the prefix an- jumps to the end of the sentence. Ich ziehe meinen Mantel an. (I put on my coat.) You'll study separable verbs fully in lesson 21 — for now just remember that an- goes to the end.

  • Ich trage einen Pullover. — I'm wearing a sweater. (state)
  • Ich ziehe einen Pullover an. — I put on a sweater. (action)
  • Imperative: Zieh die Jacke an! — Put your jacket on!

Pronunciation tip

Watch the vowels in the seasons: Frühling has the rounded ü ("ee" with rounded lips), and Herbst packs four consonants together — say "hairpst" in one breath. In Schnee the sch is a full "sh" and the ee is a long "ay". The w in Wind, Wolke and Wetter is an English v: "VET-ter".

Reading: Die vier Jahreszeiten

In Deutschland gibt es vier Jahreszeiten. Im Frühling wird es wärmer und die Blumen blühen. Im Sommer ist es oft heiß und sonnig, und viele Leute fahren in den Urlaub. Im Herbst regnet es viel, der Wind weht und die Blätter fallen. Im Winter ist es kalt und manchmal schneit es. Dann tragen die Menschen einen warmen Mantel, einen Schal und feste Schuhe.

Translation

In Germany there are four seasons. In spring it gets warmer and the flowers bloom. In summer it is often hot and sunny, and many people go on holiday. In autumn it rains a lot, the wind blows and the leaves fall. In winter it is cold and sometimes it snows. Then people wear a warm coat, a scarf and sturdy shoes.

Culture note

Weather is the great German small-talk topic, much as in Britain — and the changeable Aprilwetter ("April weather", sun and showers in minutes) is proverbial enough to have its own saying: April, April, der macht, was er will ("April does what it wants"). Germans take their seasons seriously: the first warm spring day brings everyone outdoors, and a sunny summer evening fills the Biergärten and lake shores.

Practical clothing culture is real, too. There's a popular adage — Es gibt kein schlechtes Wetter, nur falsche Kleidung ("There's no bad weather, only the wrong clothes") — and children are sent to Kindergarten with rain trousers and sturdy shoes in any season. In winter, a warm Mantel, Schal and Mütze (hat) are simply expected.

Talk about it

  • Wie ist das Wetter heute bei dir? — What's the weather like where you are today?
  • Welche Jahreszeit magst du am liebsten und warum? — Which season do you like best and why?
  • Was ziehst du bei Regen an? — What do you put on when it rains?
  • Was machst du gern, wenn es schneit? — What do you like to do when it snows?
  • Trägst du lieber eine Jacke oder einen Mantel im Winter? — Do you prefer to wear a jacket or a coat in winter?
