Grammar

du or Sie? Reading the relationship

The choice of pronoun is the single biggest register signal in German. Getting it wrong is socially noticeable.

  • Sie (capital S always): strangers, officials, customers, older people, the workplace by default. Verb in the 3rd person plural form.
  • du: friends, family, children, students among themselves, and increasingly in start-ups and online communities.
  • The switch from Sie to du is a small ritual — the senior or older person offers it: „Wollen wir uns nicht duzen?“ Until then, stay with Sie.

Spoken vs written: the same idea, two registers

Beyond the pronoun, whole vocabulary and structures shift. Formal German favours full forms, the nominal style and subjunctive politeness; casual German contracts, drops endings and uses particles.

Softening and hedging

Politeness in German leans heavily on Konjunktiv II and hedging words. Compare the blunt and the softened forms:

  • Direct: Geben Sie mir das. → Softer: Könnten Sie mir das vielleicht geben?
  • Direct: Ich will einen Termin. → Softer: Ich hätte gern einen Termin.
  • Hedges: vielleicht, eventuell, eigentlich, ich glaube, wenn es Ihnen recht ist.

Markers of casual speech

Real spoken German is full of contractions and particles that you'd never write in a formal text: gibt's (gibt es), 'ne / 'nen (eine/einen), hab / hast with dropped -e, and flavour words like halt, eben, mal, ja, doch (Das ist halt so. / Komm mal her.).

Pronunciation tip

Casual German swallows sounds. Haben wir becomes "hamwa", ich habe es becomes "ich hab's", auf einmal becomes "auf'mal". You don't need to produce these, but you must hear them — and you should keep your own formal speech crisp, pronouncing the full -en endings (könnten, hätten) so you sound polished rather than offhand.

Reading: Eine Nachricht, zwei Register

Stellen wir uns vor, jemand möchte ein Treffen verschieben. An einen guten Freund schreibt er per Messenger: „Hey, schaffe es morgen doch nicht, sorry! Können wir's auf nächste Woche schieben? Meld dich mal.“ Das ist locker, kurz und voller Abkürzungen.

An einen Geschäftspartner sieht dieselbe Nachricht völlig anders aus: „Sehr geehrter Herr Bauer, leider muss ich unseren morgigen Termin aus dringenden Gründen absagen. Wäre es möglich, das Treffen auf die kommende Woche zu verschieben? Für Ihr Verständnis danke ich Ihnen im Voraus. Mit freundlichen Grüßen, …“

Beide Texte teilen dieselbe Information mit, doch der Ton ist grundverschieden. Im Deutschen entscheidet die Beziehung zwischen den Personen über Anrede, Wortwahl und Höflichkeitsgrad. Wer das Register verwechselt, wirkt entweder distanziert und steif oder unhöflich und zu vertraulich.

Translation

Let's imagine someone wants to postpone a meeting. To a good friend he writes by messenger: "Hey, I'm not going to make it tomorrow after all, sorry! Can we push it to next week? Get in touch." That is relaxed, short and full of abbreviations.

To a business partner the same message looks completely different: "Dear Mr Bauer, unfortunately I have to cancel our meeting tomorrow for urgent reasons. Would it be possible to postpone the meeting to the coming week? I thank you in advance for your understanding. Kind regards, …"

Both texts convey the same information, yet the tone is fundamentally different. In German the relationship between the people decides the form of address, the choice of words and the degree of politeness. Whoever confuses the register comes across either as distant and stiff or as rude and too familiar.

Culture note

The du/Sie distinction is one of the most socially loaded features of German. Offering the du can be a small ceremony — sometimes sealed with a handshake (per du sein) — and being addressed with du by a stranger can feel either friendly or insulting depending on context. The retail chain IKEA famously uses du with all customers in Germany as a deliberate cultural statement; many traditional companies still insist on Sie even between long-time colleagues.

Written correspondence is just as rule-bound. The opening Sehr geehrte… is followed by a comma, and — a classic trap — the next line begins with a lower-case letter. Closings range from the neutral Mit freundlichen Grüßen down to the warm Liebe Grüße (often shortened to LG in texts and emails between friends). Matching the closing to the relationship is part of sounding genuinely native.

Talk about it

  • In welchen Situationen fällt es Ihnen schwer, zwischen du und Sie zu entscheiden? — In which situations do you find it hard to decide between du and Sie?
  • Wird in Ihrer Kultur ähnlich stark zwischen formell und informell unterschieden? — Does your culture distinguish as strongly between formal and informal?
  • Finden Sie formelle Grußformeln nützlich oder eher altmodisch? — Do you find formal closing formulas useful or rather old-fashioned?
  • Wie reagieren Sie, wenn Sie jemand zu früh duzt? — How do you react if someone uses du with you too soon?
  • Welche umgangssprachlichen Wörter haben Sie zuletzt gelernt? — Which colloquial words have you learned most recently?
