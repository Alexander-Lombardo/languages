Relative Clauses

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Form relative clauses with der, die, das and plural die to add information about a noun.
  • Choose the relative pronoun by the GENDER of the antecedent and the CASE of its role inside the clause.
  • Send the verb of the relative clause to the very end.
  • Combine a preposition with a relative pronoun (der Mann, mit dem ich spreche).
  • Use wo and was as relatives for places and for whole-clause references.

Dialogue

Lena

Kennst du den Mann, der gerade aus dem Café gekommen ist?

Do you know the man who just came out of the café?

Tom

Das ist der Nachbar, den ich dir letzte Woche gezeigt habe.

That's the neighbour whom I showed you last week.

Lena

Ach ja, der Mann, mit dem du im Sportverein bist?

Oh right, the man with whom you're in the sports club?

Tom

Genau. Und die Frau, die neben ihm saß, ist seine Kollegin.

Exactly. And the woman who was sitting next to him is his colleague.

Lena

Ist das die Kollegin, deren Wohnung du renoviert hast?

Is that the colleague whose flat you renovated?

Tom

Ja, die Wohnung, die ich renoviert habe, gehört ihr.

Yes, the flat that I renovated belongs to her.

Lena

Und das Café, in dem sie waren, kann ich das empfehlen?

And the café they were in — can I recommend it?

Tom

Klar. Das ist das Café, das die besten Kuchen der Stadt hat.

Sure. That's the café that has the best cakes in town.

Lena

Ist das der Ort, wo wir uns kennengelernt haben?

Is that the place where we first met?

Tom

Stimmt! Etwas, woran ich mich gern erinnere.

That's right! Something that I like to remember.

Grammar

What a relative clause does

A relative clause adds information about a noun (the antecedent) without starting a new sentence. In English we use who, which, that, whose. In German we mostly use the relative pronouns der, die, das (and plural die) — they look like the definite articles but they are not articles here; they point back to the noun.

Two things decide which form you use: the gender (and number) come from the noun outside the clause; the case comes from the job the pronoun does inside the relative clause.

And the most important rule: a relative clause is a subordinate clause, so the conjugated verb moves to the very end, and the clause is set off by commas: Der Mann, der dort steht, ist mein Bruder.

Examples for one masculine noun, der Mann, with the case changing by the pronoun's role:

  • Nom (subject): der Mann, der dort wohnt — the man who lives there
  • Acc (object): der Mann, den ich kenne — the man whom I know
  • Dat (indirect object): der Mann, dem ich helfe — the man whom I help
  • Gen (possession): der Mann, dessen Auto kaputt ist — the man whose car is broken

Preposition + relative pronoun

If the verb or idea needs a preposition, that preposition comes before the relative pronoun, and the preposition decides the case (just as it always does). It never moves to the end the way English allows.

  • der Mann, mit dem ich spreche — the man (with) whom I speak (mit → dative)
  • die Stadt, in der ich wohne — the city in which I live (in → dative)
  • das Thema, über das wir reden — the topic about which we talk (über → accusative)

wo and was as relatives

For places, German often uses wo instead of in dem / in der: das Café, wo wir uns trafen. After indefinite words like etwas, alles, nichts, das and after a whole clause, use was (or wo(r)- + preposition): alles, was ich habe; Er kam zu spät, was mich ärgerte; etwas, woran ich denke.

Pronunciation tip

Don't drop the comma — it is what marks the relative clause in writing, and in speech a clear little pause does the same job. Listen for the pronoun and the verb at the end: …, mit dem ich spreche ends on the verb. The genitive forms dessen and deren are stressed on the first syllable: "DES-sen", "DAY-ren".

Reading: Die Wohnung, die alles veränderte

Vor zwei Jahren fand ich eine Anzeige, die mein Leben veränderte. Es war eine kleine Wohnung, die im Zentrum lag und die ich mir kaum leisten konnte. Der Vermieter, dessen Familie das Haus seit Generationen besitzt, war ein freundlicher Mann, dem man sofort vertraute.

Die Nachbarn, die ich am ersten Tag traf, luden mich zum Kaffee ein. Besonders die Frau, deren Wohnung neben meiner liegt, wurde eine gute Freundin. Sie empfahl mir ein Café, in dem sich viele Leute treffen, die neu in der Stadt sind. Dort lernte ich Menschen kennen, mit denen ich heute noch befreundet bin.

Die Wohnung, die ich damals gemietet habe, ist heute mein Zuhause — ein Ort, wo ich mich wirklich wohlfühle.

Translation

Two years ago I found an advert that changed my life. It was a small flat that was in the centre and that I could barely afford. The landlord, whose family has owned the building for generations, was a friendly man whom you trusted immediately.

The neighbours whom I met on the first day invited me for coffee. Especially the woman whose flat is next to mine became a good friend. She recommended a café to me in which many people meet who are new to the city. There I got to know people with whom I am still friends today.

The flat that I rented back then is my home today — a place where I really feel at ease.

Culture note

Germans build long, information-rich sentences and the relative clause is one of their favourite tools — newspaper sentences in particular can stack several of them. For the learner the reward is huge: once you can drop in a der/die/das clause, your speech instantly sounds more adult and connected, instead of a string of short sentences.

A practical cultural note: when Germans recommend a flat, a doctor or a tradesperson, they almost always describe them with a relative clause — Ich kenne einen Handwerker, der wirklich zuverlässig ist. Reliability (Zuverlässigkeit) is a high compliment, and the relative clause is how that recommendation is naturally delivered.

Talk about it

  • Beschreibe einen Menschen, den du sehr bewunderst, in einem Satz mit Relativsatz. — Describe a person whom you greatly admire, in one sentence with a relative clause.
  • Wie heißt die Stadt, in der du wohnst, und was magst du an ihr? — What is the name of the city in which you live, and what do you like about it?
  • Gibt es einen Ort, wo du dich besonders wohlfühlst? — Is there a place where you feel especially at ease?
  • Erzähle von einem Gegenstand, den du nie verlieren möchtest. — Tell us about an object that you never want to lose.
  • Wer ist ein Freund, dessen Meinung dir wichtig ist? — Who is a friend whose opinion matters to you?
