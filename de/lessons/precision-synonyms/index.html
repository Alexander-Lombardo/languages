Precision: Synonyms & Word Choice

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Replace overused all-purpose verbs (machen, sagen, gehen) with precise alternatives.
  • Upgrade vague adjectives (gut, schön, schlecht) to context-specific ones.
  • Use established German collocations (eine Entscheidung treffen, Maßnahmen ergreifen) correctly.
  • Recognise that the 'right' word depends on context, register and collocation, not just meaning.
  • Avoid Vagheit (vagueness) and Wiederholung (repetition) by drawing on synonym families.
  • Self-edit a draft for weak word choice and tighten it.

Dialogue

Frau Berg

Ihr Bericht ist solide, aber stilistisch etwas eintönig. Sie schreiben fünfmal „machen“.

Your report is solid, but stylistically a bit monotonous. You write 'machen' five times.

Markus

Stimmt. Womit könnte ich es ersetzen?

True. What could I replace it with?

Frau Berg

Je nach Kontext: eine Entscheidung trifft man, einen Fehler begeht man, einen Termin vereinbart man.

Depending on the context: you make a decision, you commit an error, you arrange an appointment.

Markus

Und statt „Wir machen Maßnahmen“?

And instead of 'We make measures'?

Frau Berg

Da heißt es zwingend „Maßnahmen ergreifen“ oder „umsetzen“. Das ist eine feste Kollokation.

There it must be 'take measures' or 'implement' them. That's a fixed collocation.

Markus

Verstanden. Die Bedeutung ist ähnlich, aber das richtige Wort hängt vom Partnerwort ab.

Understood. The meaning is similar, but the right word depends on its partner word.

Frau Berg

Genau. Präzision entsteht nicht durch schwere Wörter, sondern durch das treffende.

Exactly. Precision doesn't come from heavy words, but from the apt one.

Grammar

Retire the all-purpose verbs

A few verbs do far too much work. Replacing them with a specific verb is the single biggest upgrade to advanced German. The vague verb usually still works — it's just colourless.

Retire the all-purpose adjectives

The same applies to gut, schön and schlecht. The exact replacement depends on what you're describing:

Caution: synonyms are rarely interchangeable in every slot. Ein fundiertes Argument is well-founded; ein gelungenes Argument is well-executed — close, but not identical. Choose by sense, not at random.

Collocations: words have fixed partners

A Kollokation is a habitual word partnership. The meaning may be transparent, but only one verb is idiomatic. Learning these as whole chunks is non-negotiable at C1:

Self-editing for precision

Treat your first draft as raw material. On the second pass, hunt for three things: (1) repeated verbs and nouns, (2) the vague big-five (machen, sagen, gehen, gut, Sache), and (3) wrong collocations. Replacing even a handful sharpens the whole text. Beware the opposite trap, though — stacking rare words to sound clever (geschwollen, bombastic) reads worse than honest plainness. Aim for treffend, not kompliziert.

Pronunciation tip

Many of these precise verbs are formed with inseparable prefixes (be-, er-, ver-, über-, unter-), which are always unstressed: say be-GEhen, er-GREI-fen, ü-ber-NEH-men with the stress on the root, never on the prefix. Misplacing the stress is a subtle but instant tell. Contrast this with separable prefixes (UM-setzen, AUF-brechen), where the prefix is stressed — which is also why one separates and the other does not.

Reading: Das treffende Wort

Es gibt im Deutschen eine Handvoll Wörter, die fast überall passen — und genau das ist ihr Problem. „Machen“, „sagen“, „gehen“, „gut“, „Sache“: Wer sie zu oft verwendet, wirkt nicht falsch, aber unscharf. Der Text liest sich grau, weil ihm die Konturen fehlen.

Präzision bedeutet nicht, möglichst seltene oder gelehrte Wörter zu wählen. Sie bedeutet, genau das Wort zu finden, das der Sache entspricht. „Eine Entscheidung treffen“ klingt selbstverständlich, doch wer „eine Entscheidung machen“ schreibt, verrät sofort, dass Deutsch nicht seine Muttersprache ist — nicht weil es unverständlich wäre, sondern weil die Kollokation nicht stimmt. Wörter haben feste Partner, und diese Partnerschaften muss man lernen wie Vokabeln selbst.

Wer präzise schreiben will, prüft seinen Entwurf ein zweites Mal: Welche Verben wiederholen sich? Welches „gut“ ließe sich durch „überzeugend“, „gelungen“ oder „fundiert“ ersetzen? Oft reicht ein einziger Durchgang, um aus einem grauen Text einen klaren zu machen.

Translation

There is a handful of words in German that fit almost everywhere — and that is precisely their problem. "Machen," "sagen," "gehen," "gut," "Sache": anyone who uses them too often doesn't seem wrong, but blurry. The text reads grey because it lacks contours.

Precision doesn't mean choosing the rarest or most learned words possible. It means finding exactly the word that matches the thing. "To make a decision" (treffen) sounds obvious, yet anyone who writes "eine Entscheidung machen" instantly gives away that German isn't their native language — not because it would be incomprehensible, but because the collocation is wrong. Words have fixed partners, and these partnerships have to be learned like vocabulary itself.

Anyone who wants to write precisely checks their draft a second time: Which verbs repeat? Which "good" could be replaced by "convincing," "successful" or "well-founded"? Often a single pass is enough to turn a grey text into a clear one.

Culture note

German has an unusually strong culture of lexical precision, reflected in its reference works. The Duden is the authority on spelling and usage, but serious writers also reach for the Duden — Das Stilwörterbuch and dedicated collocation dictionaries (Wörterbuch der Kollokationen), because Germans treat the right partner-word as a matter of correctness, not mere taste. Online, the corpus tool DWDS lets you check which words genuinely co-occur in real texts.

This precision shows up in everyday register too. German distinguishes finely between near-synonyms English lumps together — verbessern vs. korrigieren, erledigen vs. bewältigen, Fehler vs. Irrtum vs. Versehen. For learners, the practical lesson is that vocabulary at C1 is learned in chunks: not the word alone, but the word plus the company it keeps.

  • Wie lernt man Kollokationen am besten — einzeln oder im Kontext? — What's the best way to learn collocations — individually or in context?
