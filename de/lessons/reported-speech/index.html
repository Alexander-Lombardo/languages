Reported Speech (Konjunktiv I)

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Recognise indirect speech (indirekte Rede) signalled by Konjunktiv I.
  • Form Konjunktiv I of sein, haben, können, gehen and regular verbs (er sei, habe, könne, gehe, komme).
  • Apply the substitution rule: when Konjunktiv I looks like the indicative, switch to Konjunktiv II.
  • Handle tense shifts so that past statements are reported correctly.
  • Read and write news-style reports that keep a neutral, distancing tone.

Dialogue

Lena

Hast du gehört, was der Minister gesagt hat?

Did you hear what the minister said?

Tom

Nur kurz. Er habe neue Maßnahmen angekündigt, oder?

Only briefly. He's said to have announced new measures, right?

Lena

Genau. Er sagte, die Lage sei ernst, aber unter Kontrolle.

Exactly. He said the situation was serious but under control.

Tom

Und die Opposition? Was behauptet sie?

And the opposition? What do they claim?

Lena

Sie behauptet, die Regierung habe zu spät reagiert.

They claim the government reacted too late.

Tom

Der Sprecher meinte, das stimme nicht. Alles sei rechtzeitig geschehen.

The spokesperson said that wasn't true. Everything happened in time, he said.

Lena

Angeblich gebe es nächste Woche eine weitere Pressekonferenz.

Allegedly there's going to be another press conference next week.

Tom

Mal sehen, ob die Zahlen wirklich besser würden.

Let's see whether the figures really do get better.

Grammar

What is indirect speech?

When you report what someone else said without quoting them directly, formal German marks this with the Konjunktiv I. It signals: these are not my words — I'm just relaying them. Compare direct speech Er sagt: „Ich bin krank“ with indirect Er sagt, er sei krank. The Konjunktiv I keeps the reporter at a neutral distance — which is exactly why journalists love it.

Forming Konjunktiv I

Take the infinitive stem and add the endings -e, -est, -e, -en, -et, -en. The most important form by far is the er/sie/es form, which simply ends in -e: komme, gehe, habe, könne. The verb sein is irregular and the one you must know cold.

The substitution rule: switch to Konjunktiv II

Problem: many Konjunktiv I forms look identical to the indicative — e.g. sie haben (indicative) and sie haben (Konjunktiv I) are the same. When that happens, the form would be ambiguous, so German switches to Konjunktiv II instead: sie hätten, sie kämen, sie würden kommen. This is why news reports mix in würde-forms.

Tense shifts

Indirect speech has only three time frames, formed with the Konjunktiv I of the auxiliary:

OriginalIndirect
Present: „Ich bin müde.“Er sagt, er sei müde.
Past (all past tenses): „Ich war / bin gewesen müde.“Er sagt, er sei müde gewesen.
Future: „Ich werde kommen.“Er sagt, er werde kommen.

So all the past tenses collapse into one indirect past: sei/habe + Partizip II.

Pronunciation tip

The tell-tale signal of reported speech is that little -e ending: komme, gehe, habe, sei. Give it a clear, light schwa — don't swallow it, or the listener won't notice you've shifted into Konjunktiv I. In speech, Germans also often drop the conjunction dass and just keep the subjunctive verb to mark the indirect speech: Er sagt, er sei krank.

Reading: Bericht: Streit um das neue Gesetz

Bei der gestrigen Pressekonferenz hat die Regierung das neue Klimagesetz verteidigt. Eine Sprecherin erklärte, das Gesetz sei ein wichtiger Schritt und werde die Emissionen deutlich senken. Man habe alle Bürger im Blick, betonte sie; niemand werde benachteiligt.

Die Opposition sieht das anders. Ihr Vorsitzender behauptete, die Pläne seien unrealistisch und kämen viel zu spät. Die Kosten für normale Familien würden steigen, sagte er, und die Regierung habe keine klaren Antworten.

Laut mehreren Experten ist die Wahrheit komplizierter. Eine Forscherin teilte mit, das Gesetz könne durchaus wirken, aber nur, wenn es konsequent umgesetzt werde. Sie fügte hinzu, man dürfe die Erwartungen nicht zu hoch schrauben. Ob das Gesetz wie geplant in Kraft trete, werde sich erst in den nächsten Monaten zeigen.

Translation

At yesterday's press conference, the government defended the new climate law. A spokeswoman declared that the law was an important step and would significantly reduce emissions. They had all citizens in mind, she stressed; no one would be disadvantaged.

The opposition sees things differently. Its leader claimed that the plans were unrealistic and came far too late. The costs for ordinary families would rise, he said, and the government had no clear answers.

According to several experts, the truth is more complicated. A researcher reported that the law could indeed work, but only if it were implemented consistently. She added that expectations should not be raised too high. Whether the law comes into force as planned will only become clear in the coming months.

Culture note

The Konjunktiv I is the grammatical backbone of German journalism. Open any issue of a serious paper like the Süddeutsche Zeitung or Die Zeit and you'll find sentences built around er sei, sie habe, die Regierung werde. The mood is a professional courtesy: it lets a reporter relay a claim accurately while signalling "I am quoting, not endorsing." Switching even briefly into the indicative can imply the journalist personally vouches for the statement — which is why editors watch the mood closely.

In casual conversation, by contrast, most Germans simply use the indicative (Er hat gesagt, dass er krank ist) and reserve Konjunktiv I for written reports, exams and formal speech. Mastering it is therefore less about everyday chat and more about reading the news critically and writing in a credible, neutral register — a core B2 skill.

Talk about it

  • Warum benutzen Journalisten so gern den Konjunktiv I? Welchen Effekt hat das auf den Leser? — Why do journalists like the Konjunktiv I so much? What effect does it have on the reader?
  • Berichte über eine Nachricht, die du diese Woche gehört hast – in indirekter Rede. — Report on a piece of news you heard this week – in indirect speech.
  • Glaubst du, dass Medien immer neutral berichten? Begründe deine Meinung. — Do you think the media always report neutrally? Justify your opinion.
  • Wie würde sich der Ton ändern, wenn man im Text statt Konjunktiv I den Indikativ benutzte? — How would the tone change if the indicative were used in the text instead of Konjunktiv I?
