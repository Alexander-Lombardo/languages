Weather & Seasons

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Ask and answer “Che tempo fa?” to describe the weather.
  • Use the three weather patterns: fa + adjective, c’è + noun, and weather verbs.
  • Name the four seasons and the twelve months and say what the weather is like in them.
  • Make simple plans that depend on the weather (“If it’s sunny, we’ll go…”).

Dialogue

Giulia

Pronto, Marco! Che tempo fa a Torino oggi?

Hello, Marco! What’s the weather like in Turin today?

Marco

Ciao Giulia! Fa freddo e c’è molta nebbia. E da te?

Hi Giulia! It’s cold and there’s a lot of fog. And where you are?

Giulia

Qui a Napoli fa bello, c’è il sole. È una bella giornata.

Here in Naples the weather is nice, it’s sunny. It’s a lovely day.

Marco

Beata te! Da noi piove da tre giorni.

Lucky you! Here it’s been raining for three days.

Giulia

Senti, domenica viene il sole anche al Nord?

Listen, is the sun coming up north on Sunday too?

Marco

Sì, dicono che domenica farà bello e non pioverà.

Yes, they say Sunday will be nice and it won’t rain.

Giulia

Perfetto. Se fa bello, andiamo in montagna insieme?

Perfect. If the weather’s nice, shall we go to the mountains together?

Marco

Volentieri! In primavera la montagna è bellissima.

Gladly! In spring the mountains are beautiful.

Giulia

Però porta una giacca: di sera fa ancora freddo.

But bring a jacket: in the evening it’s still cold.

Marco

Certo. E se nevica ancora, andiamo a sciare!

Of course. And if it snows again, we’ll go skiing!

Giulia

Affare fatto. Ci sentiamo sabato sera, allora.

It’s a deal. We’ll talk Saturday evening, then.

Grammar

Three ways to talk about the weather

Italian describes the weather in three patterns, and you must pick the right one for each word. The most common question is “Che tempo fa?” (literally “What weather does it make?”). Notice the verb fare (to make/do), not essere.

PatternUse it forExample
fa + adjectivegeneral statesfa caldo, fa freddo, fa bello, fa brutto, fa fresco
c’è / ci sono + nounvisible thingsc’è il sole, c’è vento, c’è la nebbia, ci sono le nuvole
weather verb (3rd sg.)rain/snow etc.piove (it rains), nevica (it snows), tira vento (it’s windy)

So you say fa caldo (NOT “è caldo” for the weather) and c’è il sole (NOT “fa sole” in standard Italian). The verbs piove and nevica are impersonal: they only exist in the “it” form and take no subject.

Seasons and months

The four seasons take the preposition in (in primavera, in estate, in autunno, in inverno). Months take a / ad (a marzo, ad aprile). Seasons and months are not capitalised in Italian.

StagioneMesi
la primavera (spring)marzo, aprile, maggio
l’estate (summer)giugno, luglio, agosto
l’autunno (autumn)settembre, ottobre, novembre
l’inverno (winter)dicembre, gennaio, febbraio

Weather plans with the future

To make plans that depend on the weather, Italians often use a simple “se + presente” clause plus a present or future verb. The simple future of fare is farà, and of piovere it’s pioverà.

  • Se fa bello, andiamo al mare. — If it’s nice, we’ll go to the sea. (present for a near, sure plan)
  • Domani farà caldo. — Tomorrow it will be hot.
  • Domenica non pioverà. — On Sunday it won’t rain.

Pronunciation tip

The soft and hard c: in caldo and freddo the c is hard (a k sound), while in fa fresco the sc before o stays hard too (FREH-skoh). Compare nevica (NEH-vee-kah, hard) — the stress falls on the first syllable, not the middle, so resist saying “neh-VEE-kah.”

The gg/ggi sound: pioggia is PYOHD-jah — the double gg before i gives a long, soft “dj” sound, and the i is silent, just softening the g. Likewise giugno (JOO-nyoh) and maggio (MAHD-joh).

Reading: Le quattro stagioni in Italia

In Italia ci sono quattro stagioni. In primavera, da marzo a maggio, fa bello e spesso piove un po’. In estate, da giugno ad agosto, fa molto caldo e c’è il sole. In autunno fa fresco, tira vento e cadono le foglie. In inverno, da dicembre a febbraio, fa freddo e in montagna nevica.

Translation

In Italy there are four seasons. In spring, from March to May, the weather is nice and it often rains a little. In summer, from June to August, it’s very hot and sunny. In autumn it’s cool, it’s windy and the leaves fall. In winter, from December to February, it’s cold and in the mountains it snows.

Culture note

Italy’s weather changes a lot from north to south, and the conversation does too: a Milanese may complain about la nebbia in the Po Valley while a Sicilian enjoys il sole in the same week. In August the heat (il caldo) becomes a national topic, and many shops close for Ferragosto (15 August) as cities empty toward the coast and mountains. Italians also love a vivid phrase for sudden bad weather: “piove a catinelle” — literally “it’s raining basinfuls,” the equivalent of “it’s pouring.”

Talk about it

  • Che tempo fa oggi dove vivi? _(What’s the weather like today where you live?)_
  • Qual è la tua stagione preferita e perché? _(What’s your favourite season and why?)_
  • Cosa fai quando piove tutto il giorno? _(What do you do when it rains all day?)_
  • Se domani fa bello, cosa farai? _(If the weather is nice tomorrow, what will you do?)_
