Passato Prossimo vs Imperfetto

Dialogue

Giulia

Ciao Marco! Cosa hai fatto ieri sera?

Hi Marco! What did you do yesterday evening?

Marco

Niente di speciale. Ero a casa e leggevo un libro.

Nothing special. I was at home and I was reading a book.

Giulia

Tutto qui? Sembrava una serata tranquilla.

That’s all? It sounded like a quiet evening.

Marco

All’inizio sì. Ma mentre leggevo, è suonato il telefono.

At first yes. But while I was reading, the phone rang.

Giulia

E chi era?

And who was it?

Marco

Era mio fratello. Mi ha detto che arrivava in città.

It was my brother. He told me he was coming to town.

Giulia

Che bello! E allora cosa hai fatto?

How nice! So what did you do?

Marco

Stavo per andare a letto, ma sono uscito e l’ho aspettato alla stazione.

I was about to go to bed, but I went out and waited for him at the station.

Giulia

Da bambini uscivate sempre insieme, vero?

As kids you always used to go out together, right?

Marco

Sì, ogni estate andavamo al mare. Ieri abbiamo parlato fino a tardi.

Yes, every summer we used to go to the seaside. Yesterday we talked until late.

Grammar

Two pasts, two jobs

Italian has two main past tenses, and choosing between them is about the kind of action, not just when it happened. The passato prossimo tells you what happened — a completed, finished event. The imperfetto sets the scene: what things were like, what used to happen, what was going on in the background.

Use the imperfetto for…Use the passato prossimo for…
background, description (era buio, faceva freddo)a single completed action (ho aperto la porta)
habits, repetition (ogni giorno andavo a scuola)a sequence of events (sono entrato, ho visto, sono uscito)
age, time, feelings (avevo dieci anni, ero stanco)something that happened a set number of times (l’ho visto una volta)
an ongoing action interrupted by anotherthe action that interrupts it

Signal words often point you to one tense. Imperfetto loves di solito, sempre, ogni giorno, spesso, da bambino, mentre. Passato prossimo loves ieri, all’improvviso, una volta, poi, a un certo punto.

Mentre + imperfetto

Mentre (while) introduces an action in progress, so it is almost always followed by the imperfetto. A shorter, completed action that cuts in goes in the passato prossimo.

  • Mentre guardavo la tv, è suonato il telefono. — While I was watching tv, the phone rang.
  • Mentre mangiavamo, è arrivata Maria. — While we were eating, Maria arrived.
  • If both actions are ongoing in parallel, use the imperfetto twice: Mentre io leggevo, lui dormiva.

Stavo per + infinitive

To say you were about to / were just going to do something (but maybe didn’t), use the imperfetto of stare + per + the infinitive.

Stavo per andare a letto, ma è arrivato mio fratello. — I was about to go to bed, but my brother arrived.

Combining the two in a story

A typical narration weaves the tenses: the imperfetto paints the setting, the passato prossimo moves the plot forward. Era una bella giornata e faceva caldo (scene, imperfetto). Sono uscito, ho incontrato un amico e abbiamo preso un gelato (events, passato prossimo).

Pronunciation tip

Listen for the difference between -avo / -evo / -ivo (imperfetto, ongoing) and the ho / sono + participle shape of the passato prossimo. In leggevo the double gg before e sounds like a soft "j" ("led-JEH-voh"), while in ho letto the tt is a sharp, doubled "t". Stress the stem clearly: guardàvo, mangiàvi.

Reading: Una mattina diversa

Di solito Anna si svegliava alle sette e prendeva il caffè con calma. Quella mattina però dormiva ancora quando, all’improvviso, è suonata la sveglia tardi. Si è alzata di corsa, si è vestita ed è uscita senza colazione. Mentre correva alla fermata, ha visto che l’autobus arrivava: per fortuna l’ha preso!

Translation

Anna usually woke up at seven and had her coffee calmly. That morning, though, she was still sleeping when, all of a sudden, the alarm went off late. She got up in a hurry, got dressed and left without breakfast. While she was running to the stop, she saw the bus coming: luckily she caught it!

Culture note

Italians lean heavily on the imperfetto when sharing childhood memories — phrases like «Quando ero piccolo, d’estate andavamo sempre dai nonni» are the classic opening of a nostalgic story. In the spoken language of central and northern Italy the passato prossimo covers nearly all completed past actions, even fairly distant ones, while in much of the South and in literary writing you will also hear the passato remoto (e.g. andai, vidi) for events felt as long ago and finished.

Talk about it

  • Cosa facevi di solito da bambino il fine settimana? _(What did you usually do as a child on the weekend?)_
  • Racconta una cosa interessante che ti è successa ieri. _(Tell about one interesting thing that happened to you yesterday.)_
  • Cosa stavi per fare quando ti ho chiamato? _(What were you about to do when I called you?)_
  • Descrivi com’era il tempo e come ti sentivi questa mattina. _(Describe what the weather was like and how you felt this morning.)_
