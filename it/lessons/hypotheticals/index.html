Grammar

The three types of periodo ipotetico

An Italian conditional sentence has two parts: the protasi (the se-clause, the condition) and the apodosi (the main clause, the consequence). The verb forms you choose signal how likely you consider the hypothesis. There are three classic types.

TypeProtasi (se…)ApodosiExample
1. Reale (real / certain)se + indicativoindicativo (or imperativo)Se studi, impari.
2. Possibile (possible / unlikely)se + congiuntivo imperfettocondizionale presenteSe studiassi, imparesti.
3. Irreale (impossible / past)se + congiuntivo trapassatocondizionale passatoSe avessi studiato, avresti imparato.

Golden rule: never put a condizionale after se. The condition takes the indicative or the subjunctive; the consequence takes the conditional. Se avrei studiato is a classic mistake — say Se avessi studiato.

Building the irreale (the hardest one)

The irreale describes a past condition that did not happen and its (now impossible) outcome. You need two compound tenses: the congiuntivo trapassato (avessi/fossi + participle) in the se-clause and the condizionale passato (avrei/sarei + participle) in the main clause.

PersonCongiuntivo trapassato (parlare)Condizionale passato (parlare)
ioavessi parlatoavrei parlato
tuavessi parlatoavresti parlato
lui/leiavesse parlatoavrebbe parlato
noiavessimo parlatoavremmo parlato
voiaveste parlatoavreste parlato
loroavessero parlatoavrebbero parlato

With motion/reflexive verbs the auxiliary is essere: Se fossi partito prima, sarei arrivato in tempo. A common stylistic variant in speech mixes the past: Se avessi studiato, passavi l’esame (imperfetto indicativo for the consequence) — accepted but less formal than avresti passato.

magari + congiuntivo — wishes and regrets

Beyond meaning ‘maybe’, magari introduces an intense wish. With the congiuntivo imperfetto it expresses a desire for the present/future; with the congiuntivo trapassato a regret about the past.

  • Magari vincessi alla lotteria! — If only I won the lottery! (present wish)
  • Magari fossi rimasto a casa! — If only I had stayed home! (past regret)
  • As a one-word answer, magari! means ‘I wish!’ / ‘if only!’

come se + congiuntivo imperfetto/trapassato

Come se (‘as if’) always introduces an unreal comparison, so it always takes the subjunctive — never the indicative, even though the main clause may be in the present. Use the imperfetto for a simultaneous unreal action and the trapassato for a prior one.

  • Mi parla come se mi conoscesse da anni. — He talks to me as if he had known me for years.
  • Tremava come se avesse visto un fantasma. — She was trembling as if she had seen a ghost.

Other refined connectors that govern the subjunctive: qualora (= se, formal), purché / a patto che (provided that), and a meno che (non) (unless). E.g. Verrò, a meno che non piova.

Pronunciation tip

Don’t let the doubled forms of the congiuntivo trapassato collapse in speech: avessi, avessimo, avessero all carry a clearly geminated (long) -ss-. Lengthen it audibly — a-VES-si — so it isn’t confused with the passato remoto avesti. Likewise pronounce magari with the stress on the middle syllable, ma-GA-ri, and let it ring with longing when it means ‘if only!’.

Reading: Se solo avessi saputo

«Se avessi saputo che la riunione iniziava alle otto, mi sarei svegliato prima», sospirò Marco entrando in ufficio. La collega lo guardò come se non lo credesse del tutto. «Magari l’avessi saputo anch’io», rispose. «Ho corso come una pazza per arrivare in tempo.»

In realtà, se Marco fosse arrivato puntuale, avrebbe potuto presentare lui stesso il progetto. Adesso, invece, il rimpianto lo accompagnava per tutta la mattinata. Continuava a ripetersi che, qualora avesse controllato il calendario la sera prima, nulla di tutto questo sarebbe successo.

«Senti», disse il capo, «se ti impegni davvero, recuperi in un attimo. Purché tu prepari le slide entro mezzogiorno, presenterai tu nel pomeriggio. A meno che tu non preferisca lasciarlo fare a un altro.» Marco si raddrizzò sulla sedia, all’improvviso pieno di energia, come se quelle parole gli avessero ridato fiducia.

«Se mi dà questa possibilità, non la deluderò», promise. E pensò che, in fondo, se ogni errore offrisse una seconda occasione come questa, sbagliare non sarebbe poi così grave.

Translation

“If I had known the meeting started at eight, I would have woken up earlier,” sighed Marco as he walked into the office. His colleague looked at him as if she didn’t quite believe him. “If only I had known too,” she replied. “I ran like a madwoman to get here on time.”

In fact, if Marco had arrived on time, he could have presented the project himself. Now, instead, regret followed him all morning. He kept telling himself that, had he checked the calendar the night before, none of this would have happened.

“Listen,” said the boss, “if you really apply yourself, you’ll catch up in no time. Provided you prepare the slides by noon, you’ll present this afternoon. Unless you’d rather let someone else do it.” Marco straightened up in his chair, suddenly full of energy, as if those words had given him back his confidence.

“If you give me this chance, I won’t let you down,” he promised. And he thought that, after all, if every mistake offered a second chance like this one, making mistakes wouldn’t be so bad.

Culture note

In everyday Italian, magari is wonderfully versatile and very common in spoken Tuscany and beyond. On its own it’s an enthusiastic ‘I wish!’ (— «Ti piacerebbe andare in Italia?» — «Magari!»), but in mid-sentence it softens to ‘maybe / perhaps’ (magari ci vediamo domani). Italians also famously prefer the ‘incorrect’ but everyday se avessi saputo, te lo dicevo (imperfetto in place of the conditional) in casual speech — yet in writing and exams the textbook form te l’avrei detto is expected. Mastering the full irreale is a real mark of advanced, elegant Italian.

Talk about it

  • Se potessi ricominciare gli studi da capo, cosa studieresti e perché? _(If you could start your studies over from scratch, what would you study and why?)_
  • C’è una decisione che, se avessi scelto diversamente, avrebbe cambiato la tua vita? _(Is there a decision that, had you chosen differently, would have changed your life?)_
  • Magari avessi imparato l’italiano da bambino: quali altre abilità rimpiangi di non aver coltivato prima? _(If only you had learned Italian as a child — what other skills do you regret not having cultivated earlier?)_
  • Qualora vincessi una grande somma di denaro, come cambieresti la tua routine quotidiana? _(Should you win a large sum of money, how would you change your daily routine?)_
  • Ti capita mai di comportarti come se non fossi stanco anche quando lo sei? Perché lo facciamo? _(Do you ever act as if you weren’t tired even when you are? Why do we do this?)_
