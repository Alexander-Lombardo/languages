Grammar

The Italian alphabet (21 letters)

The standard Italian alphabet has only 21 letters. Five letters — j, k, w, x, y — are not native and appear almost only in foreign or borrowed words (jeans, taxi, weekend). The letter h is always silent: it never has a sound of its own, but it changes the sound of c and g before e and i.

The 7 vowel sounds

Italian has five vowel letters but seven vowel sounds, because e and o can be open or closed. Vowels are pure and short — never glided like in English. They are always pronounced clearly, even at the end of a word.

Hard vs soft c and g

Before a, o, u the letters c and g are hard (like k and g in go). Before e and i they turn soft (like ch in chair and g in gem). The silent h keeps the sound hard, and an extra i keeps it soft.

The digraphs gn, gl and sc

Three combinations make special sounds:

  • gn = like ñ in Spanish / ny in canyon: gnocchi, lasagne, signore.
  • gl (before i) = like lli in million: famiglia, figlio, gli. (Before other vowels, as in globo, it is a plain g + l.)
  • sc before e/i = soft sh: pesce, sciare. Before a/o/u or with an h it is hard sk: scuola, schema.

Pronunciation tip

Italian double consonants are really doubled — hold them longer than a single one. Compare nono (ninth) with nonno (grandfather), or pala (shovel) with palla (ball). And remember: every written vowel is pronounced, so grazie has three clear syllables: GRAH-tsyeh.

Culture note

In Italy the greeting often depends on the time of day: buongiorno in the morning, buonasera from the late afternoon, and buonanotte only when parting for the night. Ciao is friendly and informal — with strangers, shopkeepers or elders, Italians prefer buongiorno or salve. And the letters j, k, w, x, y feel so foreign that the J in the football club Juventus is pronounced the Italian way, roughly ‘yoo-VEN-toos’.
