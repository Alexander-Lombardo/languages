Shopping & Clothing

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Name common items of clothing and the main colours.
  • Make colours and sizes agree with the noun they describe.
  • Handle a shop conversation: ask to try something on, ask for a size, and buy it.
  • Use questo/quello and molto/troppo to point things out and react to them.

Dialogue

Commessa

Buongiorno! Desidera?

Good morning! Can I help you?

Marco

Buongiorno. Cerco una camicia azzurra.

Good morning. I'm looking for a light-blue shirt.

Commessa

Che taglia porta?

What size do you take?

Marco

Porto la taglia media. Posso provarla?

I take a medium. Can I try it on?

Commessa

Certo, il camerino è là. Questa le piace?

Of course, the fitting room is over there. Do you like this one?

Marco

Mi piace, ma è un po’ troppo grande. Avete quella più piccola?

I like it, but it's a bit too big. Do you have that smaller one?

Commessa

Sì, ecco. Questa è più piccola e costa meno: è in saldo.

Yes, here. This one is smaller and costs less: it's on sale.

Marco

Perfetta! Quanto costa?

Perfect! How much does it cost?

Commessa

Venticinque euro. È molto elegante.

Twenty-five euros. It's very elegant.

Marco

Bene, la prendo. Posso pagare con la carta?

Good, I'll take it. Can I pay by card?

Commessa

Certo. Grazie e buona giornata!

Of course. Thank you and have a good day!

Grammar

Colours that agree

In Italian, colours are adjectives, so they usually come after the noun and must agree with it in gender and number. Most colours follow the normal -o / -a / -i / -e pattern. A few — like blu, rosa, viola, arancione — are invariable and never change.

So: un vestito rosso, una gonna rossa, i pantaloni rossi, le scarpe rosse — but always una gonna blu, le scarpe blu.

questo and quello — this and that

Questo (this/these) points to something near you; quello (that/those) points to something farther away. Both agree with their noun. Questo is easy: questo / questa / questi / queste. Quello before a noun follows the same endings as the definite article (il, lo, la, l’, i, gli, le).

When quello stands alone (no noun after it), it simply becomes quello / quella / quelli / quelle: Non voglio questa, voglio quella. (I don't want this one, I want that one.)

molto vs troppo

Molto means very / a lot; troppo means too (much) — more than you want. Before an adjective, both are adverbs and stay invariable: molto bella (very nice), troppo caro (too expensive). Before a noun, they become adjectives and agree: molti vestiti (many clothes), troppe scarpe (too many shoes).

  • La gonna è molto elegante. — The skirt is very elegant. (adverb, no change)
  • Questa camicia è troppo grande. — This shirt is too big. (adverb, no change)
  • Ci sono molte scarpe in saldo. — There are many shoes on sale. (adjective, agrees)

In the shop: useful phrases

To shop, learn these set expressions: Che taglia porta? (What size do you take?), Posso provarlo / provarla? (Can I try it on?) — the -lo / -la on the end agrees with the item, Lo prendo / La prendo (I'll take it), and Quanto costa? (How much does it cost?).

Pronunciation tip

The soft gli and gn: in maglietta and taglia, the gli is a liquid "ly" sound — like the middle of million, with a silent g: "mah-LYEHT-tah", "TAH-lyah". In gonna the double nn is held a beat longer than a single n.

c before i/e is soft: camicia is "kah-MEE-chah" and camerino is "kah-meh-REE-noh" — but cha, che, chi with an h stay hard, like in che taglia ("keh").

Reading: Lo shopping di Anna

Oggi Anna va al centro commerciale per comprare un vestito nuovo. Entra in un negozio e vede una gonna rossa molto bella, ma è troppo cara. La commessa le porta una gonna verde più economica. Anna la prova nel camerino, le piace e la prende.

Translation

Today Anna goes to the shopping centre to buy a new dress. She goes into a shop and sees a very nice red skirt, but it's too expensive. The shop assistant brings her a cheaper green skirt. Anna tries it on in the fitting room, she likes it and she takes it.

Culture note

In Italy clothing sizes use a different scale from the US or UK: a women's dress is often a 40, 42, 44… and a man's shirt a 46, 48, 50…, while shoes use a separate numero (a 38 or 39, not the same as the clothing taglia). The two big sale seasons, the saldi, are fixed by region and run roughly in early January and early July — shoppers wait for them all year. And when you walk into a small boutique, it's normal and polite to greet the staff with a buongiorno: shopping in Italy is a social exchange, not just a transaction.

Talk about it

  • Qual è il tuo colore preferito per i vestiti? _(What is your favourite colour for clothes?)_
  • Preferisci fare shopping in negozio o online? Perché? _(Do you prefer shopping in a shop or online? Why?)_
  • Che taglia porti di solito? _(What size do you usually take?)_
  • Compri spesso vestiti in saldo? _(Do you often buy clothes on sale?)_
