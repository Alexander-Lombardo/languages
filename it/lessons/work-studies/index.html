What you'll learn

  • Talk about your job, workplace and field of study with the right vocabulary.
  • Say how long you have been doing something with da + present tense (Lavoro qui da tre anni).
  • Express purpose with per + infinitive (Studio per trovare un lavoro migliore).
  • Describe a career path and ask others about theirs.

Dialogue

Giulia

Allora, da quanto tempo lavori in questa azienda?

So, how long have you been working at this company?

Marco

Lavoro qui da tre anni, da quando mi sono laureato in ingegneria.

I've been working here for three years, since I graduated in engineering.

Giulia

Bello. E ti trovi bene con i colleghi e con il capo?

Nice. And do you get along well with your colleagues and your boss?

Marco

Sì, abbastanza. Studio l’inglese la sera per fare carriera più in fretta.

Yes, fairly well. I study English in the evening to advance my career faster.

Giulia

Capisco. Io invece ho appena finito un tirocinio in uno studio di architettura.

I see. I, on the other hand, have just finished an internship at an architecture firm.

Marco

E com’è andata? Ti hanno offerto un contratto?

And how did it go? Did they offer you a contract?

Giulia

Per ora no, ma mi hanno chiamata per un secondo colloquio la settimana prossima.

Not for now, but they called me for a second interview next week.

Marco

In bocca al lupo! Da quanto tempo cerchi lavoro nel settore?

Good luck! How long have you been looking for work in the field?

Giulia

Cerco da circa sei mesi. Non è facile, ma studio molto per essere pronta.

I've been looking for about six months. It isn't easy, but I study a lot to be ready.

Marco

Vedrai che ti assumeranno. Hai le competenze giuste.

You'll see, they'll hire you. You have the right skills.

Giulia

Speriamo! Vorrei diventare libera professionista, ma prima serve esperienza.

Let's hope so! I'd like to become a freelancer, but first I need experience.

Grammar

da + present tense: ‘how long’ you’ve been doing something

To say how long an action has been going on — and is still going on now — Italian uses the present tense + da + a length of time. Where English switches to a perfect tense (‘I have been living’), Italian stays in the present, because the action continues into the present moment.

Italian (present + da)English (present perfect)
Lavoro qui da tre anni.I have been working here for three years.
Studio l’italiano da sei mesi.I have been studying Italian for six months.
Vivo a Roma da molto tempo.I have lived in Rome for a long time.
Conosco Marco da quando eravamo bambini.I have known Marco since we were children.

Note that da covers both English ‘for’ (a duration: da tre anni) and ‘since’ (a starting point: dal 2020, da lunedì, da quando…).

Asking ‘how long?’ — da quanto tempo?

The matching question is Da quanto tempo + present tense? The answer mirrors it, keeping the present:

  • — Da quanto tempo studi l’italiano? — Lo studio da due anni.
  • — Da quanto tempo lavori in quest’azienda? — Ci lavoro dal 2021.

Watch out: a common English-speaker mistake is to use the passato prossimo here — ho lavorato qui da tre anni is wrong if you still work there. Use the perfect only for finished actions: Ho lavorato lì per tre anni = ‘I worked there for three years’ (but no longer).

per + infinitive: expressing purpose (‘in order to’)

To say why you do something — the goal or purpose — use per + the infinitive. It translates English ‘to / in order to’.

ItalianEnglish
Studio per trovare un lavoro migliore.I study (in order) to find a better job.
Faccio un tirocinio per imparare il mestiere.I'm doing an internship to learn the trade.
È qui per fare un colloquio.He's here to do a job interview.

Both clauses must share the same subject. If the subjects differ, you need perché + subjunctive instead (Te lo dico perché tu capisca). Don’t confuse per (purpose, ‘in order to’) with per + a noun meaning ‘for’ a duration: per due ore = ‘for two hours’.

Pronunciation tip

laurea / laurearsi: the stress falls early — LAU-re-a (LAH-oo-reh-ah), with the au as one quick gliding syllable, not “low-RAY-ah”. The reflexive laurearsi is what you do (‘to graduate oneself’); Mi sono laureato/a = ‘I graduated’.

In azienda the z is the voiced ‘dz’ sound (ah-DZYEN-dah), while in licenziare the z is the sharper ‘ts’ (lee-chen-TSYAH-reh). Italian uses both for the letter z — let context and habit guide you.

Culture note

In Italy, the route into many professions runs through a laurea (university degree) followed by a tirocinio (internship) and, for regulated fields like law, engineering or accounting, a state exam called the esame di Stato to join the professional register (albo). The posto fisso — a stable, permanent job, classically in the public sector — has long been a cultural ideal, prized for its security, though younger Italians increasingly juggle short-term contracts or work as liberi professionisti with a partita IVA (a VAT/freelance tax number). When someone heads into an interview or exam, you wish them In bocca al lupo! — to which the reply is Crepi!

Talk about it

  • Da quanto tempo studi o lavori nel tuo campo attuale? _(How long have you been studying or working in your current field?)_
  • Che cosa fai per migliorare le tue competenze professionali? _(What do you do to improve your professional skills?)_
  • Preferiresti essere dipendente di un’azienda o libero professionista? Perché? _(Would you rather be a company employee or a freelancer? Why?)_
  • Qual è stato il colloquio più difficile che hai fatto? _(What was the most difficult interview you've done?)_
  • Se potessi cambiare lavoro domani, che cosa faresti e per quale motivo? _(If you could change jobs tomorrow, what would you do and for what reason?)_
