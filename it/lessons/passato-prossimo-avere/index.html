Passato Prossimo with Avere

What you'll learn

  • Form the passato prossimo with avere + a past participle.
  • Build regular participles ending in -ato, -uto and -ito.
  • Use the most common irregular participles correctly.
  • Place past-time markers like ieri and la settimana scorsa in a sentence.

Dialogue

Sara

Ciao Luca! Che cosa hai fatto ieri?

Hi Luca! What did you do yesterday?

Luca

Ho lavorato tutto il giorno, poi ho letto un libro.

I worked all day, then I read a book.

Sara

Io ho comprato un regalo per mia sorella.

I bought a present for my sister.

Luca

Bello! Hai visto il nuovo film al cinema?

Nice! Did you see the new film at the cinema?

Sara

Sì, l’ho visto la settimana scorsa con Marta.

Yes, I saw it last week with Marta.

Luca

Ti è piaciuto? Io non ho ancora avuto tempo.

Did you like it? I haven't had time yet.

Sara

Mi è piaciuto molto. Ho anche scritto una recensione.

I liked it a lot. I even wrote a review.

Luca

Really? Did you say everything online?

Sara

Yes, and I got lots of messages.

Luca

Allora stasera prendo un caffè e lo leggo!

Then tonight I'll have a coffee and read it!

Grammar

What is the passato prossimo?

The passato prossimo is the everyday past tense in Italian. It is a compound tense made of two parts: a helping verb (here avere, in the present tense) plus the past participle of the main verb. Ho mangiato means both I ate and I have eaten.

Regular past participles

To build a regular participle, drop the infinitive ending and add the matching suffix. With avere the participle of these verbs does not change for gender or number — it always ends in -o.

Infinitive typeEndingExample
-are-atoparlare → parlato
-ere-utovendere → venduto
-ire-itodormire → dormito

Time markers & word order

Past-time expressions tell the listener when something happened. Common ones are ieri (yesterday), la settimana scorsa (last week), l’anno scorso (last year) and due giorni fa (two days ago). They usually go at the start or end of the sentence: Ieri ho visto Maria or Ho visto Maria ieri. Short adverbs like già (already) and ancora (yet) sit between the two verbs: Ho già mangiato; Non ho ancora letto la lettera. To make the sentence negative, place non before avere: Non ho parlato.

Pronunciation tip

Listen for the doubled consonants in the irregular participles. Fatto, detto, letto, scritto all have a double t — hold it a beat longer (FAHT-toh, not FAH-toh). The length really changes the word: fato (fate) versus fatto (done). Also note that the h in ho, hai, ha, hanno is completely silent; it is only there to distinguish these forms from words like o (or) and anno (year).

Reading: Il mio fine settimana

Sabato scorso ho passato una bella giornata. La mattina ho preso un caffè al bar e ho letto il giornale. Poi ho comprato del pane fresco e ho preparato il pranzo per la mia famiglia. La sera ho visto un film e ho scritto un messaggio a un amico.

Translation

Last Saturday I had a lovely day. In the morning I had a coffee at the bar and read the newspaper. Then I bought some fresh bread and made lunch for my family. In the evening I watched a film and wrote a message to a friend.

Culture note

In spoken Italian the passato prossimo is the normal way to talk about finished past events, even those of long ago — a Roman might say Ieri ho preso un caffè just as easily as Cento anni fa hanno costruito questa casa. The older passato remoto (e.g. presi, feci) survives mainly in writing and in parts of southern Italy, especially Sicily and Tuscany, where people still use it in everyday speech. For learners, mastering the passato prossimo first will cover almost every real conversation you'll have.

Talk about it

  • Che cosa hai fatto ieri sera? _(What did you do yesterday evening?)_
  • Hai letto un buon libro la settimana scorsa? _(Did you read a good book last week?)_
  • Che film hai visto recentemente? _(What film have you seen recently?)_
  • Hai mai scritto una lettera a mano? _(Have you ever written a letter by hand?)_
