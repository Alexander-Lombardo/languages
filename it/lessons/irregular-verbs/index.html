Dialogue

Luca

Ciao Marta! Dove vai stasera?

Hi Marta! Where are you going tonight?

Marta

Vado a fare una passeggiata in centro. Vieni con me?

I’m going for a walk downtown. Are you coming with me?

Luca

Volentieri, ma prima faccio la spesa al mercato.

Gladly, but first I’m doing the grocery shopping at the market.

Marta

Va bene. Come stai oggi?

All right. How are you today?

Luca

Sto bene, grazie. E tu?

I’m well, thanks. And you?

Marta

Anch’io sto bene. Usciamo insieme alle sei?

I’m well too. Shall we go out together at six?

Luca

Perfetto. Io bevo un caffè e poi vengo.

Perfect. I’ll have (drink) a coffee and then I’ll come.

Marta

Ottimo. Ti do un messaggio quando esco di casa.

Great. I’ll give you (send) a message when I leave the house.

Grammar

The big irregular verbs

Several very common Italian verbs are irregular in the present tense. You simply have to learn them, because you will use them every day. Notice that andare (to go), fare (to do/make), dare (to give) and stare (to stay) share a similar pattern in the plural.

Dà (he/she gives) takes an accent to tell it apart from the preposition da (from).

Venire, uscire, dire, bere

Four more essential verbs. Venire (to come) and uscire (to go out) change the vowel in the stem; dire (to say) and bere (to drink) come from older Latin forms (dicere, bevere), which is why the stem looks unexpected.

Andare a + infinitive

To say you are going to do something, use andare a + infinitive. The little a is required.

  • Vado a mangiare. — I’m going to eat.
  • Andiamo a fare la spesa. — We’re going to do the shopping.
  • Vanno a bere un caffè. — They’re going to have a coffee.

Idioms with fare

Italian uses fare in many fixed expressions where English uses other verbs.

  • fare la spesa — to do the grocery shopping
  • fare una passeggiata — to take a walk
  • fare colazione — to have breakfast
  • fare una domanda — to ask a question

Note: fare la spesa means buying groceries, while fare spese (no article, plural) means general shopping for clothes and so on.

Pronunciation tip

The sc in uscire sounds like English sh before i or e: esci is "EH-shee". But in esco and escono the sc comes before o, so it returns to a hard "sk" sound: "EH-skoh".

Culture note

In Italy, fare la spesa often still means a daily trip to small specialised shops or the open-air mercato, rather than one big weekly supermarket run. Many towns hold a weekly market where locals buy fresh fruit, vegetables and cheese, and the after-dinner passeggiata — a relaxed evening stroll through the town centre — remains a beloved social ritual.
