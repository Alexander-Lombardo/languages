Health & the Body

Dialogue

Dottoressa

Buongiorno! Si accomodi. Come si sente oggi?

Good morning! Have a seat. How do you feel today?

Marco

Buongiorno, dottoressa. Non mi sento bene. Mi sento male da ieri.

Good morning, doctor. I don't feel well. I've felt sick since yesterday.

Dottoressa

Mi dispiace. Che cosa le fa male?

I'm sorry. What hurts you?

Marco

Ho mal di testa e mi fa male la gola.

I have a headache and my throat hurts.

Dottoressa

Ha la febbre?

Do you have a fever?

Marco

Sì, ho un po’ di febbre e anche il raffreddore.

Yes, I have a bit of a fever and a cold too.

Dottoressa

Capisco. Deve riposare e bere molta acqua.

I understand. You must rest and drink a lot of water.

Marco

Devo prendere una medicina?

Do I have to take a medicine?

Dottoressa

Sì, le scrivo una ricetta. Deve prendere questa medicina per cinque giorni.

Yes, I'll write you a prescription. You must take this medicine for five days.

Marco

Grazie mille. Quando starò meglio?

Thank you so much. When will I get better?

Dottoressa

Tra qualche giorno si sentirà meglio. Non deve preoccuparsi.

In a few days you'll feel better. You mustn't worry.

Marco

Perfetto. Arrivederci, dottoressa!

Perfect. Goodbye, doctor!

Grammar

Saying what hurts: Mi fa male… and Ho mal di…

There are two common ways to talk about pain. The first uses the verb fare (to do/make) with an indirect pronoun: literally "to me it makes bad". The body part is the subject, so the verb agrees with it: fa male for one part, fanno male for more than one.

ItalianLiteralMeaning
Mi fa male la testa.to-me makes bad the headMy head hurts.
Mi fa male la gola.to-me makes bad the throatMy throat hurts.
Mi fanno male i piedi.to-me make bad the feetMy feet hurt.
Ti fa male lo stomaco?to-you makes bad the stomachDoes your stomach hurt?

The second way uses avere mal di + body part (no article): Ho mal di testa (I have a headache), Ho mal di gola (I have a sore throat), Ho mal di stomaco, Ho mal di denti. This pattern is fixed and very common for everyday complaints.

Giving advice with dovere (must / have to)

Dovere means "must / to have to". It is followed by an infinitive verb. The doctor uses it to give advice: Deve riposare (You must rest).

How you feel: sentirsi (reflexive)

Sentirsi means "to feel" (a state of health or emotion). It is reflexive, so it uses the pronouns mi, ti, si, ci, vi, si and is followed by an adverb or adjective: bene (well), male (bad/ill), meglio (better), stanco/a (tired).

SubjectsentirsiExample
iomi sentoMi sento male.
tuti sentiTi senti meglio?
lui / leisi senteSi sente bene.
noici sentiamoCi sentiamo stanchi.
voivi sentiteCome vi sentite?
lorosi sentonoSi sentono meglio oggi.

Note the difference: sto male and mi sento male both mean "I feel ill" and are interchangeable here. With sentirsi you can also add meglio (better) or peggio (worse): Oggi mi sento meglio.

Pronunciation tip

The sc in schiena and the c in medicina: before e or i, c sounds soft, like English "ch" — so medicina is "meh-dee-CHEE-nah" and ricetta is "ree-CHEHT-tah". But sch keeps a hard "sk" sound, so schiena is "SKYEH-nah", not "SHYEH-nah". The h is there precisely to keep the c hard.

Double consonants count: raffreddore and febbre have long, held consonants — linger on the ff, dd and bb so they don’t sound like single letters.

Reading: Anna sta male

Oggi Anna non va a scuola perché sta male. Le fa male lo stomaco e ha un po’ di febbre. La mamma le dice che deve restare a letto e riposare. Anna prende una medicina e beve un tè caldo. Domani, forse, si sentirà meglio.

Translation

Today Anna isn't going to school because she's unwell. Her stomach hurts and she has a bit of a fever. Her mum tells her she must stay in bed and rest. Anna takes a medicine and drinks a hot tea. Tomorrow, maybe, she'll feel better.

Culture note

In Italy, basic healthcare is covered by the Servizio Sanitario Nazionale (SSN), the national health service. Everyone has a family doctor, the medico di base (or medico di famiglia), whom you visit first for most problems and who writes the ricetta (prescription) you take to the farmacia. The pharmacy, marked by a green cross, is also where Italians go for quick advice on minor ailments — for a raffreddore or a mal di gola, many people simply ask the farmacista before bothering the doctor.

Talk about it

  • Come ti senti oggi? _(How do you feel today?)_
  • Cosa fai quando hai il raffreddore o la febbre? _(What do you do when you have a cold or a fever?)_
  • Vai spesso dal medico? _(Do you often go to the doctor?)_
  • Secondo te, cosa deve fare una persona che sta male? _(In your opinion, what must a person who is unwell do?)_
