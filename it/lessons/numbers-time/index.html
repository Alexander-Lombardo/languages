Tens, hundreds & thousands

The tens are venti (20), trenta (30), quaranta (40), cinquanta (50), sessanta (60), settanta (70), ottanta (80), novanta (90). You simply attach the unit: ventitré (23), cinquantasei (56). Two spelling rules: the ten drops its final vowel before uno and otto — ventuno (21), trentotto (38) — and tre takes an accent when added: ventitré, quarantatré.

Cento (100) and mille (1000) never change for one: centouno (101), centocinquanta (150), milleduecento (1200). But the plural of mille is -mila: duemila (2000), diecimila (10 000). Italian numbers are written as one word: millenovecentonovantanove (1999).

Telling the time with essere

The time is feminine and plural because le ore (the hours) is understood. Use sono le… for every hour except one o'clock, midday and midnight, which take the singular è.

Add minutes with e (le due e dieci, 2:10) and subtract them toward the next hour with meno (le tre meno cinque, 2:55). To say at a time use all’una / alle due: Il treno parte alle otto.

Days, months, dates & seasons

Days and months are lowercase in Italian and the week starts on lunedì (Monday). For dates the pattern is il + number + month using the plain cardinal number — il cinque marzo (5 March) — except the first of the month, which uses the ordinal il primo: il primo maggio. The four seasons are la primavera (spring), l’estate (summer), l’autunno (autumn) and l’inverno (winter); to say in a season use in primavera, in inverno.

Pronunciation tip

The double z in numbers: mezzo and mezzogiorno use a buzzing dz sound (MED-dzoh), while zero can be dz too. Hold the doubled consonant a beat longer than a single one — it really is pronounced twice.

The accent on lunedì: five of the seven days end in a stressed -ì — lunedì, martedì, mercoledì, giovedì, venerdì — so the voice rises on that final syllable. Only sabato (SA-ba-to) and domenica (do-MEH-ni-ca) break the pattern.

Culture note

In Italy the week starts on Monday, so lunedì sits in the first column of any calendar. Dates are written day/month/year: 5/3/2026 is the 5th of March, never May 3rd. Everyday timetables — trains, museums, TV, shop hours — almost always use the 24-hour clock, so a film at le venti e trenta (20:30) is 8:30 p.m., even though in casual speech people just say le otto e mezza di sera.
