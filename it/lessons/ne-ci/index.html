The Particles Ne & Ci

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Use ne to replace quantities and partitives (ne ho due, ne voglio un po’).
  • Use ne to mean “of it / about it” after verbs and expressions that take di.
  • Use ci as a locative (“there”) and with verbs like pensarci and crederci.
  • Form ce n’è and ce ne sono, and make the past participle agree with ne + quantity.

Dialogue

Luca

Vado al supermercato. Ci vai anche tu?

I’m going to the supermarket. Are you going there too?

Sara

No, ci sono già stata stamattina. Cosa devi comprare?

No, I already went there this morning. What do you need to buy?

Luca

Le uova. Quante ne abbiamo in frigo?

Eggs. How many (of them) do we have in the fridge?

Sara

Ce ne sono ancora tre, ma non bastano per la torta.

There are still three (of them), but they’re not enough for the cake.

Luca

Allora ne prendo una dozzina. C’è anche del latte?

Then I’ll get a dozen. Is there any milk too?

Sara

Ce n’è ancora un po’, ma compranene un litro.

There’s still a little, but buy a litre of it.

Luca

Va bene. Pensi che dobbiamo prendere anche il caffè?

Okay. Do you think we should get coffee too?

Sara

Sì, ci tengo molto al caffè la mattina. Non posso farne a meno.

Yes, I really care about coffee in the morning. I can’t do without it.

Luca

Tranquilla, ci penso io. Ho voglia di fare una torta stasera.

Don’t worry, I’ll take care of it. I feel like making a cake tonight.

Sara

Davvero? Quasi non ci credo! Ne sei capace?

Really? I almost don’t believe it! Are you capable of it?

Luca

Vedremo. Se viene bene, ne saremo orgogliosi tutti e due.

We’ll see. If it turns out well, we’ll both be proud of it.

Sara

Allora vai, che si fa tardi. Me ne vado a preparare la teglia!

Then go, it’s getting late. I’m off to get the cake tin ready!

Grammar

The particle ne: “of it / of them”

The pronoun ne replaces a noun introduced by a number, a quantity word, or the preposition di. It goes before a conjugated verb and English often leaves it untranslated. Its three big jobs:

JobReplacesExample
Quantity / numbera noun + number“Quante mele vuoi?” → “Ne voglio due.” (I want two of them)
Partitive (“some / any”)del / della / un po’ di + noun“Vuoi del pane?” → “Sì, ne voglio un po’.” / “No, non ne voglio.”
“about / of it” (di)di + thing/topicparlare di → “Ne parliamo domani.” (We’ll talk about it tomorrow)

Many fixed expressions need ne because the verb governs di: avere bisogno di → ne ho bisogno; fare a meno di → non posso farne a meno; essere capace di → ne sei capace?

Participle agreement with ne + quantity

In compound tenses (passato prossimo), when ne stands for a quantity, the past participle agrees in gender and number with the noun that quantity refers to — not with the subject.

QuestionAnswer with neAgreement
Quante mele hai mangiato?Ne ho mangiate due.fem. plural → -e
Quanti libri hai letto?Ne ho letti tre.masc. plural → -i
Quanta torta hai preso?Ne ho presa una fetta.fem. singular → -a

The particle ci: place and verbs

Ci most often means “there”, replacing a place introduced by a, in, su, da: “Vai a Roma?” → “Sì, ci vado domani.” It also pairs with verbs that govern a / in / su to mean “about it / in it / on it”:

  • pensare a → pensarci: “Ci penso io.” (I’ll take care of it / think about it)
  • credere a/in → crederci: “Non ci credo!” (I don’t believe it!)
  • tenere a → tenerci: “Ci tengo molto.” (I really care about it)
  • volerci = to take/be needed: “Ci vuole un’ora.” / “Ci vogliono due uova.”

Ce n’è and ce ne sono

When ci (here in its existential use, “there is/are”) meets ne, ci becomes ce. Use ce n’è with singular/uncountable amounts and ce ne sono with plurals:

FormUseExample
ce n’èsingular / “some”“C’è del pane?” → “Sì, ce n’è ancora un po’.”
ce ne sonoplural + number“Ci sono uova?” → “Sì, ce ne sono tre.”

Note the order of weak pronouns: ci/ce always comes before ne, never the other way round.

Pronunciation tip

ci = “chee”, ce = “cheh”. The letter c before i or e is the soft “ch” of church: ci vado (chee VAH-doh), ce ne sono (cheh neh SOH-noh). Don’t pronounce it like English “see.”

The elision n’è. In ce n’è the e of ne drops before è, giving a smooth “cheh neh.” Keep the accent on that final è (the verb essere) — it carries the meaning “there is.” Compare ce ne (unstressed, before another word) in ce ne sono.

Culture note

The particles ne and ci hide inside some of the most Italian phrases there are. “Non ci credo!” is the everyday cry of disbelief, and “ci penso io” is what a host or a friend says to wave away your offer of help — a small verbal gesture of generosity. At the bar, you’ll constantly hear “ce n’è ancora?” (is there any left?). And the reflexive andarsene — literally “to take oneself away from here” — is far more common than plain andare via: when an Italian sighs “me ne vado”, those two little particles carry all the feeling of getting up and leaving.

Talk about it

  • C’è qualcosa di cui non puoi fare a meno la mattina? _(Is there something you can’t do without in the morning?)_
  • A cosa tieni di più nella vita? Parlane un po’. _(What do you care about most in life? Talk about it a bit.)_
  • Quanto tempo ci vuole per arrivare al tuo lavoro? _(How long does it take to get to your work?)_
  • C’è un sogno in cui credi ancora? Ci pensi spesso? _(Is there a dream you still believe in? Do you think about it often?)_
  • Quando hai voglia di andartene da una festa, cosa dici? _(When you feel like leaving a party, what do you say?)_
