Narrating a Story

What you'll learn

  • Combine passato prossimo and imperfetto to tell what happened against a background.
  • Use the trapassato prossimo to mark an action that happened before another past action.
  • Sequence a story with connectors (prima, poi, dopo, infine, allora, a un certo punto, improvvisamente).
  • Read and understand a longer past-tense narrative.

Dialogue

Marta

Allora, com’è andata ieri sera? Mi sembri ancora sconvolta!

So, how did it go last night? You still seem shaken!

Giulia

Te la racconto. Dunque, ero appena uscita dall’ufficio ed era già buio.

Let me tell you. So, I had just left the office and it was already dark.

Marta

E pioveva, immagino, con il tempo che faceva.

And it was raining, I imagine, with the weather we were having.

Giulia

Sì, pioveva forte. Mentre camminavo verso la macchina, ho sentito un rumore strano.

Yes, it was raining hard. While I was walking towards the car, I heard a strange noise.

Marta

Un rumore? E tu cosa hai fatto?

A noise? And what did you do?

Giulia

Prima mi sono fermata, poi mi sono girata. A un certo punto è apparso un gatto enorme!

First I stopped, then I turned around. At a certain point a huge cat appeared!

Marta

Un gatto? Pensavo a qualcosa di più pericoloso!

A cat? I was thinking of something more dangerous!

Giulia

Aspetta. Avevo già aperto la portiera quando, improvvisamente, è saltato dentro la macchina.

Wait. I had already opened the car door when, suddenly, it jumped into the car.

Marta

Non ci credo! E poi?

I don’t believe it! And then?

Giulia

Allora ho acceso la luce e mi sono accorta che aveva un collare. Apparteneva a qualcuno.

So I turned on the light and I realised that it had a collar. It belonged to someone.

Marta

Quindi avevi trovato un gatto smarrito mentre tornavi a casa.

So you had found a lost cat while you were going home.

Giulia

Esatto. Infine ho chiamato il numero sul collare e, alla fine, l’ho riportato alla sua padrona.

Exactly. Finally I called the number on the collar and, in the end, I took it back to its owner.

Grammar

The three past tenses of narration

A good Italian story weaves together three past tenses. Each one answers a different question:

TenseJob in the storyExample
Imperfettobackground, description, ongoing or habitual actions (“setting the scene”)Era buio e pioveva. (It was dark and it was raining.)
Passato prossimothe events that move the plot forward, completed single actionsHo sentito un rumore e mi sono girata. (I heard a noise and turned around.)
Trapassato prossimoan action completed before another past action (“the past of the past”)Avevo già aperto la portiera quando il gatto è saltato dentro. (I had already opened the door when…)

Imperfetto vs. passato prossimo

Think of the imperfetto as the stage and the passato prossimo as the action on it. The background keeps going (imperfetto) while a punctual event interrupts it (passato prossimo). The connector mentre (“while”) almost always introduces an imperfetto:

  • Mentre camminavo (background, imperfetto), ho sentito un rumore (event, passato prossimo).
  • Da bambino giocavo sempre qui (habit → imperfetto) vs. Ieri ho giocato a calcio (one finished event → passato prossimo).
  • States of mind and weather are usually imperfetto: avevo paura, era tardi, faceva freddo.

The trapassato prossimo

Formed exactly like the passato prossimo but with the auxiliary in the imperfetto: avevo / ero + past participle. It marks what had already happened before the moment you are narrating. Same rules for choosing avere or essere, and the same participle agreement with essere.

Sequencing connectors

These little words give a story its shape. Use them to open lines and link events:

  • prima = first / beforehand; poi = then; dopo = afterwards; infine / alla fine = finally / in the end.
  • allora = so, at that point (a reaction to what just happened): Allora ho acceso la luce.
  • a un certo punto = at a certain point; improvvisamente / all’improvviso = suddenly — perfect for the dramatic turn.
  • appena = as soon as: Appena sono arrivato, ho chiamato.

Pronunciation tip

Stress in the imperfetto. In the noi form the stress falls on the -va- syllable, not the final one: parla-VA-mo, sali-VA-mo, aspetta-VA-mo, era-VA-mo. In the voi form, likewise: parla-VA-te. Keep the vowels clean and don’t rush the double consonants.

improvvisamente is a mouthful: break it as im-prov-vi-za-MEN-te, with the double v held a touch longer and the stress on -MEN-. In speech Italians often prefer the shorter all’improvviso.

Reading: La gita finita male

Quell’estate avevamo deciso di fare un’escursione in montagna. Ci eravamo preparati con cura: la sera prima avevamo controllato lo zaino, avevamo caricato i telefoni e avevamo studiato il sentiero sulla mappa. La mattina della gita c’era un bel sole e tutto sembrava perfetto. Siamo partiti presto, verso le sei, mentre il paese dormiva ancora. Prima abbiamo camminato lungo il fiume, poi siamo saliti tra i boschi. Mentre salivamo, parlavamo e ridevamo, perché eravamo di ottimo umore. A un certo punto, però, il cielo è cambiato. Le nuvole che prima erano lontane erano diventate scure e improvvisamente ha cominciato a piovere. Non avevamo portato gli ombrelli, perché il meteo aveva promesso bel tempo tutto il giorno. Allora abbiamo cercato un riparo sotto un grande albero e abbiamo aspettato. Mentre aspettavamo, mi sono accorto che avevo dimenticato la mappa nello zaino di Luca, che era già tornato indietro. Dopo quasi un’ora la pioggia è finita e siamo riusciti a continuare. Infine, stanchi e bagnati, siamo arrivati in cima. Il panorama era splendido e, alla fine, quella gita finita male è diventata il ricordo più bello dell’estate.

Translation

That summer we had decided to go on a hike in the mountains. We had prepared carefully: the evening before we had checked the backpack, charged the phones and studied the trail on the map. On the morning of the trip there was beautiful sunshine and everything seemed perfect. We set off early, around six, while the village was still sleeping. First we walked along the river, then we climbed up through the woods. As we were climbing, we talked and laughed, because we were in a great mood. At a certain point, however, the sky changed. The clouds that before had been far away had turned dark and suddenly it started to rain. We hadn’t brought umbrellas, because the forecast had promised good weather all day. So we looked for shelter under a big tree and waited. While we were waiting, I realised that I had left the map in Luca’s backpack, and he had already gone back. After almost an hour the rain ended and we managed to continue. Finally, tired and wet, we reached the top. The view was splendid and, in the end, that trip that had gone badly became the best memory of the summer.

Culture note

Italians are natural storytellers, and you’ll hear a story begin with the little word “allora…” — a verbal throat-clearing that means roughly “right, so…”. The tradition of the racconto runs deep: from the medieval tales of Boccaccio’s Decameron, where ten young people flee the plague and pass the time telling stories, to the fables that Italo Calvino collected from every region in Fiabe italiane. Around the family table, the past tenses you’re learning come alive: the imperfetto paints “com’era una volta” (how things used to be), while the passato prossimo delivers the punchline of what finally happened.

Talk about it

  • Raccontami una volta in cui qualcosa è andato storto durante un viaggio. _(Tell me about a time when something went wrong during a trip.)_
  • Ti è mai successo qualcosa di improvviso e inaspettato? Cosa stavi facendo in quel momento? _(Has something sudden and unexpected ever happened to you? What were you doing at that moment?)_
  • Qual è il ricordo d’infanzia che racconti più spesso? _(What childhood memory do you tell most often?)_
  • Quando eri piccolo, com’era una tua giornata tipica? _(When you were little, what was a typical day of yours like?)_
  • Hai mai trovato un oggetto o un animale smarrito? Com’è finita la storia? _(Have you ever found a lost object or animal? How did the story end?)_
