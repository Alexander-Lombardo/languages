Dialogue

Giulia

Senti, hai ancora il libro di cucina che ti ho prestato?

Listen, do you still have the cookbook I lent you?

Marco

Sì, ce l’ho ancora. Te lo restituisco domani, va bene?

Yes, I still have it. I’ll give it back to you tomorrow, okay?

Giulia

Tranquillo, non c’è fretta. Hai provato quella ricetta della pasta?

No worries, there’s no rush. Did you try that pasta recipe?

Marco

L’ho provata ieri sera! L’ho fatta per Anna e le è piaciuta tantissimo.

I tried it last night! I made it for Anna and she loved it.

Giulia

Davvero? Allora me la spieghi? Vorrei prepararla anch’io.

Really? So will you explain it to me? I’d like to make it too.

Marco

Certo, te la spiego volentieri. Anzi, te la scrivo e te la mando per messaggio.

Of course, I’ll gladly explain it. Actually, I’ll write it down and send it to you by text.

Giulia

Perfetto. E gli ingredienti? Dove li hai comprati?

Perfect. And the ingredients? Where did you buy them?

Marco

Li ho presi al mercato. Se vuoi, posso comprarteli anche a te.

I got them at the market. If you want, I can buy them for you too.

Giulia

Compramene un po’, allora! Ti do i soldi appena ci vediamo.

Buy me some, then! I’ll give you the money as soon as we meet.

Marco

Non preoccuparti, me ne occupo io. Comunque non costano molto.

Don’t worry, I’ll take care of it. Anyway, they’re not expensive.

Giulia

Sei un tesoro. Glielo dirò ad Anna che cucini benissimo!

You’re a darling. I’ll tell Anna that you cook really well!

Marco

Non esagerare! Comunque grazie, mi fa piacere.

Don’t exaggerate! Anyway thanks, that’s nice of you.

Grammar

1. Direct vs. indirect object pronouns

A direct object answers chi? / che cosa? (whom? / what?) with no preposition: Leggo il libro → Lo leggo. An indirect object answers a chi? (to whom?) and replaces a + person: Scrivo a Marco → Gli scrivo.

PersonDirect (lo/la…)Indirect (a + person)
memimi
you (sing.)titi
him / it (m.)logli
her / it (f.)lale
uscici

them (m.)ligli (loro)
them (f.)legli (loro)

Notice the overlap: mi, ti, ci, vi are identical as direct and indirect. The differences appear in the third person: direct lo / la / li / le vs. indirect gli / le / gli. In modern spoken Italian gli covers both to him and to them; the formal alternative loro goes after the verb: Scrivo loro.

2. Past-participle agreement with lo, la, li, le

In the passato prossimo with avere, the past participle normally does not change. But when a direct-object pronoun lo, la, li, le comes before the verb, the participle agrees with it in gender and number. Lo and la usually elide to l’.

QuestionAnswer with agreement
Hai comprato il pane?L’ho comprato.
Hai visto la partita?L’ho vista.
Hai preso i biglietti?Li ho presi.
Hai fatto le foto?Le ho fatte.

Agreement is obligatory with lo, la, li, le (and with ne). With mi, ti, ci, vi it is optional: Ci ha visti / Ci ha visto are both heard. Indirect pronouns never cause agreement: Le ho scritto (to her, no -a).

3. Combined pronouns: indirect + direct

When both pronouns appear, the indirect comes first, then the direct, and before lo/la/li/le/ne the final -i of the indirect becomes -e: mi → me, ti → te, ci → ce, vi → ve. The third-person indirect gli and le both become glie-, written as one word: glielo, gliela, glieli, gliele, gliene.

+ lo+ la+ li+ le+ ne
me lome lame lime leme ne
te lote late lite lete ne
glieloglielaglieliglielegliene
ce loce lace lice lece ne

Examples: Me lo dai? (Will you give it to me?) — Te la spiego. (I’ll explain it to you.) — Glielo dico domani. (I’ll tell it to him/her tomorrow.) — Te ne porto due. (I’ll bring you two of them.) The participle still agrees with the direct part: Gliel’ho data (the letter → la).

4. Position: before the verb, or attached to it

With a normal conjugated verb the pronouns go before it: Te lo do. But they attach to the end (forming one word) of an infinitive (which drops its final -e), a gerund, and the informal imperative.

FormExampleMeaning
infinitiveVoglio dartelo.I want to give it to you.
gerundSpiegandomelo, ho capito.By explaining it to me, I understood.
imperative (tu)Dammilo! / Dammelo!Give it to me!
imperative (negative)Non dirglielo! / Non glielo dire!Don’t tell it to him!

With dovere, potere, volere + infinitive, both positions are correct: Te lo posso dire = Posso dirtelo. In short tu imperatives (da’, di’, fa’, va’, sta’) the first consonant of the pronoun doubles: dammi, dillo, fallo, vacci — but with gli it does not: digli, faglielo.

Pronunciation tip

One word, one stress. When a pronoun attaches to a verb, the combination is written and stressed as a single word, and the original stress of the verb stays put: dàmmelo (DAM-meh-loh), spiegàrtelo (spyeh-GAR-teh-loh), portàglielo (por-TA-lyeh-loh). The added pronouns are pronounced lightly, never stressed.

glie- = “LYEH”. The gli in glielo, gliela, gliene is the same soft “lly” sound as in famiglia, never a hard g: say glielo as “LYEH-loh”, not “glee-eh-loh”.

Culture note

The little word glielo is everywhere in real Italian conversation, and so is the polite habit of restituire un favore — returning a favour. In Italy, exchanges of small favours among friends, neighbours and family knit daily life together: someone lends you a trapano (drill) or passes on a ricetta di famiglia, and you’re expected to reciprocate when the moment comes. You’ll often hear Te lo devo (“I owe you one”) or the warm, all-purpose thank-you Mi fa piacere — exactly the kind of phrases that come loaded with the combined pronouns this lesson drills.

Talk about it

  • Quando presti qualcosa a un amico, glielo ricordi di restituirtelo? _(When you lend something to a friend, do you remind them to give it back to you?)_
  • C’è una ricetta che qualcuno ti ha insegnato? Chi te l’ha spiegata? _(Is there a recipe someone taught you? Who explained it to you?)_
  • Se un amico ti chiede un favore difficile, glielo fai volentieri? _(If a friend asks you for a difficult favour, do you do it for them gladly?)_
  • Ti fidi facilmente delle persone o ci metti tempo a fidarti? _(Do you trust people easily, or does it take you time to trust them?)_
  • Qual è l’ultimo regalo che hai fatto a qualcuno? Perché gliel’hai regalato? _(What is the last gift you gave someone? Why did you give it to them?)_
