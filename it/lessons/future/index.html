The Future Tense (futuro semplice)

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Form the regular futuro semplice for -are, -ere and -ire verbs.
  • Apply spelling changes (cercherò, pagherò, comincerò) correctly.
  • Use the common irregular future stems (sarò, avrò, farò, andrò…).
  • Express probability or a guess about the present with the future of probability.

Dialogue

Sara

Allora, cosa farai dopo la laurea?

So, what will you do after graduation?

Davide

Mi laureerò a luglio e poi cercherò lavoro a Milano.

I'll graduate in July and then I'll look for work in Milan.

Sara

Davvero? Ti trasferirai lì da solo?

Really? Will you move there on your own?

Davide

Sì, all’inizio sarà difficile, ma fra qualche mese starò meglio.

Yes, at first it'll be hard, but in a few months I'll be doing better.

Sara

Avrai un colloquio presto?

Will you have an interview soon?

Davide

Ne avrò due la settimana prossima. Vedremo come andrà.

I'll have two next week. We'll see how it goes.

Sara

In bocca al lupo! E dove abiterai?

Good luck! And where will you live?

Davide

Non lo so ancora. Probabilmente prenderò una stanza in centro.

I don't know yet. I'll probably take a room in the centre.

Sara

Tuo fratello è già a Milano, no? Sarà contento di rivederti.

Your brother is already in Milan, right? He must be happy to see you again.

Davide

Sì! Faremo molte cose insieme. Chissà, magari resterò per sempre.

Yes! We'll do lots of things together. Who knows, maybe I'll stay for good.

Grammar

The regular futuro semplice

The future tense translates English will / shall (and the colloquial I'm going to…). It is built on the infinitive: drop the final -e, and for -are verbs change the a of the ending to e. So all three conjugations share the same set of endings (-rò, -rai, -rà, -remo, -rete, -ranno), and -are and -ere verbs end up identical (-erò, -irò).

Note the written accent on the io and lui/lei forms: parlerò, parlerà. Without it the word is wrong.

Spelling changes

Two groups of -are verbs adjust their spelling so the sound of the stem stays the same:

  • Verbs in -care / -gare add an h to keep the hard k/g sound: cercare → cercherò, pagare → pagherò, giocare → giocherò, spiegare → spiegherò.
  • Verbs in -ciare / -giare drop the i (it isn't needed before e): cominciare → comincerò, mangiare → mangerò, cominciare/lasciare → lascerò.

Some very common verbs use a shortened or modified stem, but they take the same regular endings. Many drop the vowel of the infinitive ending (avere → avr-); a few double the r (venire → verr-).

The future of probability (futuro epistemico)

Italian uses the future not only for what will happen, but to guess about the present — like English must be / is probably / I wonder. This is the futuro di probabilità.

  • Che ore sono? — Saranno le otto. (It must be about eight.)
  • Dov’è Marco? — Sarà a casa. (He's probably at home.)
  • Quanti anni ha? — Avrà trent’anni. (He must be around thirty.)

It expresses a present guess, not a future event, so it usually pairs with words like forse, probabilmente, chissà.

Pronunciation tip

Mind the stressed accent. The io form ends in a stressed -ò (par-leh-RÒH) and the lui/lei form in a stressed -à (par-leh-RÀH). Don't let the stress slide back — parlerò sounds quite different from the present parlerei (conditional). The -anno ending (parleranno) has a clear long double n: par-leh-RAHN-noh.

In the doubled-r stems (verrò, vorrò, berrò) really trill or hold the rr; otherwise verrò (I'll come) can be confused with vorrò sounds blurred together.

Culture note

In everyday spoken Italian, people often use the present tense for near-future plans that feel certain — Domani vado a Roma rather than andrò a Roma — much as English uses I'm going. The full futuro semplice is kept for things that are more distant, less certain, or for promises and predictions (Un giorno cambierà tutto). The future of probability, though, is alive and well in daily speech: hear someone say Saranno le tre ("it must be around three") or Avrà fame ("he's probably hungry") and you'll know they're guessing, not predicting.

Talk about it

  • Cosa farai il prossimo fine settimana? _(What will you do next weekend?)_
  • Dove andrai in vacanza l’anno prossimo? _(Where will you go on holiday next year?)_
  • Come sarà la tua vita fra dieci anni, secondo te? _(What do you think your life will be like in ten years?)_
  • Ti trasferirai mai in un altro Paese? Perché sì o perché no? _(Will you ever move to another country? Why or why not?)_
  • Che ore saranno adesso in Italia? Prova a indovinare con il futuro di probabilità. _(What time must it be in Italy now? Try to guess using the future of probability.)_
