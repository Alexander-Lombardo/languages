Dialogue

Nonna

Quando ero giovane, vivevo in campagna con i miei genitori.

When I was young, I used to live in the countryside with my parents.

Sofia

Davvero? E com’era la vita lì, nonna?

Really? And what was life like there, grandma?

Nonna

Era tranquilla. Ogni giorno facevo una lunga passeggiata.

It was quiet. Every day I used to take a long walk.

Sofia

Andavi anche a scuola a piedi?

Did you also go to school on foot?

Nonna

Sì, mentre camminavo, parlavo con le mie amiche.

Yes, while I was walking, I would talk with my friends.

Sofia

E che cosa bevevate a colazione?

And what did you used to drink for breakfast?

Nonna

Bevevamo latte fresco. I nonni avevano una mucca!

We used to drink fresh milk. The grandparents had a cow!

Sofia

Che bello! Eri felice da bambina?

How lovely! Were you happy as a child?

Nonna

Molto. Avevo otto anni e il mondo mi sembrava enorme.

Very. I was eight years old and the world seemed enormous to me.

Sofia

Mi piace quando dici queste storie.

I like it when you tell these stories.

Grammar

What the imperfetto is for

The imperfetto is a past tense that describes what used to happen, what was happening, or how things were. Use it for: repeated habits in the past (ogni giorno camminavo), an ongoing background action (mentre leggevo…), descriptions of people, places and feelings (la casa era grande), and for age, time, and weather in the past (avevo dieci anni; erano le tre; faceva freddo).

Regular endings

Take the stem and add the endings below. Notice that the only difference between the three verb groups is the vowel: -av-, -ev-, -iv-. The personal endings (-o, -i, -a, -amo, -ate, -ano) are the same for all verbs.

The irregulars you must know

The imperfetto is wonderfully regular — almost no verb is irregular. The big exception is essere. A few others (fare, dire, bere) only look irregular because they use their old Latin stem, then take the normal -ev- endings.

Age, time and weather

These are almost always in the imperfetto, because they describe a state, not a single completed event.

  • Age: Avevo sei anni. — I was six years old.
  • Time: Erano le otto di sera. — It was eight in the evening.
  • Weather: Faceva freddo e pioveva. — It was cold and raining.
  • Two background actions: Mentre cucinavo, ascoltavo la radio. — While I was cooking, I was listening to the radio.

Pronunciation tip

Don’t confuse parlavamo (we used to speak, imperfetto) with parliamo (we speak, present). The imperfetto noi form always has that extra -va- in the middle: par-la-VA-mo. Keep the stress on the -va- and you’ll sound natural.

Reading: L’estate dai nonni

Da bambino, ogni estate andavo dai nonni in campagna. Faceva caldo e il cielo era sempre azzurro. La mattina mi svegliavo presto, bevevo un bicchiere di latte e poi giocavo in giardino fino a sera. Erano giorni felici e tranquilli.

Translation

As a child, every summer I used to go to my grandparents’ in the countryside. It was hot and the sky was always blue. In the morning I would wake up early, drink a glass of milk and then play in the garden until evening. They were happy, peaceful days.

Culture note

In Italy, the imperfetto is the natural tense of storytelling and nostalgia. Grandparents — i nonni — hold a treasured place in family life, and Sunday lunches often fill with tales that begin «Quando ero giovane…» (When I was young…). Many older Italians grew up in the campagna before the post-war move to the cities, so their childhood stories of fresh milk, village markets and life without television are a beloved part of family memory.

Talk about it

  • Dove vivevi quando eri bambino o bambina? _(Where did you live when you were a child?)_
  • Che cosa facevi ogni estate? _(What did you used to do every summer?)_
  • Com’era la tua scuola quando eri piccolo? _(What was your school like when you were little?)_
  • Che cosa ti piaceva fare con i nonni? _(What did you like to do with your grandparents?)_
