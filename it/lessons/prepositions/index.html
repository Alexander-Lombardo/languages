Dialogue

Sara

Vado dal panettiere all’angolo: ci vuoi qualcosa?

I'm going to the baker's on the corner: do you want anything from there?

Luca

Sì, prendi del pane e un po’ di latte, per favore.

Yes, get some bread and a bit of milk, please.

Sara

Compro anche delle paste per il dolce di stasera.

I'll also buy some pastries for tonight's dessert.

Luca

Ottimo. Questo libro sul tavolo è tuo o della biblioteca?

Great. Is this book on the table yours or the library's?

Sara

È della biblioteca; lo riporto tornando dal centro.

It's the library's; I'll take it back on my way from the centre.

Luca

A che ora torni dai tuoi?

What time are you coming back from your parents'?

Sara

Sto dai miei fino alle sette, poi passo in farmacia.

I'm staying at my parents' until seven, then I'll stop by the pharmacy.

Luca

Aspetta, ho del lavoro da finire: è un progetto degli studenti.

Wait, I have some work to finish: it's the students' project.

Sara

Quel quadro alla parete è dipinto da un amico, vero?

That picture on the wall is painted by a friend, right?

Luca

Sì, lo conosco dai tempi dell’università. Vivo qui da tre anni con lui vicino.

Yes, I've known him since university days. I've lived here for three years near him.

Sara

Bello. Allora ci vediamo per cena, verso le otto.

Nice. So we'll see each other for dinner, around eight.

Grammar

Forming the articulated prepositions

Five prepositions must fuse with a following definite article: di, a, da, in, su. The result is one written word. Note that di → de- and in → ne- change their vowel; the others keep it.

con and per do not contract in modern standard Italian: say con il treno, per la festa. You may still see the old forms col (con + il) and rarely coi, but they are optional and best avoided in writing.

da vs di: which one?

Both can translate English ‘of/from’, but they cover different jobs. Roughly: di shows possession, material, content and origin-of-birth; da shows motion away from, the agent of a passive, a point in time (‘since/for’), purpose, and ‘at someone’s place’.

Watch the contrast: di Roma = ‘from Rome’ as a birthplace; da Roma = ‘(coming) from Rome’ as a movement. Vengo da Roma (I’m travelling from Rome) ≠ Sono di Roma (I was born there).

The partitive: del / dei / delle = ‘some / any’

The articulated forms of di double as the partitive article, meaning an unspecified quantity — English ‘some’ or ‘any’. Use the form that agrees with the noun: del/dello/della for uncountable singulars, dei/degli/delle as the plural of the indefinite article (the ‘some’ that ‘un/una’ lacks in the plural).

  • Vorrei del pane e dell’acqua. (some bread and some water)
  • Ho comprato delle mele e dei biscotti. (some apples and some biscuits)
  • Plural of un libro → dei libri (some books).

Alternatives that mean the same: un po’ di (+ uncountable: un po’ di latte), qualche (+ singular noun: qualche mela = a few apples), and alcuni/alcune (+ plural). In negative sentences the partitive is usually dropped: Non ho pane. (I don’t have any bread.)

Pronunciation tip

Don’t glue what stays apart: only di, a, da, in, su fuse with the article. Keep con and per as separate words — con la macchina, not “colla macchina” in speech.

gli before contracting: in degli, agli, dagli, negli, sugli the gli keeps the soft "ly" sound (as in English "million"): deh-lyee, ah-lyee, neh-lyee. And mind the apostrophe forms dell’, all’, dall’, nell’, sull’ before a vowel — link them smoothly: nell’acqua = nehl-LAK-kwa.

Culture note

The little word da powers one of Italy’s most charming idioms: andare dal + a profession or shop. Italians say vado dal fornaio (I’m going to the baker’s), dal macellaio (the butcher’s), dal barbiere (the barber’s) — naming the person rather than the place, a habit from a time when the bottega (small artisan shop) was known by its owner. The same da means ‘at someone’s home’ (ceniamo da Marco) and even appears on restaurant signs: Da Luigi, Da Nonna — literally ‘At Luigi’s’.

Talk about it

  • Da quanto tempo studi l’italiano? _(How long have you been studying Italian?)_
  • Di solito compri del pane fresco o dei prodotti surgelati? _(Do you usually buy some fresh bread or some frozen products?)_
  • Quando vai dal medico o dal dentista, ci vai a piedi o in macchina? _(When you go to the doctor’s or dentist’s, do you go on foot or by car?)_
  • Qual è un oggetto che tieni sempre sul tuo tavolo? _(What’s an object you always keep on your table?)_
  • Da chi hai imparato qualcosa di importante nella vita? _(From whom have you learned something important in life?)_
