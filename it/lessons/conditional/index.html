The Conditional & Polite Requests

Dialogue

Cliente

Buongiorno, scusi il disturbo. Vorrei prenotare un tavolo per stasera.

Good morning, sorry to bother you. I’d like to book a table for tonight.

Cameriere

Certo, signore. Per quante persone sarebbe?

Of course, sir. For how many people would it be?

Cliente

Saremmo in quattro. Potrei avere un tavolo vicino alla finestra?

There’d be four of us. Could I have a table near the window?

Cameriere

Vediamo… Sì, gliene darei volentieri uno, ma è prenotato fino alle nove.

Let’s see… Yes, I’d gladly give you one, but it’s booked until nine.

Cliente

Va bene. Allora preferiremmo arrivare verso le nove e mezza.

All right. Then we’d prefer to arrive around half past nine.

Cameriere

Perfetto. Le consiglierei il menù di pesce: stasera sarebbe ottimo.

Perfect. I’d recommend the fish menu to you: tonight it would be excellent.

Cliente

Magari! Però mia moglie non mangia pesce. Cosa direbbe di proporre?

I wish! But my wife doesn’t eat fish. What would you suggest?

Cameriere

Direi una bella grigliata di carne. A lei piacerebbe sicuramente.

I’d say a nice mixed grill. She would surely like it.

Cliente

Ottimo. Un’ultima cosa: potremmo pagare con la carta?

Excellent. One last thing: could we pay by card?

Cameriere

Senz’altro. Dovrebbe solo avvisarci se cambiate l’orario. A stasera!

Absolutely. You’d only need to let us know if you change the time. See you tonight!

Grammar

How the present conditional is built

The present conditional (condizionale presente) expresses what would happen, and it is the natural mood for politeness, advice and tentative opinions. It is built on the same stem as the future tense, plus a single set of endings used by all three verb groups. As in the future, -are verbs change their a to e, so parlare → parler-.

Irregular stems (the same as the future)

The verbs that are irregular in the future use exactly the same stem in the conditional — just swap the future endings for the conditional ones. Memorise the stem once and it serves both tenses.

Polite requests, advice and opinions

The conditional softens what would sound blunt in the present. Compare Voglio un caffè (“I want a coffee”) with the far more courteous Vorrei un caffè (“I’d like a coffee”). Three modal verbs do most of the work:

  • vorrei — for polite requests and wishes: Vorrei prenotare un tavolo.
  • potrei / potresti — to ask permission or make a request: Potresti aiutarmi, per favore? (“Could you help me?”)
  • dovrei / dovresti — to give advice (“should / ought to”): Dovresti riposarti di più. (“You should rest more.”)

For tentative opinions and hypotheses, the conditional makes a claim sound like a suggestion rather than a fact: Direi di sì (“I’d say yes”), Sarebbe meglio aspettare (“It would be better to wait”), Mi piacerebbe vivere a Roma (“I’d love to live in Rome”).

Pronunciation tip

Double consonants carry the meaning: the conditional lives on its long consonants. Sarebbe is sah-REHB-beh with a clearly held bb, and vorrei (vor-RAY), potrei and dovrei need a strong rr/tr/vr. Rushing them turns vorrei into the present-like vorei, which sounds off to an Italian ear.

noi vs loro: don’t confuse -emmo (parleremmo, “we would”) with the future -emo, nor -ebbero (parlerebbero, “they would”). Stress the -EB- and let the -bero trail off lightly: par-leh-REHB-beh-roh.

Culture note

In Italy the conditional is the heartbeat of everyday courtesy. Walk into a bar and you’ll rarely hear “Voglio un caffè”; the polite norm is “Vorrei un caffè, per favore” or simply “Un caffè, per cortesia.” The word magari deserves special note: paired with the conditional it can mean “maybe,” but on its own — “Magari!” — it becomes a heartfelt “I wish!” or “If only!”, one of those small words that instantly makes your Italian sound native.

Talk about it

  • Cosa vorresti fare il prossimo fine settimana? _(What would you like to do next weekend?)_
  • Se potessi vivere in qualsiasi città italiana, quale sceglieresti? _(If you could live in any Italian city, which would you choose?)_
  • Che consiglio daresti a chi inizia a studiare l’italiano? _(What advice would you give to someone starting to study Italian?)_
  • Cosa diresti a un cameriere per chiedere gentilmente il conto? _(What would you say to a waiter to kindly ask for the bill?)_
  • Ti piacerebbe cambiare lavoro o città? Perché? _(Would you like to change job or city? Why?)_
