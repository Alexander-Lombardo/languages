Travel & Transport

What you'll learn

  • Buy tickets and ask about schedules, platforms and connections.
  • Give and follow directions using formal commands.
  • Talk about plans that depend on conditions ('if the train is late...').
  • Handle delays, cancellations and lost luggage with confidence.
  • Use travel vocabulary naturally in real situations.

Dialogue

Viajera

Buenos días, quisiera un billete para Guadalajara.

Good morning, I'd like a ticket to Guadalajara.

Agente

¿Para hoy? El próximo tren sale a las diez y media.

For today? The next train leaves at ten thirty.

Viajera

Perfecto. ¿De qué andén sale?

Perfect. Which platform does it leave from?

Agente

Del andén cuatro, pero lleva quince minutos de retraso.

From platform four, but it's running fifteen minutes late.

Viajera

¿Hay que hacer transbordo en algún punto?

Do I have to change trains at some point?

Agente

Sí, cambie en Querétaro. Si pierde la conexión, hay otro tren a la una.

Yes, change at Querétaro. If you miss the connection, there's another train at one.

Viajera

Entendido. ¿Puedo reservar un asiento junto a la ventana?

Understood. Can I reserve a window seat?

Agente

Claro. Tome su billete y vaya directo al andén.

Of course. Take your ticket and go straight to the platform.

Viajera

Muchas gracias. ¿Dónde dejo el equipaje grande?

Thank you very much. Where do I leave the large luggage?

Agente

Déjelo en el vagón de adelante. ¡Buen viaje!

Leave it in the front carriage. Have a good trip!

Grammar

Formal commands (usted) — giving directions

When you ask for or give directions to a stranger, you use the formal command. To form it, take the yo form of the present, drop the -o, and add the opposite vowel ending.

tomartomotome
seguirsigosiga
cambiarcambiocambie
volvervuelvovuelva

For ustedes (plural), add -n: tomen, sigan, vayan.

Object pronouns attach to the end of an affirmative command (and need an accent): Déjelo aquí (Leave it here), Tómelo (Take it).

Real conditional: si + present

To talk about a likely condition and its result, use si + present indicative in the 'if' clause, and present or future in the result.

  • Si el tren llega tarde, tomamos el siguiente. — If the train is late, we'll take the next one.
  • Si pierde la conexión, hay otro a la una. — If you miss the connection, there's another at one.
  • Llamaré si hay un retraso. — I'll call if there's a delay.

Note: never use si + future. Say si llega, not si llegará.

Pronunciation tip

Linking words (enlace): Spanish speakers run words together. el_andén sounds like one word, and de_adelante blends the two vowels. Don't pause between a word ending in a vowel and the next word starting with one.

The soft 'd': between vowels, d softens almost to the 'th' in this: llegada, salida, retraso → listen for that gentle llega-da.

Reading: Un cambio de planes

Marta llegó a la estación temprano para no perder el tren de las nueve. En la pantalla vio que su vuelo de conexión tenía dos horas de retraso. El agente le dijo: «Si pierde el tren, tome el siguiente y cambie en la próxima parada». Marta hizo cola, compró un café y esperó con calma. Al final, abordó sin problemas y guardó su maleta junto al asiento. Pensó: si todo sale bien, llegaré antes de la cena.

Translation

Marta arrived at the station early so as not to miss the nine o'clock train. On the screen she saw that her connecting flight had a two-hour delay. The agent told her: 'If you miss the train, take the next one and change at the next stop.' Marta stood in line, bought a coffee and waited calmly. In the end, she boarded without problems and stored her suitcase next to her seat. She thought: if everything goes well, I'll arrive before dinner.

Culture note

In much of Latin America, the autobús is king for long-distance travel. Comfortable, reliable coach lines connect cities across Mexico, Colombia and Argentina, often with reclining seats and onboard service. The vocabulary shifts by country: a bus is el autobús in Mexico, el colectivo or el micro in Argentina, la guagua in the Caribbean, and el camión in some Mexican regions.

The word billete for ticket is common in Spain; many Latin Americans say boleto or pasaje. All are understood, so don't worry about mixing them up.

Talk about it

  • ¿Prefieres viajar en tren, en avión o en autobús? ¿Por qué? — Do you prefer traveling by train, plane or bus? Why?
  • Cuenta una vez que perdiste un vuelo o un tren. ¿Qué hiciste? — Tell about a time you missed a flight or train. What did you do?
  • Si tu vuelo se cancela, ¿cuál es tu plan? — If your flight is cancelled, what is your plan?
  • ¿Qué llevas siempre en tu equipaje de mano? — What do you always carry in your hand luggage?
