Grammar

What reported speech does

El estilo indirecto reports what someone said without quoting it literally. The original words become a subordinate clause introduced by que (for statements), si (for yes/no questions) or a question word (for information questions). The clause is governed by a reporting verb: decir, afirmar, preguntar, pedir…

Directo: María dice: «Estoy cansada.» → Indirecto: María dice que está cansada.

The key variable: the reporting verb's tense

If the reporting verb is in the present, future or present perfect (dice, dirá, ha dicho), the reported tense usually stays the same. If it is in a past tense (dijo, decía, había dicho), the reported verb normally shifts back (el desplazamiento temporal).

Backshift of tenses (reporting verb in the past)

Direct (original)Reported (backshifted)
Presente: «Trabajo aquí.»Imperfecto: dijo que trabajaba allí.
Pretérito: «Llegué tarde.»Pluscuamperfecto: dijo que había llegado tarde.
Pret. perfecto: «He comido.»Pluscuamperfecto: dijo que había comido.
Futuro: «Vendré.»Condicional: dijo que vendría.
Pres. de subjuntivo: «Espero que venga.»Imperfecto de subj.: dijo que esperaba que viniera.
Imperativo: «¡Sal!»Subjuntivo: le dijo que saliera.

The imperfecto, the pluscuamperfecto and the condicional do not shift further — they are already 'back'.

When NOT to backshift

If what was said is still true or remains a general truth, native speakers often keep the present: Dijo que el español se habla en veinte países. Both versions are acceptable; the present sounds more immediate.

Reporting questions and commands

  • Yes/no question: «¿Vienes?» → Me preguntó si iba.
  • Information question: «¿Dónde vives?» → Me preguntó dónde vivía. (The accent on the question word stays.)
  • Command / request: «¡Espera!» → Me pidió que esperara. (Verb of influence + subjunctive.)

Pronunciation tip

The reporting tag is unstressed: in «Dijo que vendría», glide quickly over dijo que and land the stress on the content verb, vendría. Spanish marks new information with a slight rise; the framing verb stays low and flat.

Mind the embedded question word: dónde, cómo, cuándo, qué keep their written accent and a small intonation peak even inside a statement: Me preguntó dónde vivía — the pitch lifts on dón- and falls through the rest of the sentence, since the whole utterance is no longer a question.

Reading: El testimonio que cambió de forma

Cuando la periodista publicó la entrevista, el político afirmó que jamás había dicho aquello. Aseguró que sus palabras habían sido tergiversadas y exigió una rectificación inmediata. La periodista, sin embargo, señaló que conservaba la grabación completa y aclaró que no había alterado ni una sola frase. Según el testimonio original, el político había prometido que reduciría los impuestos antes de fin de año. Al parecer, lo que generó el malentendido fue una coma mal colocada en la transcripción. Varios analistas comentaron que el rumor se había propagado más rápido que la propia aclaración. Un experto en comunicación advirtió que, en la era digital, una declaración mal reportada puede recorrer el mundo antes de que alguien la corrija. Al final, ambas partes reconocieron que el problema no era la mentira, sino la prisa con que todos repetían lo que creían haber oído.

Translation

When the journalist published the interview, the politician stated that he had never said that. He claimed that his words had been distorted and demanded an immediate correction. The journalist, however, pointed out that she still had the complete recording and clarified that she had not altered a single sentence. According to the original account, the politician had promised that he would lower taxes before the end of the year. Apparently, what caused the misunderstanding was a misplaced comma in the transcript. Several analysts commented that the rumour had spread faster than the clarification itself. A communication expert warned that, in the digital age, a poorly reported statement can travel the world before anyone corrects it. In the end, both sides acknowledged that the problem was not the lie, but the haste with which everyone repeated what they thought they had heard.

Culture note

In Spanish-language journalism, the condicional de rumor (the 'conditional of hearsay') is a quiet but powerful tool of reported speech. A headline such as «El ministro habría ocultado los datos» uses the conditional to signal that the claim is reported, unverified information — the writer is distancing themselves from it. English does the same job with words like allegedly (presuntamente) or reportedly.

Style guides at outlets like El País and RAE commentators debate this usage: purists argue it can spread innuendo without evidence, while editors defend it as a concise way to attribute. For a learner, recognising the habría + participle pattern is essential — it tells you the sentence is not a fact but a relayed, and possibly doubtful, account.

Talk about it

  • ¿Crees que los medios suelen tergiversar las declaraciones, o que el público las malinterpreta por leer demasiado rápido? — Do you think the media tend to distort statements, or that the public misinterprets them by reading too quickly?
  • Cuenta una ocasión en la que repetiste algo que te habían dicho y resultó ser un malentendido. ¿Qué pasó? — Tell about a time you repeated something you had been told and it turned out to be a misunderstanding. What happened?
  • ¿Hasta qué punto cambia el sentido de una frase cuando se reporta en lugar de citarse literalmente? — To what extent does the meaning of a sentence change when it is reported instead of quoted literally?
  • Si fueras periodista, ¿preferirías citar textualmente o resumir lo que dijo tu fuente? ¿Por qué? — If you were a journalist, would you prefer to quote verbatim or summarize what your source said? Why?
  • ¿Por qué crees que los rumores se propagan más rápido que las aclaraciones? — Why do you think rumours spread faster than clarifications?
