The Preterite (Past Simple)

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Form the preterite of regular -ar, -er and -ir verbs.
  • Talk about completed past actions: what you did yesterday.
  • Recognise three high-frequency irregular preterites: ir, ser, hacer.
  • Use common past-time markers like ayer and anoche.
  • Tell a short, simple story in the past.

Dialogue

Sofía

¡Hola, Diego! ¿Qué hiciste el fin de semana?

Hi, Diego! What did you do over the weekend?

Diego

Viajé a la playa con mi familia.

I traveled to the beach with my family.

Sofía

¡Qué bien! ¿Cuándo llegaron?

How nice! When did you arrive?

Diego

Llegamos el sábado muy temprano.

We arrived very early on Saturday.

Sofía

¿Y qué comieron allí?

And what did you (all) eat there?

Diego

Comimos pescado fresco. ¡Estuvo delicioso!

We ate fresh fish. It was delicious!

Sofía

¿Hiciste algo más?

Did you do anything else?

Diego

Sí, anoche fuimos al cine y vimos una película.

Yes, last night we went to the movies and watched a film.

Sofía

¿Te gustó la película?

Did you like the movie?

Diego

Sí, me gustó mucho. ¿Y tú? ¿Qué hiciste?

Yes, I liked it a lot. And you? What did you do?

Sofía

Yo trabajé el sábado, pero el domingo descansé en casa.

I worked on Saturday, but on Sunday I rested at home.

Diego

¡Qué tranquilo! La semana pasada yo también descansé poco.

How peaceful! Last week I also rested little.

Grammar

The preterite: finished past actions

The preterite (pretérito) describes actions that started and ended in the past: I ate, she traveled, we arrived. Think of a single, completed event with a clear endpoint.

Regular endings

Take the infinitive, drop -ar / -er / -ir, and add these endings. Note that -er and -ir verbs share the same endings.

Pronoun-ar (hablar)-er (comer)-ir (vivir)
yohablécomíviví
túhablastecomisteviviste
él / ella / ustedhablócomióvivió
nosotroshablamoscomimosvivimos

The accent on yo and él forms matters: hablo (I speak, present) vs. habló (he spoke, past).

Three must-know irregulars

ir (to go) and ser (to be) share the exact same preterite — context tells them apart:

Pronounir / serhacer
yofuihice
túfuistehiciste

nosotrosfuimoshicimos
ellos / ustedesfueronhicieron

Time markers

  • ayer — yesterday
  • anoche — last night
  • la semana pasada — last week
  • el fin de semana — the/this past weekend

Pronunciation tip

Move the stress to the end. Regular preterite yo and él forms carry a written accent on the final syllable: hablé (ah-BLEH), comió (koh-MYOH), viajó (byah-HOH). Push hard on that last vowel — it is what separates past from present.

Watch the irregulars. hice, hizo, fui, fue have no written accent and the stress falls naturally on the first syllable: HEE-seh, EE-soh, FWEE, FWEH.

Culture note

In much of Latin America, the preterite does heavy lifting in everyday speech: people say ¿Ya comiste? (Did you eat yet?) where a Spaniard would often use the present perfect ¿Ya has comido? So if you are learning neutral Latin-American Spanish, leaning on the preterite for recent completed actions will sound natural. Storytelling — fui, vi, hice — is built on this tense, which is why it is one of the first past tenses worth mastering.
