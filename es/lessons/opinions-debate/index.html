Grammar

Indicative vs. subjunctive after verbs of opinion

This is the grammatical heart of debating in Spanish. When a verb of opinion is affirmed, the speaker presents the idea as real, so the subordinate clause stays in the indicative. When that same verb is negated, the idea is cast into doubt and the clause flips to the subjunctive.

Affirmed → indicativeNegated → subjunctive
Creo que tiene razón.No creo que tenga razón.
Pienso que es justo.No pienso que sea justo.
Me parece que funciona.No me parece que funcione.

Asking a question can also pull the clause toward subjunctive when real doubt is implied: ¿Crees que sea buena idea?

Impersonal expressions of value or doubt → subjunctive

Expressions that judge or question (rather than state a fact) trigger the subjunctive even when affirmed:

  • Es importante que escuchemos al otro. (value judgement)
  • Es posible que el dato esté sesgado. (doubt)
  • No es cierto que la prensa sea neutral. (negated certainty)

But expressions of certainty stay in the indicative: Es evidente que existe un sesgo. / Está claro que hay debate.

Pronunciation tip

Entonación de la concesión: when you concede before countering (Es verdad que…, pero…), let your pitch rise slightly on the concession and then fall firmly on the rebuttal. This signals "I hear you, and yet" without sounding dismissive.

Synalepha in connectors: fast speakers blend vowels across word boundaries. de ahí que often sounds like de-AY-ke, and a fin de cuentas links into one breath. Practise saying these as single units, not word by word.

Reading: El arte de discrepar

En una sociedad saturada de información, saber discrepar bien se ha vuelto una habilidad escasa. Muchos confunden el debate con una pelea en la que solo importa ganar, cuando en realidad su objetivo es acercarse a una verdad compartida. Un buen argumento no se mide por el volumen de la voz, sino por la calidad de la evidencia que lo sostiene. Por eso, antes de rebatir, conviene escuchar y, si hace falta, conceder lo que el otro plantea con razón. No creo que exista una postura completamente neutral; todos cargamos con prejuicios que conviene reconocer. Sin embargo, admitir un sesgo no nos condena a la postverdad, sino que nos obliga a buscar datos más fundamentados. Es importante que distingamos entre opinar y afirmar: lo primero admite matices, lo segundo exige pruebas. A fin de cuentas, el consenso rara vez nace de imponer una idea, sino de matizarla entre todos. Quien sabe decir "tienes razón en esto, pero discrepo en aquello" domina el verdadero arte de discrepar.

Translation

In a society saturated with information, knowing how to disagree well has become a scarce skill. Many confuse debate with a fight in which only winning matters, when in reality its goal is to get closer to a shared truth. A good argument is not measured by the volume of one's voice, but by the quality of the evidence that supports it. That is why, before rebutting, it is wise to listen and, if necessary, to concede what the other person rightly raises. I don't think a completely neutral stance exists; we all carry biases that are worth acknowledging. However, admitting a bias does not condemn us to post-truth; rather, it forces us to seek more well-founded data. It is important that we distinguish between giving an opinion and asserting: the former admits nuance, the latter demands proof. At the end of the day, consensus rarely arises from imposing an idea, but from nuancing it together. Whoever can say "you're right about this, but I disagree about that" has mastered the true art of disagreeing.

Culture note

In much of Latin America, public debate prizes cortesía verbal — softening disagreement with formulas like con todo respeto, no quiero ofender, pero… or permíteme matizar. Bluntly saying estás equivocado ("you're wrong") can feel confrontational; speakers prefer no estoy del todo de acuerdo ("I don't entirely agree").

The verb polemizar and the noun polémica appear constantly in journalism, where a tertulia (a panel of commentators debating live) is a staple of radio and television. Watching a Spanish-language tertulia is one of the fastest ways to absorb the connectors and concession formulas in this lesson.

Talk about it

  • ¿Crees que sea posible debatir sin que nadie cambie de opinión? ¿Para qué sirve entonces? — Do you think it's possible to debate without anyone changing their mind? What is the point, then?
  • ¿Hasta qué punto es legítimo conceder un argumento al rival sin perder tu propia postura? — To what extent is it legitimate to concede an argument to your opponent without losing your own stance?
  • ¿Estás de acuerdo con que ninguna fuente de información es realmente neutral? Justifica tu respuesta. — Do you agree that no information source is truly neutral? Justify your answer.
  • ¿Qué papel juega la postverdad en los debates actuales y cómo podríamos rebatirla? — What role does post-truth play in current debates, and how could we rebut it?
  • ¿Es el consenso siempre deseable, o a veces la discrepancia resulta más productiva? — Is consensus always desirable, or is disagreement sometimes more productive?
