Gustar & Similar Verbs

What you'll learn

  • Use gustar correctly with me, te, le, nos, les.
  • Choose gusta vs. gustan depending on what you like.
  • Talk about what you like and dislike doing with gustar + infinitive.
  • Use similar verbs like encantar, interesar and doler.
  • Add a, mí, ti for emphasis: a mí me gusta…

Dialogue

Sara

¿Te gusta el café?

Do you like coffee?

Diego

Sí, me gusta mucho. ¿Y a ti?

Yes, I like it a lot. And you?

Sara

A mí no me gusta. Prefiero el té.

I don't like it. I prefer tea.

Diego

¿Y la música? ¿Te gustan los conciertos?

And music? Do you like concerts?

Sara

¡Me encantan! Me gusta mucho bailar.

I love them! I really like dancing.

Diego

A mí también. Pero no me gusta cantar.

Me too. But I don't like singing.

Sara

A mí tampoco. ¿Te interesa el cine?

Me neither. Are you interested in movies?

Diego

Sí, me interesan las películas viejas.

Yes, I'm interested in old films.

Sara

¿Vamos al cine el sábado?

Shall we go to the movies on Saturday?

Diego

Hoy no puedo, me duele la cabeza.

Not today, my head hurts.

Sara

Ay, lo siento. ¿El domingo entonces?

Oh, I'm sorry. Sunday then?

Diego

Perfecto. A mí me encanta esa idea.

Perfect. I love that idea.

Grammar

How gustar really works

In Spanish you don't “like” something the way you do in English. Instead, something is pleasing to you. So Me gusta el café literally means “Coffee is pleasing to me.” The thing you like is the subject, and it decides whether the verb is singular or plural.

The two forms: gusta vs. gustan

What you likeVerbExample
one thing / an actiongustaMe gusta el café.
more than one thinggustanMe gustan las verduras.
a verb (to do something)gustaMe gusta bailar.

The pronoun tells you WHO

Put one of these little words before the verb to say who is pleased:

PronounWhoExample
meIMe gusta.
teyouTe gusta.
lehe / she / you (formal)Le gusta.
nosweNos gusta.
lesthey / you allLes gusta.

Adding emphasis: a mí, a ti…

To stress or contrast, add a mí, a ti, a él, etc. before the pronoun: A mí me gusta, pero a ti no te gusta.

Verbs that work the same way

  • encantar = to love: Me encantan los perros.
  • interesar = to interest: Nos interesa la historia.
  • doler = to hurt: Me duele la cabeza. / Me duelen los pies.

Pronunciation tip

Linking words together: Spanish speakers blend me gusta into one smooth chunk: “meh-GOOS-tah.” Don't pause between the pronoun and the verb.

gustan ends in -an (GOOS-tahn) — the final n is light, not the strong English “n.” Keep the stress on the first syllable: GOOS-tah, GOOS-tahn.

Culture note

In Spanish-speaking countries, sharing what you like is a common way to bond. Notice how the dialogue uses también (“me too”) and tampoco (“me neither”) to agree — these short reactions keep conversation flowing and friendly.

Inviting someone with ¿Vamos al cine? (“Shall we go to the movies?”) is casual and warm. Declining politely with a reason, like me duele la cabeza, and offering another day is considered good manners rather than a rejection.
