Present Tense: Regular Verbs

What you'll learn

  • Recognise the three verb families: -ar, -er, and -ir.
  • Conjugate regular verbs in the present tense for all six subjects.
  • Talk about everyday actions: speak, eat, live, work, study.
  • Drop the subject pronoun when the verb ending makes it clear.
  • Build simple sentences about your daily routine.

Dialogue

Sara

Hola, Marco. ¿Estudias español?

Hi, Marco. Do you study Spanish?

Marco

Sí, estudio español todos los días.

Yes, I study Spanish every day.

Sara

¡Qué bien! ¿Y dónde trabajas?

How nice! And where do you work?

Marco

Trabajo en un café. ¿Y tú?

I work in a café. And you?

Sara

Yo trabajo en una librería. Leo mucho.

I work in a bookshop. I read a lot.

Marco

Nosotros aprendemos rápido, ¿verdad?

We learn fast, right?

Sara

Sí. ¿Dónde vives ahora?

Yes. Where do you live now?

Marco

Vivo cerca del parque. Camino al café.

I live near the park. I walk to the café.

Sara

¿Comes en el café también?

Do you eat at the café too?

Marco

A veces como ahí y bebo mucho agua.

Sometimes I eat there and I drink a lot of water.

Sara

Yo siempre escribo en español por la tarde.

I always write in Spanish in the afternoon.

Marco

¡Practicamos juntos mañana!

Let's practise together tomorrow!

Grammar

Three verb families

Every Spanish verb's dictionary form (the infinitive) ends in one of three ways: -ar, -er, or -ir. To use a verb, you drop that ending and add a new one that matches the subject. These endings tell you who is doing the action, so Spanish often skips the subject pronoun.

Notice the -er and -ir endings are identical except for nosotros and vosotros.

Dropping the pronoun

  • Yo hablo español and Hablo español both mean 'I speak Spanish'. The ending -o already says 'I'.
  • Add the pronoun back for emphasis or contrast: Yo trabajo, tú estudias.
  • In Latin America, vosotros is rarely used — people use ustedes for 'you all'.

Pronunciation tip

Endings carry the stress naturally. In most present-tense forms you stress the second-to-last syllable: HA-blo, co-ME-mos, vi-VI-mos. No written accent is needed there.

The double e in leer and creer is two clear vowels: leh-EHR, not 'leer' like English. Say each 'e'.

Culture note

In Latin America you'll almost never hear vosotros. From Mexico to Argentina, 'you all' is ustedes, and the verb takes the same ending as ellos. In parts of Argentina, Uruguay and Central America, people also replace tú with vos (vos hablás, vos comés) — a feature called voseo. As a beginner, stick with tú and ustedes; you will be understood everywhere.
