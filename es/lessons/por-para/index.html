Por vs Para

What you'll learn

  • Distinguish the core meanings of por (cause/means) and para (goal/destination).
  • Use por for duration, exchange, movement through, and on behalf of someone.
  • Use para for purpose, deadlines, recipients, and destinations.
  • Recognise fixed expressions built with por and para.
  • Self-correct the most common por/para mistakes in writing and speech.

Dialogue

Marta

¿Para quién es ese paquete tan grande?

Who is that huge package for?

Diego

Es para mi hermana. Es un regalo por su graduación.

It's for my sister. It's a gift for her graduation.

Marta

¡Qué detalle! ¿Y cómo lo vas a enviar?

How thoughtful! And how are you going to send it?

Diego

Lo mando por correo. Tiene que llegar para el viernes.

I'm sending it by mail. It has to arrive by Friday.

Marta

Pasé por la oficina de correos esta mañana y había mucha gente.

I passed by the post office this morning and there were a lot of people.

Diego

Por eso voy temprano. Pagué veinte pesos por el envío exprés.

That's why I'm going early. I paid twenty pesos for express shipping.

Marta

Buena idea. ¿Lo haces tú o lo manda alguien por ti?

Good idea. Are you doing it yourself or is someone sending it for you?

Diego

Lo hago yo, para asegurarme de que llegue bien.

I'm doing it myself, to make sure it arrives safely.

Marta

Tu hermana te lo va a agradecer por mucho tiempo.

Your sister will thank you for it for a long time.

Diego

Eso espero. Trabajé mucho para pagarlo, ¡pero vale la pena!

I hope so. I worked hard to pay for it, but it's worth it!

Grammar

The big idea: cause vs. goal

Both por and para often translate as English for, but they point in opposite directions. Think of por as looking backward at a cause, source, or motive, and para as looking forward toward a goal, destination, or recipient.

por (cause / means)para (goal / destination)
Sensebecause of, through, byin order to, toward, for
ExampleGracias por tu ayuda.Estudio para aprobar.
MeaningThanks because of your help.I study in order to pass.

When to use POR

  • Cause / motive: Lo hago por amor. (out of love)
  • Duration: Viví ahí por dos años.
  • Movement through: Caminamos por el parque.
  • Exchange / price: Pagué diez dólares por el libro.
  • Means: Te llamo por teléfono.
  • On behalf of: Firmo por mi jefe.

When to use PARA

  • Purpose / goal: Estudio para ser médico. (followed by an infinitive)
  • Recipient: Este café es para ti.
  • Destination: Salgo para Lima mañana.
  • Deadline: La tarea es para el lunes.
  • Opinion / standard: Para mí, es muy fácil.

A quick contrast

Trabajo por dinero. = I work because money is the motive. Trabajo para ganar dinero. = I work in order to earn money. Same situation, different angle.

Pronunciation tip

Two unstressed syllables, two clean vowels. Keep por short with a soft tapped r at the end — don't let it drift into an English 'pour'. In para, both a's are the open 'ah' sound, and the single r between vowels is a light flap, like the tt in American 'butter': PAH-rah.

In fast speech, para is often shortened to pa' in casual Latin-American speech (pa' qué), but write the full para in anything formal.

Reading: Una carta de agradecimiento

Querida abuela: te escribo para darte las gracias por el suéter que me tejiste. Lo hiciste a mano, así que sé que pasaste muchas horas por mí. Salgo para la universidad la próxima semana y quiero llevarlo conmigo para los días fríos. Por lo tanto, será como tenerte cerca. Gracias por todo tu esfuerzo; lo guardaré para siempre.

Translation

Dear grandma: I'm writing to thank you for the sweater you knitted me. You made it by hand, so I know you spent many hours for my sake. I'm leaving for university next week and I want to take it with me for the cold days. Therefore, it will be like having you nearby. Thank you for all your effort; I'll keep it forever.

Culture note

The por/para distinction trips up nearly every learner because English collapses both into for. Native speakers rarely think about the rules — they feel the difference. A useful shortcut from Spanish teachers across Latin America: if you can replace the English for with 'in order to' or 'destined for', use para; if you mean 'because of', 'through', or 'in exchange for', use por.

You'll also hear por woven into everyday courtesy: por favor, por supuesto, por nada. Mastering these set phrases first makes the grammar feel natural much faster.

Talk about it

  • ¿Para qué estudias español? ¿Tienes un objetivo concreto? — What are you studying Spanish for? Do you have a concrete goal?
  • ¿Qué cosa harías por un amigo aunque te costara mucho esfuerzo? — What would you do for a friend even if it took you a lot of effort?
  • ¿Cuánto pagarías por un regalo perfecto para alguien especial? — How much would you pay for a perfect gift for someone special?
  • ¿Cuál es un plazo importante que tienes para esta semana? — What is an important deadline you have for this week?
