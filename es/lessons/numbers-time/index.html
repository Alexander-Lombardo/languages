The tens are: diez (10), veinte (20), treinta (30), cuarenta (40), cincuenta (50). From 31 on, join them with y: treinta y uno (31), cuarenta y dos (42).

Telling the time with ser

Use es for one o'clock and son for everything else, because the verb agrees with the number of hours.

TimeSpanish
1:00Es la una.
2:00Son las dos.
3:15Son las tres y cuarto.
3:30Son las tres y media.
3:45Son las cuatro menos cuarto.

To say at a time, use a la una / a las dos: El concierto es a las ocho.

Days & dates

Days and months are written in lowercase in Spanish. The week starts on lunes (Monday). For dates use the pattern el + number + de + month: el cinco de marzo. Use the plain number (cinco), not an ordinal.

Pronunciation tip

Linking words: in las ocho the s glides straight into the o, so it sounds like one word: la-SOH-cho. Spanish speakers connect the final consonant of one word to the vowel that starts the next.

The accent on dieciséis: the written accent pulls the stress onto the last syllable — dyeh-see-SEYS. Most number words, though, are stressed on the second-to-last syllable: CUA-tro, SIE-te, QUIN-ce.

Culture note

In most of Latin America and Spain, the week starts on Monday, not Sunday, so calendars place lunes in the first column. Dates are written day/month/year: 5/3/2026 means the 5th of March, not May 3rd. And while official schedules (trains, TV, events) often use the 24-hour clock — las veinte horas for 8 p.m. — in everyday speech people simply say las ocho de la noche.
