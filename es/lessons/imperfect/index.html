The Imperfect

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Form regular -ar, -er and -ir verbs in the imperfect tense.
  • Use the three irregular imperfect verbs: ser, ir and ver.
  • Describe past habits, routines and repeated actions.
  • Set the scene: age, time, weather and feelings in the past.
  • Recognise common time markers like antes, siempre and todos los días.

Dialogue

Marta

¿Dónde vivías de niña?

Where did you live as a child?

Carlos

Vivía en un pueblo pequeño y muy tranquilo.

I lived in a small, very quiet town.

Marta

¿Y qué hacías todos los días?

And what did you do every day?

Carlos

Iba a la escuela y después jugaba en la calle.

I went to school and afterwards played in the street.

Marta

¿Tenías muchos amigos?

Did you have many friends?

Carlos

Sí, siempre estábamos juntos. Éramos felices.

Yes, we were always together. We were happy.

Marta

¿Visitabas a tu abuela?

Did you visit your grandmother?

Carlos

Claro. Los domingos comíamos en su casa antigua.

Of course. On Sundays we ate at her old house.

Marta

¡Qué bonito recuerdo!

What a lovely memory!

Carlos

Sí. A veces extraño esos días.

Yes. Sometimes I miss those days.

Grammar

What is the imperfect for?

The imperfect (el imperfecto) describes the past without a clear end point. Use it for habits and routines ("I used to play"), descriptions (weather, age, feelings) and background scenes. Think: "used to" or "was/were ...-ing".

Regular endings

Drop the -ar / -er / -ir and add these endings. Notice that -er and -ir share the same endings.

Helpful time words

  • antes — before, in the past
  • siempre — always; a veces — sometimes
  • todos los días — every day
  • cuando era niño/a — when I was a child

Pronunciation tip

Watch the í: the -er/-ir imperfect endings all carry a written accent on the í: comía, vivías, teníamos. Pronounce it as a clear, stressed "EE".

-ábamos / íbamos / éramos: these nosotros forms are stressed on the accented vowel — ha-BLÁ-ba-mos, Í-ba-mos, É-ra-mos. The b in -aba endings is soft, almost like a 'v'.

Culture note

In Spanish-speaking cultures, the Sunday family meal is a cherished ritual. Across Latin America, extended families gather at la casa de la abuela for a long, unhurried lunch — often the social high point of the week. The imperfect tense is the natural way to narrate these memories: los domingos comíamos juntos ("on Sundays we used to eat together"). When older speakers reminisce about el pueblo where they grew up, you'll hear the imperfect everywhere.
