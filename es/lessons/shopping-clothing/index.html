Shopping & Clothing

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Name common clothing items and colours.
  • Ask for sizes, prices, and try things on in a shop.
  • Use demonstratives (este, ese, aquel) to point things out.
  • Handle a polite shopping conversation from start to finish.

Dialogue

Cliente

Buenas tardes. Estoy buscando una camisa.

Good afternoon. I'm looking for a shirt.

Vendedora

Claro. ¿De qué color la quiere?

Of course. What colour would you like it?

Cliente

Azul, por favor. ¿Tiene esta en azul?

Blue, please. Do you have this one in blue?

Vendedora

Sí. ¿Cuál es su talla?

Yes. What's your size?

Cliente

Uso la talla mediana.

I wear medium.

Vendedora

Aquí tiene. ¿Quiere probársela?

Here you go. Would you like to try it on?

Cliente

Sí. ¿Dónde está el probador?

Yes. Where is the fitting room?

Vendedora

Al fondo, a la derecha.

At the back, on the right.

Cliente

Me queda bien. ¿Cuánto cuesta?

It fits me well. How much does it cost?

Vendedora

Cuesta veinte euros. Hoy tiene un descuento.

It costs twenty euros. Today it has a discount.

Cliente

¡Qué barato! Me la llevo.

How cheap! I'll take it.

Vendedora

Perfecto. Puede pagar en la caja.

Perfect. You can pay at the checkout.

Grammar

Demonstratives: this, that, that over there

Spanish has three levels of distance. They agree with the noun in gender and number, just like articles.

Distancemasc. sing.fem. sing.masc. pl.fem. pl.
this (near me)esteestaestosestas
that (near you)eseesaesosesas
that over thereaquelaquellaaquellosaquellas

Examples: este vestido (this dress), esa camisa (that shirt), aquellos zapatos (those shoes over there).

Asking about price

Use ¿cuánto cuesta? for one item and ¿cuánto cuestan? for several. The verb is costar (o → ue):

Subjectcostar
esto / itcuesta
estos / theycuestan

Trying things on: probarse

Probarse is reflexive and stem-changing (o → ue). You add the right reflexive pronoun:

  • me pruebo — I try on
  • te pruebas — you try on
  • se prueba — he/she tries on

You can also attach the pronoun to the infinitive: ¿Quiere probársela? (Would you like to try it on?).

Pronunciation tip

The double L: talla, llevo and aquella all use 'll', pronounced like English 'y': TAH-yah, YEH-boh, ah-KEH-yah.

Linking words: Spanish glides words together. Me la llevo sounds like one phrase: meh-lah-YEH-boh. Don't pause between the little words.

Culture note

In most Spanish-speaking countries, clothing sizes follow the European system, not US numbers, and letters like S, M, L (often called chica, mediana, grande) are common. When you enter a small shop, a friendly buenas tardes is expected before you start browsing.

Bargaining is normal in open-air markets (mercados) and street stalls, where you might ask ¿me hace un descuento? But in regular stores and malls, prices are fixed — there you simply pay en la caja.
