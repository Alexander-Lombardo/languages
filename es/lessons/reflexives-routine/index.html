Reflexive Verbs & Daily Routine

What you'll learn

Dialogue

Marta

¿A qué hora te despiertas normalmente?

What time do you usually wake up?

Diego

Me despierto temprano, a las seis y media.

I wake up early, at six thirty.

Marta

¡Qué temprano! ¿Y qué haces primero?

How early! And what do you do first?

Diego

Primero me levanto y me ducho.

First I get up and shower.

Marta

¿Te vistes antes o después de desayunar?

Do you get dressed before or after having breakfast?

Diego

Me visto después. Luego me cepillo los dientes.

I get dressed afterwards. Then I brush my teeth.

Marta

Yo me levanto muy tarde los fines de semana.

I get up very late on weekends.

Diego

Yo también. ¿A qué hora te acuestas?

Me too. What time do you go to bed?

Marta

Me acuesto a las once, pero me duermo tarde.

I go to bed at eleven, but I fall asleep late.

Diego

Yo me duermo rápido. ¡Estoy muy cansado!

I fall asleep fast. I'm very tired!

Grammar

What is a reflexive verb?

A reflexive verb describes an action you do to yourself. In the dictionary it ends in -se: levantarse (to get oneself up), ducharse (to shower oneself). To use it, you drop the -se, conjugate the verb as normal, and add a reflexive pronoun in front.

The reflexive pronouns

yomeme levanto
tútete levantas

nosotrosnosnos levantamos
vosotrososos levantáis
ellos / ellas / ustedessese levantan

Stem changes

Some routine verbs change their stem vowel. Despertarse goes e→ie (me despierto), and acostarse and dormirse go o→ue (me acuesto, me duermo). The reflexive pronoun stays the same.

Sequencing your day

  • primero = first, luego / después = then / afterwards.
  • antes de + infinitive = before doing: antes de desayunar.
  • después de + infinitive = after doing: después de ducharme.

Pronunciation tip

The pronoun is unstressed: in me levanto, push the stress onto the verb (le-VAN-to), not the little word me. Let the pronoun lean lightly onto the verb.

Linking: when a pronoun ends in a vowel and the verb begins with one, they glide together: se acuesta sounds like seh-a-KWES-tah, almost one word.

Culture note

Daily rhythms shift across the Spanish-speaking world. In much of Spain and parts of Latin America, lunch (la comida or el almuerzo) is the largest meal and falls in the early afternoon, while dinner (la cena) can come quite late — 9 or 10 p.m. is normal. The famous siesta is less common in big cities today, but the late, social evening still shapes when people se acuestan. Don't be surprised if friends invite you out well after the hour you'd normally go to bed.
