Object Pronouns Together

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Distinguish direct object pronouns (lo, la, los, las) from indirect ones (le, les).
  • Combine two object pronouns in the correct order: indirect before direct.
  • Apply the le → se rule when two third-person pronouns meet.
  • Place double pronouns correctly before a conjugated verb or attached to an infinitive/gerund.
  • Answer questions naturally by replacing whole noun phrases with pronouns.

Grammar

Two pronouns, one verb

When a sentence has both an indirect object (to/for whom) and a direct object (what), Spanish often replaces both with pronouns. The rule of order is fixed: indirect before direct — remember ID (Indirect, then Direct).

IndirectDirect
melo
tela
le → selos
noslas
les → se

¿Me prestas el libro? → Sí, te lo presto. (te = to you, lo = the book)

The le → se rule

You can never say le lo or les las. When an indirect le/les bumps into a direct lo/la/los/las, the indirect pronoun changes to se:

  • Le doy el regalo. → Se lo doy. (I give it to him/her)
  • Les explico las reglas. → Se las explico. (I explain them to them)

Because se is ambiguous, you can add a él / a ella / a usted / a ellos for clarity: Se lo doy a ella.

Where do the pronouns go?

  • Before a conjugated verb: Te lo mando.
  • Attached to an infinitive or gerund: Voy a mandártelo / Estoy mandándotelo. (note the added accent)
  • Attached to an affirmative command: Devuélvemelo.
  • With poder/querer/ir a + infinitive, either position works: Me lo puedes dar = Puedes dármelo.

Pronunciation tip

The new accent mark. When you attach pronouns to a verb, the stress doesn't move — but the spelling rules force a written accent to protect it. manda → mándamelo, explicar → explicárselo. Count three syllables back from the end and you'll usually need the tilde.

Say it as one word. Clusters like se lo and te la blend smoothly — don't pause between them: se-lo-doy, not se … lo … doy.

Reading: El paquete perdido

Marta me pidió un favor: necesitaba un regalo para su hermana y no tenía tiempo de comprarlo. Yo se lo compré y se lo mandé por correo. Cuando el paquete no llegó, ella me llamó preocupada y me pidió el número de seguimiento; se lo di enseguida. La empresa lo había perdido, así que les escribí y me lo reembolsaron. Al final compré otro regalo y se lo entregué a Marta en persona. Ella me lo agradeció muchísimo.

Translation

Marta asked me a favor: she needed a gift for her sister and didn't have time to buy it. I bought it for her and sent it to her by mail. When the package didn't arrive, she called me worried and asked me for the tracking number; I gave it to her right away. The company had lost it, so I wrote to them and they refunded it to me. In the end I bought another gift and handed it to Marta in person. She thanked me for it very much.

Culture note

In everyday Latin-American speech you'll constantly hear strings like dámelo, préstamela, se lo dije. Native speakers rely on these double pronouns to avoid repeating nouns once the topic is clear — a conversation can run for several turns with nobody naming the actual object again. For learners this is a milestone: handling se lo / se la smoothly is one of the clearest signals that you've crossed from sounding like a textbook into sounding fluent. Don't be afraid to over-clarify at first with a él, a ella or a ustedes — even natives add them when context gets murky.

Talk about it

  • ¿Cuándo fue la última vez que alguien te pidió un favor? ¿Se lo hiciste? — When was the last time someone asked you a favor? Did you do it for them?
  • Si un amigo te presta algo importante, ¿se lo devuelves enseguida o se te olvida? — If a friend lends you something important, do you return it to them right away or do you forget?
  • ¿Prefieres regalar cosas hechas a mano o comprarlas? ¿Por qué? — Do you prefer to give handmade things or to buy them? Why?
  • Cuenta una situación en la que tuviste que explicarle algo difícil a alguien. ¿Se lo explicaste bien? — Tell about a situation where you had to explain something difficult to someone. Did you explain it to them well?
