Idioms & Connectors

What you'll learn

  • Link ideas smoothly with discourse connectors (sin embargo, por lo tanto, además).
  • Use common Spanish idioms naturally in conversation.
  • Express cause, contrast and consequence in a single sentence.
  • Recognise when an idiom should NOT be translated word for word.
  • Sequence an argument or a story with first–then–finally connectors.

Dialogue

Rosa

Estoy harta de este proyecto. ¿Me echas una mano?

I'm fed up with this project. Can you lend me a hand?

Diego

Claro. Aunque tengo poco tiempo, vale la pena ayudarte.

Sure. Even though I have little time, it's worth helping you.

Rosa

Gracias. La verdad, no tengo ganas de seguir sola.

Thanks. Honestly, I don't feel like continuing alone.

Diego

Lo entiendo. Sin embargo, ya casi terminamos.

I get it. However, we're almost done.

Rosa

¿En serio? ¿No me estás tomando el pelo?

Really? You're not pulling my leg?

Diego

Para nada. De hecho, solo falta el resumen.

Not at all. In fact, only the summary is left.

Rosa

Perfecto. Por eso te quería aquí; tú no metes la pata.

Perfect. That's why I wanted you here; you don't mess up.

Diego

Bueno, a veces sí. Pero, en cambio, escribo rápido.

Well, sometimes I do. But, on the other hand, I write fast.

Rosa

Me da igual cómo, con tal de acabar hoy.

I don't care how, as long as we finish today.

Diego

Por lo tanto, empecemos ya. En resumen: una hora más.

Therefore, let's start now. In short: one more hour.

Grammar

Connectors: linking ideas like a native

Connectors (conectores) glue sentences together so your Spanish flows instead of sounding like a list. They fall into a few families by the job they do.

JobConnectorExample
Contrastsin embargo, en cambio, aunqueEs caro; sin embargo, lo compro.
Consequencepor lo tanto, por esoLlueve, por lo tanto me quedo.
Additionademás, de hechoEs útil; además, es barato.
Clarify / sum upo sea, en resumenO sea, no te interesa.

Contrast: aunque vs sin embargo

aunque joins two clauses inside one sentence: Aunque llueve, salgo. sin embargo usually starts a new sentence and is set off by a comma: Llueve. Sin embargo, salgo.

Idioms don't translate literally

Many high-frequency expressions are fixed. Learn them whole:

  • tener ganas de + infinitive = to feel like doing: Tengo ganas de viajar.
  • valer la pena = to be worth it: No vale la pena discutir.
  • dar igual = to not matter (works like gustar): Me da igual. / Nos da igual.
  • meter la pata = to mess up (literally 'to put the leg in').
  • tomar el pelo = to tease (literally 'to take the hair').

Notice dar igual takes an indirect-object pronoun: me/te/le/nos/les da igual.

Pronunciation tip

Linking words together (enlace): in natural speech, a word ending in a vowel blends into the next word starting with a vowel. o sea sounds like one word: oh-SEH-ah. de hecho blends as deh-EH-cho (remember the h is silent).

Sentence rhythm: connectors at the start of a sentence (Sin embargo,…) carry a small pause after the comma — let your voice dip and then restart. This intonation is what makes the contrast clear to the listener.

Reading: Un favor que valió la pena

Marta estaba harta de organizar la fiesta sola, por lo tanto le pidió ayuda a su hermano. Aunque él tenía mucho trabajo, le echó una mano sin quejarse. De hecho, terminaron antes de lo previsto. Sin embargo, al final Marta metió la pata y olvidó comprar el pastel. En cambio, su hermano ya había traído uno. En resumen, el favor valió la pena y todos quedaron contentos.

Translation

Marta was fed up with organizing the party alone, therefore she asked her brother for help. Although he had a lot of work, he lent her a hand without complaining. In fact, they finished earlier than expected. However, in the end Marta messed up and forgot to buy the cake. On the other hand, her brother had already brought one. In short, the favor was worth it and everyone was happy.

Culture note

Idioms (modismos) and connectors are some of the most region-flavoured parts of Spanish. While tomar el pelo and meter la pata are understood almost everywhere, each country layers on its own colourful expressions: Mexicans might say ¡no manches! for disbelief, while Argentines lean on che and boludo. The neutral connectors in this lesson — sin embargo, por lo tanto, además — are safe across all 20 Spanish-speaking countries and are exactly what you'll see in news, essays and formal writing. Master them and your Spanish instantly sounds more fluido.

Talk about it

  • ¿Cuándo fue la última vez que metiste la pata? ¿Qué pasó? — When was the last time you messed up? What happened?
  • ¿Qué actividad crees que vale la pena, aunque sea difícil? — What activity do you think is worth it, even though it's hard?
  • ¿Prefieres pedir ayuda o hacerlo todo solo? ¿Por qué? — Do you prefer to ask for help or do everything alone? Why?
  • ¿De qué cosa estás harto últimamente y por eso quieres cambiar? — What are you fed up with lately and therefore want to change?
