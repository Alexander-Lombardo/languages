Weather & Seasons

Open the interactive lesson — free

What you'll learn

  • Describe the weather using <em>hace</em>, <em>está</em> and <em>hay</em>.
  • Name the four seasons and the months they cover.
  • Talk about temperature and ask what the weather is like.
  • Use <em>hacer</em> in the present to form weather expressions.
  • Plan an activity based on the forecast.

Dialogue

Sofía

¿Qué tiempo hace hoy?

What's the weather like today?

Mateo

Hace mucho sol, pero hace un poco de viento.

It's very sunny, but it's a bit windy.

Sofía

¿Y la temperatura? ¿Hace calor?

And the temperature? Is it hot?

Mateo

Sí, hay veinticinco grados. Hace calor.

Yes, it's twenty-five degrees. It's hot.

Sofía

Perfecto. Quiero ir a la playa.

Perfect. I want to go to the beach.

Mateo

Buena idea, porque mañana llueve.

Good idea, because tomorrow it's going to rain.

Sofía

¿Llueve? No me gusta la lluvia.

It rains? I don't like the rain.

Mateo

En primavera siempre llueve mucho aquí.

In spring it always rains a lot here.

Sofía

Es verdad. Prefiero el verano.

That's true. I prefer summer.

Mateo

Yo también. En invierno hace demasiado frío.

Me too. In winter it's too cold.

Sofía

Entonces, ¡vamos a la playa hoy!

Then, let's go to the beach today!

Mateo

Vale. Llevo agua y protector solar.

Okay. I'll bring water and sunscreen.

Grammar

Talking about the weather: three little verbs

Spanish doesn't say "the weather is hot." It uses three different verbs depending on the word. Learn which one goes with which.

VerbUsed forExample
hace (from hacer)calor, frío, sol, vientoHace frío.
está (from estar)nublado, despejadoEstá nublado.
hay (there is/are)niebla, tormenta, gradosHay tormenta.
special weather verbsllover, nevarLlueve. / Nieva.

To ask, the standard question is ¿Qué tiempo hace? — literally "What weather does it make?"

The verb hacer (to do / make) — present tense

Weather uses only the él/ella form (hace), but here is the full present tense, because hacer is one of the most useful verbs in Spanish.

The seasons (las estaciones)

  • la primavera — spring
  • el verano — summer
  • el otoño — autumn
  • el invierno — winter

Use en with seasons: en verano (in summer), en invierno (in winter).

Pronunciation tip

The double ll: in lluvia and llueve the ll sounds like the English 'y' in yes: YOO-vyah, YWEH-veh.

Linking words: Spanish speakers glide words together. Hace un poco sounds like "AH-seh-oon-POH-koh" with no hard stops. Don't pause between the words.

The j in nada is soft, but the v in viento and verano is a gentle 'b'. Practise: viento = BYEN-toh.

Culture note

Because the Spanish-speaking world spans both hemispheres, the seasons are flipped: when it's invierno in Madrid or Mexico City, it's verano in Buenos Aires or Santiago. So a Chilean spends New Year's Eve on the beach in shorts, while a Spaniard bundles up by a fireplace.

Most of Latin America uses Celsius (grados centígrados), so "30 degrees" means a hot day, not a freezing one. Near the equator — Colombia, Ecuador — many cities have spring-like weather all year, so people talk less about seasons and more about the temporada de lluvias (rainy season) versus the temporada seca (dry season).
