Narrating a Story

What you'll learn

  • Tell a story in the past combining the preterite and the imperfect.
  • Use the imperfect for background, description and ongoing actions.
  • Use the preterite for the events that move the story forward.
  • Link events with connectors like de repente, entonces and al final.
  • Recognise common irregular preterite verbs used in storytelling.

Dialogue

Marta

¿Sabes qué me pasó ayer cuando volvía a casa?

Do you know what happened to me yesterday when I was coming home?

Diego

No, cuéntame. ¿Qué ocurrió?

No, tell me. What happened?

Marta

Caminaba por el parque y todo estaba muy tranquilo.

I was walking through the park and everything was very quiet.

Diego

¿Y entonces?

And then?

Marta

De repente, un perro enorme salió de unos arbustos.

Suddenly, a huge dog came out of some bushes.

Diego

¡No me digas! ¿Te asustaste?

No way! Did you get scared?

Marta

Mucho. Mientras yo corría, el perro me seguía.

A lot. While I was running, the dog was following me.

Diego

¿Y qué hiciste al final?

And what did you do in the end?

Marta

Me di cuenta de que solo quería jugar, así que me detuve.

I realised that it just wanted to play, so I stopped.

Diego

Por suerte todo terminó bien. ¡Menudo susto!

Luckily it all ended well. What a scare!

Marta

Sí. Sin embargo, ahora siempre miro hacia los arbustos.

Yes. However, now I always look towards the bushes.

Grammar

Two past tenses, one story

To narrate in Spanish you weave together the imperfecto (the background) and the pretérito (the events). Learning when to use each is the heart of B1 storytelling.

Imperfecto — the scenery

Use it for descriptions, weather, time, age, feelings, and actions in progress or repeated. It answers what was going on?

  • Description: El parque estaba tranquilo.
  • Ongoing action: Yo caminaba por el parque.
  • Two parallel actions with mientras: Yo corría mientras el perro me seguía.

Pretérito — the plot

Use it for single, completed events that push the story forward. It answers what happened next?

  • Un perro salió de los arbustos.
  • Me detuve y todo terminó bien.

Pronunciation tip

Preterite stress matters: regular preterite endings carry a written accent on the final vowel in yo and él/ella forms: caminé, caminó, salió. Move the stress and you change the tense: camino (I walk) vs. caminó (he walked).

Irregular preterites lose that accent: hice, dijo, tuvo, fue are all stressed normally with no written accent on the ending. Don't say hi-CE — it's HI-ce.

Reading: El ruido en la cocina

Érase una vez una noche de invierno en la que todo estaba en silencio. Marta leía un libro en el sofá mientras la lluvia caía con fuerza. De repente, oyó un ruido extraño en la cocina y se asustó. Se levantó despacio y entró sin hacer ruido. Al final, descubrió que solo era el gato, que había tirado un vaso. Por suerte, no pasó nada grave y Marta volvió a su libro.

Translation

Once upon a time, on a winter night when everything was silent, Marta was reading a book on the sofa while the rain fell hard. Suddenly, she heard a strange noise in the kitchen and got scared. She got up slowly and went in without making noise. In the end, she discovered it was just the cat, which had knocked over a glass. Luckily, nothing serious happened and Marta went back to her book.

Culture note

Storytelling has deep roots across the Spanish-speaking world. Latin American literature gave us realismo mágico, where Gabriel García Márquez and Isabel Allende blend the ordinary and the fantastic — many of their tales open with a sweeping past-tense scene before the first big event lands. In everyday speech, people often start an anecdote with ¿Sabes qué me pasó? (Do you know what happened to me?) or the classic fairy-tale opener Érase una vez. Listening for the switch between imperfecto and pretérito is one of the fastest ways to follow a story told at natural speed.

Talk about it

  • ¿Recuerdas un suceso curioso que te ocurrió de niño? Cuéntalo. — Do you remember a curious event that happened to you as a child? Tell it.
  • ¿Qué hacías normalmente los fines de semana cuando eras joven? — What did you usually do on weekends when you were young?
  • ¿Cuál es la mejor historia que alguien te ha contado? ¿De qué se trataba? — What's the best story someone has told you? What was it about?
  • ¿Prefieres las historias con un desenlace feliz o sorprendente? ¿Por qué? — Do you prefer stories with a happy or surprising ending? Why?
